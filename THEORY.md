# Program Ecologies: Stochastic Stability in Open-Source Games

Operating hypothesis for how the model works. Rejected alternatives live in REJECTED.md. Update this file, not the conversation, when the model changes.

---

## 1. The object

**Base game.** A finite symmetric game $G$, or an asymmetric game symmetrized by `ROLE` (§3). Currently 2-player; $k$-player is a stated goal (§9).

**ROLE.** A first-class atom of the canonical language, present in every arm and every game: a per-match antisymmetric public tag drawn by the environment — `ROLE` evaluates to $A_0$ for one player and $A_{k-1}$ for the other, opposite for the two, fresh each match. It is Aumann's correlating device, the traffic light. This is a modeling commitment, not a primitive of the base game: it asserts that the environment supplies public randomness, and "what the language can stably express" is henceforth read as "given public randomness." In games whose efficient outcome is symmetric it is inert.

**Strategies.** Programs in a weakly extensional language $\mathcal{L}$ (§2): a program may condition on the opponent's behavior — what the opponent plays against anyone — but never on the opponent's source text. A program applied to an opponent yields a (possibly random) action. $u(p,q)$ is the expected base-game payoff.

**Payoffs.** Computed exactly. Runtime budget $T \to \infty$: terminating pairs get exact payoffs; divergent pairs receive the minimax action. Finite $T$ with the $\kappa t/T$ override is a computational device only; extrapolate. Every instrument run so far treats runtime as free. Whether runtime should be a selection pressure, and in what form, is **open** (§9.11). It was previously ruled out and is re-opened as a modelling question (conversation, 2026-09-30), not answered either way.

**Prior.** $\mu(p) = 2^{-|p|}$, with $|p|$ in **bits under an optimal code for $\mathcal{L}$**. Exponent fixed at 1 — the weakest simplicity assumption that yields a proper universal prior. Not a knob. Always use bits: $2^{-(\text{node count})}$ is improper for this grammar (growth ~5.66×/node needs ≥2.5 bits/node). The optimal code sits at exactly the critical exponent plus a $\sim 2\log_2 L$ length prefix, so the tail of $\mu$ is polynomial ($\sim 1/(L\log^2 L)$); roughly half the mutation mass lies beyond 7 nodes (§7).

## 2. Extensionality

Three levels of opponent access, one semantics (every arm evaluates programs by the same grounded iteration), one of them canonical.

**Weak extensionality — canonical.** Oracle access to the opponent's behavior: a program may condition on what the opponent plays against anyone, including third parties and itself. In the DSL: `THEM(P)` for any program-sort $P$. Behavior depends on the opponent's input-output function, never on source. $\pi$ is defined on this language.

**Strong extensionality — baseline arm.** Condition only on what the opponent plays *against you*. In the DSL: `THEM(ME)` is the sole opponent-referencing form; the program is effectively a function $A \to A$. This is the response-function framework the project started with. It is kept as a formal arm, not a parable, because (1) it is the minimal hypothesis for the deterrence half of the efficiency argument, (2) its failure is the empirical argument that weak is canonical and that argument needs a run, and (3) its program space is dozens, so it is exhaustively provable by hand.

**Source access — contrast arm.** Adds syntactic `eq`. Behavior is no longer a small object, equivalence is no longer a congruence, and `eq(THEM, ME)` is a 3-node self-recognizing clique (short, strict Nash, exploits ALLC). Only a computational certificate is available here, never a characterization.

**What weak extensionality buys.** (1) *Behavior against fixed incumbents is a small object* — a distribution over base-game actions per probe — so "what can an invader do" is finitely enumerable and the behavioral characterization (§7) is a proof. (2) *Behavioral equivalence is a congruence*, so dedup is sound. (3) *No syntactic cliques.* (4) *Unfakeable handshakes* (§3): passing "do you play $a^*$ against $a^*$-players" requires playing $a^*$.

**Strong vs weak: three jobs, weak does all of them.** *Deterrence* needs only on-path conditioning (strong suffices). *Invasion of inefficient outcomes* (the handshake, §3) needs a channel separate from the on-path action; third-party probes provide one; under strong access the handshake shares the grounding anchor with the on-path action and either collapses to the incumbent action or pays a tax. *Drift stability* (distinguishing reciprocators from unconditional cooperators) needs a third-party probe. So strong deters but neither invades cleanly nor stabilizes; weak does all three.

**Strong extensionality as a recombination model.** Under strong access, behavior toward incumbents (observed) and toward outsiders (unobserved) are separable loci — the architecture greenbeard theory needs. The unobserved locus drifts and produces falsebeard-like variants (ALLC) without crossover. Two-step destabilization where biology's is one-step; bears on the strong arm only; `eq`-cliques have no free locus.

**Why global extensionality is inconsistent and the fragments aren't.** All extensional functions would give $B \cong (B \to A)$, ruled out by Cantor; the computational shadow is Rice. The DSL is a *fragment* — extensional primitives without closure — and finite at each truncation.

## 3. Efficiency: where it holds and where it fails

**What was conjectured.** *In a symmetric game under weak extensionality, every stable ecology is Pareto efficient in the correlated feasible set. In a `ROLE`-symmetrized asymmetric game, it maximizes the unweighted utilitarian sum.*

**Deterrence is not the route.** In a general game, $a^*$ is deterrable iff $u(a^*,a^*) \ge \max_a\min_b u(a,b)$ — individual rationality, the folk theorem. Every IR outcome, efficient or not, is supportable by a harsh enough punisher. This holds in separable games too: grim-with-the-harshest-action ($\arg\min g$, which need not equal $a^d$) supports inefficient outcomes. Efficiency comes from two selection arguments, not from deterrence.

**Status.** The conjecture holds in coordination games under `ROLE` — Chicken, Nash demand and Battle of the Sexes reach the correlated optimum at deadweight loss $\approx 0$ (Chicken 0.000–0.001, demand 0.000–0.07, BoS 0.007–0.012 at $N=100$) — and in the Stag Hunt at $N = 1000$ in both arms (all-Stag 0.98–1.00). It fails in the dilemmas: in the PD and the exchange game the cooperative share is 0.6–2.4% for $w \in \{0.1, 0.3, 1\}$, $N \in \{100, 1000\}$, $n \in \{6, 7\}$ under the length prior, and 3.7% under a uniform prior, with all-defect at 0.97–0.99 (RESULTS.md, "Consistent sweep" and "Exponential fitness map"). It fails to select the bargaining solution in the fixed-role ultimatum game: the mode is the subgame-perfect pair $(L\,|\,L)$ at 0.39 and the Nash bargaining pair $(M\,|\,M)$ is second at 0.19, in both the mutual and the one-sided arm (RESULTS.md, "Ultimatum game").

**The mechanism: the unconditional shadow.** A conditional program that is stable against its partner is, on path, indistinguishable from the constant that plays the same action against that partner; call that constant its shadow. The shadow is a far larger syntactic class — ALLC is 31% of $\mathcal{L}_6$ by behavioral class (572 of 1,852 programs), and in the ultimatum $\mu(H)/\mu(\texttt{THEM(ME)}) \approx 110$ — and, being neutral against the conditional incumbent, it enters at exactly $\mu/N$ by drift. The shadow has no off-path response, so once it is fixed the defector that the conditional program deterred re-enters, and the population is back where it started. Two instances. In the PD the third-party reciprocator `THEM(^C)` (cooperate iff you cooperate with ALLC) enters all-D at $\mu\rho \approx 6.8\times10^{-4} \times 0.02$–$0.07$; its dominant exit is ALLC at $\mu(C)/N = 3.3\times10^{-3}$ per event at $N=100$, after which D takes all-C with $\rho = 0.06$–$0.5$; the exit rate exceeds the entry rate by 10–40× at $N=1000$ and $\sim$200× at $N=100$, for every $w$ tried. In the ultimatum the conditional proposer `THEM(ME)` paired with an $H$-responder, $(\texttt{THEM(ME)}\,|\,H)$, drifts to its shadow $(H\,|\,H)$ at $1.24\times10^{-3}$ per event (neutral, $\rho = 1/N$), $(H\,|\,H)$ drifts to $(H\,|\,L)$ at the same rate (the responder's threshold is unmet-proof at $H$ offers, so lowering it is neutral), and $(H\,|\,L)$ goes to $(L\,|\,L)$ by strong selection ($\rho = 0.058$, $7.2\times10^{-3}$ per event). Every step but the last is drift into a shadow.

**The shadow is a finite-$N$ obstruction; the faker is the limit.** The shadow's exit is neutral drift, $\mu(C)/N$, and vanishes as $N \to \infty$. The fakers `THEM(^D)`, `THEM(^X)` and `THEM(^ROLE)` play against `THEM(^C)` what they play against a third party, so they defect while it cooperates. They strictly invade it at an $N$-independent rate ($\rho(\texttt{THEM(^D)}) \approx 0.26$ at $w = 0.3$). Entry into all-D is neutral at one copy, so $\rho_{\text{enter}}$ falls like $N^{-1/2}$. In the $\varepsilon \to 0$ chain, $\pi(\text{all-}\texttt{THEM(^C)})$ therefore rises while the shadow dominates exits, peaks where the faker takes over, and then falls like $N^{-1/2}$. The peak is 0.013–0.014 at $N \approx 3{,}000$, $1{,}000$ and $300$ for $w = 0.1$, $0.3$ and $1$. By $N = 30{,}000$ fakers carry 0.92–0.99 of exits and the share is 0.003–0.007 (RESULTS.md, "lim_N of the chain with `ROLE`"). The islands found the same spoiler for the same reason: once $\mu$-weight stops mattering, only exponents do. Every probe reciprocator `THEM(^p)` has a faker of the same length, so this is not an accident of $\mathcal{L}_6$. The dilemma failure in $\lim_N$ is fakeability, not conditionality.

**Why source observation does not fix it.** Excluding the shadow requires a program that cooperates with the conditional incumbent but not with its shadow, i.e. one that tests for conditionality; testing for conditionality is self-reference — "do you cooperate with those who cooperate with you" — and self-reference needs grounding to terminate, and grounding at rate $\varepsilon$ costs $\varepsilon(S - P)$ against the defector, which puts the tester below the defector at one copy: $\rho_{\text{enter}} \ll 1/N$ (the ½- and ¼-grounded FairBots enter all-D at $\rho = 1.1\times10^{-3}$ and $8.6\times10^{-3}$ against a neutral $1/N = 10^{-2}$, and hold $< 10^{-4}$ of $\pi$). This is the Löb regress in population form. The exhaustive search over $\mathcal{L}_9$ (228,794 programs) finds no program that can enter all-D, cooperates with itself and is uninvadable from rare; the shortest known is the 12-node `or(and(and(X,X),THEM(^C)),THEM(ME))`, whose entry probability is that of `THEM(^C)` and whose exclusion is still leaky to ALLC drift; the 13-node indirect tester `and(THEM(^THEM(^C)),not(THEM(^D)))` is exploited by `THEM(^C)` itself. The bargaining form of the same obstruction: program-equilibrium commitment (Tennenholtz) requires a reader — a partner whose program responds to the commitment — and readers are neutral to constants whenever the read population is monomorphic, so the commitment drifts into its shadow before it is ever tested.

**The engines.** Engine 1 (harsh punishers lose to mild ones by drift-then-invasion) is weakly supported — the mild probe `THEM(^C)` is the reciprocator that appears, not a grim variant — and untested as stated, since no harsh punisher ever holds enough of $\pi$ to be outcompeted. Engine 2 (the unfakeable handshake) is false as stated: fakeability is not the binding constraint. The handshake is unfakeable, and the faker-like probe `THEM(^D)` does exit all-`THEM(^C)`, but at 1/40 of the rate at which the honest shadow ALLC does; the handshaker loses to its own shadow, not to a faker.

**Spatial structure.** Tested (RESULTS.md, "Spatial structure"): death-birth Moran on a torus with von Neumann neighbourhoods, $N = 1024$, $\varepsilon N = 1$, gives $P(C,C) = 0.54$ against $0.02$ on the complete graph of the same size, and the reciprocator that carries it is `THEM(^C)` at 32², joined by `or(X,THEM(THEM))`, `THEM(^ROLE)`, `THEM(^X)` and the ½-grounded FairBot `or(X,THEM(ME))` at larger sides. The mechanism is entry versus exit. Entry is pair nucleation: a lone `THEM(^C)` in a D sea earns $-1$ like its neighbours and is neutral, but two adjacent ones each earn $-0.75$ and grow, so a sweep starts with probability $\approx \tfrac14$ per nucleation attempt instead of $1/N$ — a reciprocator cluster is on-path *distinguishable* from its shadow at its boundary, which is what source observation could not achieve. Exit is the ALLC-subsidized D front: ALLC still drifts into the reciprocator sea (the shadow), and a D with two ALLC and two `THEM(^C)` neighbours earns $0$ while the bordering `THEM(^C)` earns $-0.25$, so the front advances wherever ALLC is present. The ecology on the torus is a limit cycle all-D → sweep → ALLC infiltration → subsidized-front collapse, ~15 cycles per $10^5$ generations at 32². Under mutation, fakers (`THEM(^D)`) stay at $\mu$-weight in every setting, spatial or not; without mutation they are the spoiler (Islands, below). Scaling: $P(C,C)$ rises from 32² to 64² (0.54 → 0.63) and plateaus at 128² (0.61) with seed-to-seed spread 0.25–0.96, the duty cycle (fraction of time more than halfway to efficiency) rising 0.57 → 0.70 → 0.69; at 128² the outcome per seed is a FairBot sea at $P(C,C) = 0.96$, a `THEM(^C)` cycle, or a standing three-way coexistence of reciprocator, shadow and defector at 0.25–0.34; the ALLC density at collapse falls with side (0.13 → 0.06), because the front nucleates on a local ALLC cluster. FairBot escapes the exit: a D at its border earns $0$, FairBot's own self-payoff, so its front is neutral.

**Revised claim.** Selection over source-observing programs reaches efficiency iff the efficient outcome is stable against its own unconditional shadow — which is the case when it is a strict Nash outcome of the `ROLE`-symmetrized base game, and not the case in dilemmas or in fixed-role bargaining — except under spatial structure, where the efficient outcome is reached repeatedly and held, in the $\varepsilon \to 0$ object, for a fraction of time that grows only sublinearly with $N$: nucleation is local ($\rho_{\text{enter}}$ is $N$-independent, 0.11 for `THEM(^C)` at 32² and 64²) and the residence $M_{\text{exit}}$ grows like $N^{0.5\text{–}0.8}$ (1,500 → 2,900 mutants for `THEM(^C)`, 2,600 → 7,500 for FairBot), against an $N$-independent entry cost $1/(\mu\rho_{\text{enter}}) \approx 18{,}000$ mutants, so the cooperative share is 8% at 32² and 14% at 64² and the lattice ecology is not efficient in $\lim_N$ at any rate visible here (RESULTS.md, "Per-mutant rates"). At finite $\varepsilon N = 1$ the picture is different and is an approach rate, not $\pi$: on 128² every seed converges to a `THEM(^C)` sea at $P(C,C) = 0.8$–$1.0$ because nucleation is concurrent with the slow collapse (RESULTS.md, "Mixing check"). Island structure with migration in place of mutation is not an exception: a PD run absorbs into all-`THEM(^C)` in 17.5% of runs and otherwise into positive deadweight loss (RESULTS.md, "Island model"). `ROLE` resolves the coordination obstacle (it supplies the correlation that Chicken, demand and BoS need) and nothing else.

**Grounded self-reference in space.** The ½-grounded FairBot `or(X,THEM(ME))` is the one reciprocator whose D front is neutral: a D at its border earns $0$, FairBot's own self-payoff, so ALLC inside a FairBot sea cannot subsidize a front, and the sea that one 128² seed reached held for 94% of its window. The hypothesis that this is a pruning mechanism — grounding noise makes a lone D neutral, roaming D prune ALLC, enforcement stays exercised — is false: a lone D in a FairBot sea lives 5 generations against 2 in a `THEM(^C)` sea (not 10×), and ALLC lifetimes in the two seas are indistinguishable (medians ≈ 1 generation at both sides; RESULTS.md, "Per-mutant rates" (c)). FairBot's residence is long ($M_{\text{exit}}$ 2,600 at 32², 7,500 at 64², ended by ALLC drift in 93 of 100 trials) but its entry is not: $\rho_{\text{enter}} \approx 0.005$ with $\mu \approx 4\times10^{-5}$ gives an entry cost of $\sim 5\times10^6$ mutants, so FairBot is unreachable from all-D by single-copy nucleation and enters, when it does, by neutral drift inside a `THEM(^C)` sea where it earns $0$ against both. Grounded self-reference in space is therefore a long-lived state with no road to it: the grounding cost that made it unenterable in the well-mixed chain ($\rho < 1/N$, §3 above) is what makes it unenterable on the lattice.

**Islands.** With migration replacing mutation, 64 islands of 100 on a complete graph or a ring, and every program of $\mathcal{L}_6$ seeded somewhere, the process is not ergodic. A PD run absorbs within about $10^3$ generations (median 2,170) into one of three families: all-`THEM(^C)` (56 of 320 runs), mutual defection (97), or exploitation probes such as `THEM(^ROLE)` and `THEM(^X)`, which copy a non-constant third party and so reproduce `ROLE` against `ROLE` in self-play, at payoff $-0.25$ to $-0.875$ (167). The shadow is not the obstruction here. ALLC is eaten in the first within-island scramble (extinct by a median of generation 40), and no `THEM(^C)` island ever passes to a shadow. The obstruction is the faker. With $\mu$ gone, arrivals are set by what neighbouring islands hold, not by class size, and `THEM(^C)`'s 15 advantageous invaders in $\mathcal{L}_6$ are all fakers. Of 2,358 island exits from `THEM(^C)`, 2,338 go to fakers, 18 to D and none to the shadow. `THEM(^C)` holds only when every faker has gone extinct before it. Under mutation the unconditional shadow closes the road to efficiency; without mutation the faker does. The subsidized D front appears only in the initial transient. In Chicken with `ROLE`, the conventions `ROLE` and `not(ROLE)` take most islands, but self-referential mutual-Swerve islands, whose self-play diverges to the minimax action Swerve at payoff 0, persist beside them and once took the whole graph. The two families exclude each other ($\rho \approx 10^{-17}$ both ways), so without mutation nothing moves an island from one to the other. In Chicken without `ROLE`, islands sit at the mixed equilibrium on average (payoff $-0.17$ to $-0.22$ against $-2/11$), held as a Straight minority among self-referential best-responders; single runs also land on other cool mixtures, one at $-0.34$ (RESULTS.md, "Cool check") (RESULTS.md, "Island model").

**Ergodic islands** (RESULTS.md, "Ergodic islands"). With mutation re-injected, the ε→0 object is a chain over monomorphic metapopulation states, and structure acts on two of its three rates. *Proposition (constant selection; an instance of Maruyama's invariance of fixation probability under conservative migration, 1970/1974):* on regular island graphs with no island-level selection, the faker's global fixation is $1 - 1/r$ with $r$ its constant fitness ratio (T + S = R + P here), independent of migration, island count and graph. It was checked in 11 of 11 Monte Carlo cells at $mN$ 0.1–10 on three graphs. Structure acts on entry (local relatedness, set by island size) and on the shadow ($1/(IN)$), never on the faker. So the faker's share of exits depends only on total size $M = IN$, crossing ½ at $M \approx 860$. The weak arm plateaus in $I$ at $\mu_R\,\rho_N(R|D)/f \le 0.13$, falling like $N^{-0.46}$: it is not efficient along any island family. At fixed per-island ε, adding islands *lowers* weak cooperation (0.14 → 0.04, $I$ = 64 → 256), because the global faker supply grows with $I$. The modal arm's odds grow $\propto M$ at fixed island size, against $M^{1/2}$ well mixed, and islands purge its fakeable provers. Structure and unfakeability are complements: structure serves the entry condition and the shadow, never unfakeability.

**Almost all seeds** (ε = 0, iid seeding from μ; RESULTS.md, "Almost all seeds?"). The RS's proposed result, that almost every seed under the complexity prior ends efficient without mutation, holds for the modal arm at $n = 6$ in every regime tested: the efficient fraction rises to 1.00 [0.91, 1.00] along $N \gg I$ (I = 4, N = 6,400), along $I \gg N$ (N = 100, I = 256) and on the diagonal. The mechanism is that spread between islands is one-way: a prover migrant fixes on a D island at $\propto N^{-1/2}$, a D migrant fixes on a prover island at $e^{-\Theta(N)}$, and ALLC is eaten within the first 60 generations and never returns. So the bad event is losing every prover everywhere, with probability about (per-island loss)$^I$, where the per-island chance of keeping a prover grows like $\sqrt N$. The RS's condition that islands must grow faster than their count is sufficient but not necessary in a faker-free language. The static replicator from μ reaches an efficient, persistent endpoint at $n = 6$–9 under eight priors over the whole segment from μ to uniform; but it predicts the weak arms wrongly (all-D statically, up to 0.55 efficient on islands), because at ε = 0 the faker is a finite stock rather than a constant exit, and the orders of limits differ. The seed claim's proof object is therefore sol's decomposition, seed concentration + basin robustness + spread and extinction bounds, not basin membership alone; the scramble is now a lemma (below), and the open part is between islands. Under the legibility gate the n = 6 lottery is unchanged at every budget (paired differences 0.00 at b ≥ 4, within ±0.07 at b = 1–3), while generic random pruning lowers it by 0.2–0.4 at I = 4 (RESULTS.md, "Bounded provers"). *Cutoff sensitivity* (RESULTS.md, "Almost all seeds: cutoff sensitivity in n"): at n = 6–9 the per-island chance is flat in the cutoff, p(100, n) = 0.083–0.092, with p ∝ N^0.55, and it tracks the prior mass of *D-entering self-cooperators* (0.0229 → 0.0246), not the unfakeable core: at ε = 0 a fakeable prover counts fully unless its faker is seeded nearby. Establishment is independent per island at rate p, one established island spreads to all (nucleation control: 80 of 80), so the bad event is that no island establishes. The sound core was never strictly invaded in 1,920 runs; its only loss route is displacement from a mixed island where a probe-faker feeds on a fakeable neighbour. Unfakeability is the right property for the mutation object, where fakers are re-supplied, and not for the seed lottery. *Tail* (RESULTS.md, "Almost all seeds: the tail in n"): establisher status depends only on self-play and play against D, so μ_est only accumulates; it is ≥ 0.0207 at every cutoff (rigorous), with a $1/s^2$ shell tail and an extrapolated limit of about 0.027 under the fixed infinite prior (total π²/12). Global faker mass (2.5 μ_est) is not the spoiler term: the resident-conditioned chance that a seed with an establisher also carries one of its own fakers is 0.19 → 0.27 at n = 6–12 with shrinking increments. FairBot and `BOX1(THEM(ME))` are unfakeable to n = 12. The establishment side of "almost all seeds" is therefore uniform in n. *Spoiler* (RESULTS.md, "Spoiler-conditioned establishment" and "The scramble lemma and Claim A"): at n = 6–12 and N = 100, 400 a co-seeded faker does not lower establishment (pooled discount 1.09–1.24; single-copy forced fakers null). *Claim A is proved* at every level and cutoff, without Löb, from box soundness at the stable world (Lemma 0): FairBot's pair has no fakers; every faker of a probe-reader cooperates with ALLC; every faker of the THEM(THEM) pair self-cooperates, so is an establisher or cooperates with D. The mechanism of the fakers' deaths is *demographic*, not selective: a probe-faker is mean-neutral to first order and survives the scramble like a fitness-pinned ghost, ≈ 1/(1 + τ_A) per copy with τ_A ≈ 13–19 generations growing like log N; selection adds a factor 2–7 only for D-cooperating fakers. So for fakeable establishers the expected spoiler term N·μ_q·q̄_q·h_q grows like N/log N unless the post-scramble harm h falls (measured 0.44 → 0.2 from N = 100 to 400), and the clean statement of "almost all seeds" is on the *fakerless* establishers, FairBot and `BOX1(THEM(ME))` (μ_core ≈ 0.0102), which need no spoiler term; the fakeable provers are a measured bonus to n = 12 and N = 400. *Demographic lemma* (RESULTS.md, "The demographic lemma, the per-founder spoiler bound, and the establishment formula"): survival through the scramble is now a theorem for any lineage and any stopping time, by one martingale (Lemma D′): $P(k_\tau > 0,\ \Phi_\tau \ge \varphi) \le k_0/\varphi$ with $\Phi = 1 + \int d\,e^{-R}$, which carries demography and selection together with no sign condition on fitness. Charging spoilers per founder, conditional on the target's survival, replaces the $1/P(A)$ loss of the union step by a measured $r = 0.8$–$1.7$, and the per-island bound is positive in all 27 cells at $n \le 12$, $N \le 1{,}600$; the probe-readers' spoiler term still grows like about $N^{0.45}$. The per-island chance has a formula: founders survive the scramble like near-critical lineages ($\approx 1/(1+\tau)$, size $\approx 1 + \tau$ given survival, with the establisher family gaining $w x_D x_E$ on the way, so $\ell = E[k_\tau]/k_0 \approx 1.3$), the survivors pool, and the family escapes by the exact two-type birth–death formula $u_N(K)$; it predicts $p$ within 4% from the state at ALLC extinction and within 1–17% from the seed alone. At fixed cutoff $p(N) \to 1$, following $\mathrm{erf}(\mu_{est}\,\ell\sqrt{Nc/2})$: 0.087 → 0.980 over $N = 100$ → 25,600 at $n = 9$. Each island is therefore almost surely efficient along $N$ alone for the fakerless establishers, with a growing but still small spoiler term for the fakeable ones. At I = 4, islands are independent trials only when migration is slow compared with nucleation: at mN ≥ 1 they merge during nucleation and the run-level chance moves toward p(IN), with no spoiler involvement (0 losses in 180 runs). The n = 8 free-arm lottery (0.33 / 0.55 / 0.97 / 1.00 at (100, 4) / (400, 4) / (100, 64) / (100, 256)) reproduces the n = 6 pattern within its intervals (RESULTS.md, "Proof-carrying contracts v1", lottery). *Compatibility* (RESULTS.md, "compatibility among co-seeded establishers"): incompatible establisher pairs are co-seeded routinely (0.26 of islands at N = 400, → 1 in N) and selection resolves every one on the island: two self-cooperating classes both earn R against themselves, so a stable mixture would need each to exploit the other, which is impossible; establisher pairs are neutral, bistable or dominated. 0 of 3,814 co-seeded islands failed to resolve (bound 7.9·10⁻⁴), so compatibility is not the binding condition at n ≤ 12; the only unresolved islands are anti-coordinator polymorphisms with no establisher (≈ 3·10⁻⁵ per island). κ ≈ 0.95 reflects the provers' exploitation of two near-universal suckers, the faker structure of the mutation object seen from the other side. The residual risk is *between* islands, where mutually-defecting establishers can be held separately, each efficient: a rival network at the metapopulation level. *Rival networks* (RESULTS.md, "Rival networks across islands, and the mN rule"): such a patchwork is metastable, not frozen. Mutual defection between two self-cooperators is a symmetric coordination game with a barrier of only about $N w/4$, so a minority island flips at a hazard of about $mN\cdot\rho_{DD}(N)$ with $\rho_{DD} = 1.85\cdot10^{-5}$ at $N = 100$ and $7\cdot10^{-9}$ at $N = 200$, boosted 10–20× by migrant load at $mN \approx 1$; when the per-birth migration rate is comparable to selection the archipelago is one coordination population and the larger network wins within $10^2$ generations (0.935 of clean merges, all at shares ≥ 0.6). Island-level efficiency is unaffected in every regime; what fails on the observed timescale is universality across the metapopulation, and the order of limits decides it (at fixed $N$ and $mN \ge 1$, $I \to \infty$ then $t \to \infty$ gives one network; along a path with growing $N$, separation lifetimes grow exponentially). Majorities are set by colonization from early islands, not by prior mass, and a *bridge* class that cooperates with both rivals (`BOX1(THEM(THEM))`) gains share from the conflict: rivalry selects for the most compatible prover. *The mN rule:* islands are independent nucleation trials while the expected replacement fraction during nucleation, $x = mN\cdot T_{nuc}/N$, is $\lesssim 0.3$ (local nucleation ≥ 0.75 of its $m = 0$ rate at $x \le 0.5$, halved at $x \approx 0.8$, at both $N = 100$ and $400$); the arrival count $mN\cdot T_{nuc}$ does not collapse $N$. ## 4. Separable games — the illustrative class

Separability is no longer the theorem's hypothesis; it is where the structure is cleanest.

**Definition.** $u(a_i, a_j) = f(a_i) + g(a_j)$. Dominant $a^d = \arg\max f$; efficient $a^* = \arg\max(f+g)$. The PD in use: defecting gives self $+1$, other $-2$.

**Utilitarian coefficient.** In a separable game, "weight the opponent's payoff by $\lambda$" is a well-defined action $a(\lambda) = \arg\max_a[f(a) + \lambda g(a)]$, with $a(0) = a^d$ and $a(1) = a^*$. In a general game the analogous object depends on $a_j$ and cannot be separated from strategic interaction. A scalar $\lambda$ per behavior is meaningful only here. Asymmetric case: maximizing $\lambda_1 u_1 + \lambda_2 u_2$ decouples, and each player's action is set by the ratio $\lambda_{-i}/\lambda_i$.

**Matching supports only efficient outcomes.** A reciprocator with $\rho(a^*) = a^*$ and $g(\rho(a)) \le g(a)$ deters all invaders iff $a^*$ is efficient: an invader playing $a$ earns $f(a) + g(\rho(a)) \le f(a) + g(a) \le f(a^*) + g(a^*)$, the last step being efficiency. Grim-with-$a^d$ is the stingiest such $\rho$; matching $\rho(a) = a$ is the most generous. This is the *matching* half of Engine 1 in closed form. It is **not** a claim that all stable ecologies are efficient — harsher punishers support more (§3).

**Matching over grim.** Same deterrence; matching earns 5-for-5 from partial cooperators where grim earns 0-for-0, and is noise-robust. With $\varepsilon$-grounding anchored at $a^*$, mutual matching converges to $a^*$. In continuous games pure matching against a slightly stingy partner (returns 0.9 of what it receives) collapses to zero; generous matching $\rho(a) = a + \delta$ stabilizes at $9\delta$ (Roberts & Sherratt; Killingback & Doebeli). No natural grim threshold in continuous games; matching is canonical because it is the identity.

**Matching rate as price.** In an exchange game, matching at rate $k$ is an exchange rate. Any complete trade is efficient; the rate is distributional. So the rate is the $\lambda$-ratio, the stable rate is the bargaining outcome, and the First Welfare Theorem analogy is literal: the ecology's response function plays the role of prices.

**Assortment and Hamilton's rule.** From the Price equation with $w = w_0 - c\,z_{\text{self}} + b\,z_{\text{partner}}$: cooperation increases iff $rb > c$, where $r = \text{Cov}(z_{\text{partner}}, z_{\text{self}})/\text{Var}(z_{\text{self}})$ is assortment — a statistic, not a genetic quantity (Fletcher & Doebeli 2009). Source observation is a fourth assortment mechanism after kinship, space, and tags. For a FairBot/ALLD mix, $r = 1$; with $b=2, c=1$ Hamilton's rule reproduces FairBot's weak dominance over ALLD. $r$ is endogenous to the language's recognition power.

**Greenbeards are cliques.** Biology's greenbeards fall to falsebeards because tag and behavior sit at separate loci (Gardner & West 2010). Under syntactic `eq` the tag is the whole program — no separate locus — so `eq`-cliques are unfalsifiable greenbeards and strict Nash where biological ones aren't.

## 5. The dynamics

**Population.** $N$ agents, each holding a program.

**Selection.** Fitness-proportional reproduction against the population mix (Moran). Fitness is mean-field: $F(p; s) = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\ne i} u(p, q_j)$.

**Fitness map.** $f = \exp(w \cdot \text{payoff})$ with $w$ a free selection intensity. Results vary by at most $3\times$ over $w \in [0.1, 1]$ (the cooperative share in the PD by $2.7\times$ at $N=100$, $1.1\times$ at $N=1000$; the Stag basin at $N=100$ is the one qualitative $w$-dependence). Validated against an agent-based Moran simulation at $\varepsilon N = 0.1$: mode agreement within 0.02 at every $w$ in both arms.

**Mutation.** At rate $\varepsilon$, an agent's program is replaced by a fresh draw from $\mu$.

**Regime.** Intermediate: $\varepsilon$ small enough that at most one mutant lineage is alive at a time (mutants win or lose on fitness; coalitions are structurally impossible), but not so small that drift out of polymorphic attractors dominates. The strict $\varepsilon\to 0$ limit at fixed $N$ forces monomorphism only for $\varepsilon \ll e^{-cN}$, which is unphysical. The attractor chain is the $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit and has no standing variance: between mutation events the population sits at an attractor, so a conditional program and its unconditional shadow (§3) are on-path indistinguishable at every moment the chain evaluates them. The shadow mechanism relies on exactly these monomorphic pairs, and it survives finite $\varepsilon N$: in the agent-based Moran process at $\varepsilon N \in \{0.1, 1, 10\}$ the conditional cooperator's share peaks at $\varepsilon N \approx 1$ (1.6%) and falls to its $\mu$-weight at $\varepsilon N = 10$; standing variance keeps unconditional cooperators alive as mutants, which raises the mean payoff only because exploited pairs beat mutual defection in this game ($T + S = -1 > 2P = -2$), not because conditional cooperation is selected (RESULTS.md, "Standing variance").

**Attractors.** The rest points of selection: configurations where every present type has equal fitness ("cool" states). Includes monomorphic states, stable polymorphisms, and neutral sets (e.g. the FairBot–ALLC edge, all at payoff 0). A population at a cool state aggregates into a mixed strategy over programs, and its payoff is that mixture's self-payoff. At finite $N$ a polymorphic island fluctuates around its cool rest point: the time-averaged composition sits on the rest point, while the instantaneous payoff spread among present programs is drift of order $N^{-1/2}$, whatever the migration rate. Coolness is an equilibrium property, not an efficiency one. In Chicken without `ROLE` a three-class cool state pays $-0.341$, below the mixed equilibrium's $-0.182$ (RESULTS.md, "Cool check").

**Attractor chain.** A single mutant $q\sim\mu$ perturbs attractor $A$; selection carries the population to attractor $B$ (possibly $A$, a different rest point, or a new coexistence including $q$). Transitions require $q\in B$: a mutant that dies returns the population to $A$. $P(A\to B) \propto \sum_q \mu(q)\Pr[\text{selection from } A{+}q \text{ settles at } B]$. Attractors are discovered by reachability from the monomorphic seeds.

**The ecology.** $\pi$, the stationary distribution of the attractor chain: the fraction of time the population spends in each stable configuration. $\pi$ is $\varepsilon$-independent within the regime.

**Indeterminacy rule.** If selection from some perturbed state does not converge to a rest point (a genuine limit cycle — rock-paper-scissors under replicator), the chain's premise fails and the result is reported as **indeterminate, citing the cycle**. Bistable switching — two rest points with mutation-driven transitions between them, such as the FairBot–ALLC edge and the all-ALLD sink — is *not* a cycle: selection converges every time, $\pi$ is well-defined, and the model predicts time-shares.

**Reporting.** Always report the **support and transition structure** of $\pi$ alongside its weights — which rest points, which single mutants move between them. A $\pi$ spread over qualitatively different states is where weights alone mislead ("60/40" reads as a mixture that never occurs), and the transition structure is where the mechanism lives.

## 6. The canonical object

$\pi$ is defined on the full weakly extensional language. $\mu$ is summable and the convergence bound (§7) makes the countable chain well-defined. The canonical ecology is $\Sigma = \lim_{N\to\infty}\pi_N$.

**One limit, $N$.** Truncation $n$ is a computational device (§7), $\beta$ is fixed at 1, $T$ is infinite, $\varepsilon$ is in regime.

**Why the limit of finite-$N$ chains.** $\mu(q)$ is a first-order weight on every transition at every $N$, so length is always charged. What $N$ controls is which transitions have positive probability: at finite $N$ a mutant that loses on-path can fix by drift ($\sim e^{-cN}$); at $N=\infty$ it cannot. Whether $\lim_N\pi_N$ equals the $N{=}\infty$ chain is open (§9).

**Priority structure.** On-path fitness is lexicographically first in that it *defines the state space*: attractors are rest points of selection. Length and off-path fitness then jointly weight transitions — $\mu(q)$ times the tipping probability — as a product.

**Language dependence.** $\mathcal{L}$ enters only through the Kolmogorov constant: vanishes for universal $\mathcal{L}$, small for a DSL fitted to the game.

## 7. Proof and computation

**Truncation and convergence.** Let $\pi_n$ be the chain on programs of length $\le n$. $\|\pi_n - \pi\|_1 \le \kappa_n r_n$ where $r_n = \sum_{|q|>n}\mu(q)$ and $\kappa_n$ is a condition number of the truncated chain (standard stationary-distribution perturbation bound). The support of $\pi$ is infinite — a long program behaviorally equivalent to a short fit one persists neutrally with tiny mass — so "ecologies have a maximum length" is false; what stabilizes is any fixed-precision approximation, in particular the mode.

**The tail is slow in the worst case.** Under the canonical prior $r_n \sim 1/\log n$, so the perturbation bound is weak at feasible $n$. The *effective* tail is far thinner: most long mutants die on arrival. Measurable as the fraction of length-$n$ programs that tip any attractor; if it decays geometrically the practical error does too. Making that rigorous is open (§9).

**Certification.** Under single-mutant transitions each transition costs exactly $|q|$ bits for the tipping mutant, so the question at level $n$ is: does any program in $\mathcal{L}_{n+1}\setminus\mathcal{L}_n$ tip any attractor of the $n$-chain? If none does, $\pi_{n+1} = \pi_n$ up to neutral mass. Mechanics in IMPLEMENTATION.md.

**The theorem: behavioral characterization.** For a candidate ecology, enumerate what an invader can *do* against it and show every such behavior loses or is neutral. Quantifies over behaviors, not programs; never touches $n$; available in the extensional arms; exhaustive by hand in the strong arm. The separable matching result (§4) and the handshake theorem (§3) are the instances.

**The computation: DSL simulation.** Attractor chain on the DSL at small $N$. Generates the candidate ecology the theorem is about; checks that finite behavior agrees with the characterization. It is not the proof. The DSL, arms, evaluator, chain algorithm, sweep, and repo layout are specified in IMPLEMENTATION.md.

**Predictions and outcomes.** (a) *The strong arm one level below the ¼-grounder has no stable cooperation.* Confirmed: all-D at 0.99–1.00 at $n = 6, 7$, every $w$ and $N \ge 100$. (b) *The strong arm at the ¼-grounder's level shows bistable switching between a FairBot–ALLC edge and all-D.* Failed: the grounded FairBots enter all-D at $\rho < 1/N$ (the ½-grounder at $1.1\times10^{-3}$, the ¼-grounder at $8.6\times10^{-3}$ against $10^{-2}$) and hold $< 10^{-4}$ of $\pi$; the strong arm is all-D. (c) *The weak arm at $n \ge 9$ admits PrudentBot, which collapses the edge.* Not run through the chain; the exhaustive $\mathcal{L}_9$ search for a program that enters all-D, cooperates with itself and is uninvadable from rare is negative, and at $n = 6, 7$ the edge is already collapsed — by the shadow, not by PrudentBot (§3). (d) *`eq(THEM,ME)` wins the source arm.* Confirmed at $w \ge 0.1$, $N = 100$: the clique spellings hold 0.64–0.90 of $\pi$. (e) *Stag Hunt.* All-Hare at $N \le 100$ for $w \le 0.3$ and all-Stag at $N = 1000$ in both arms, at every $w$: the basin choice is a valley-crossing competition decided by $N w$. (f) *Ultimatum* (predictions/2026-09-19-ultimatum.md): the `ROLE` arm's verdict (spread, no concentration above 0.6) was not falsified (all-M 0.37, all-L 0.35); the fixed-role verdicts — Nash bargaining $(M\,|\,M)$ in the mutual arm, responder commitment at $H$ in the one-sided arm — were both falsified by the subgame-perfect $(L\,|\,L)$; the symmetry break (responder outside option 0.3) moved the mode in the predicted direction but to $M$, not to the predicted nearest level $H$. ## 8. Evaluation — never selection

Reported as findings about $\pi$; never used to choose it:

- **Efficiency:** deadweight loss per interaction. Prediction: small but positive (the maintenance cost of conditionality).
- **Distribution:** on the Nash demand game, does the ecology land on the Nash split (Young 1993)? In separable form: which $\lambda$-ratio is selected among reciprocators at different rates? With `ROLE`: does it maximize the unweighted sum (§3)?
- **Coolness:** holds by construction (attractors are cool states); a rest-point diagnostic. Compatible with pairwise exploitation.

The moment any of these becomes a selector, the ecology reports back what was put in.

## 9. Open questions

1. **$\lim_N$ in coordination games.** The Stag basin flips from Hare to Stag between $N = 100$ and $N = 1000$ (both arms); where the threshold sits in $N w$, and whether $\pi_N$ converges beyond it. (The PD limit is answered: the cooperative share peaks near $N = 10^3$ and tends to 0 like $N^{-1/2}$, with the faker as the binding exit; §3.)
2. **The faker problem, and a characterization.** In $\lim_N$ drift exits vanish like $1/N$, and an exit survives only if it is a strict invasion. Conjecture: $\Sigma$ is efficient iff the language contains a program supporting the efficient outcome that (i) enters the inefficient state neutrally at one copy, so that $\rho_{\text{enter}}$ decays no faster than a power of $N$, which excludes grounding cost, and (ii) has no strict invader, meaning it is unfakeable. `THEM(^C)` fails (ii); the grounded FairBots fail (i); the source-arm cliques satisfy both, at the price of weak extensionality. The candidate that satisfies both while conditioning on behaviour is a provability-based self-reference primitive, a modal arm. It re-opens "Black-magic fixed points" in REJECTED.md: self-reference guarded by $\Box$ has unique fixed points (de Jongh–Sambin), so no selection rule does hidden work. Its observation channel, provable behaviour, sits between the weak and source arms. Whether that channel is acceptable is a modelling decision. The shadow problem (§3) is the finite-$N$ version of this question. *First test:* in the modal arm, where FairBot meets both conditions by construction, the ε→0 chain shows no peak. Cooperation rises to P(C,C) = 0.73 at $N = 3\cdot10^4$ ($w = 0.3$). The cooperative-to-D ratio grows like $N^{0.44}$. Exits are neutral drift into ALLC, $\propto 1/N$ (RESULTS.md, "Modal (Löbian) arm"). That is consistent with the conjecture's 'if' direction. Two limits apply: the arm builds in the two conditions, and it also drops X and `ROLE`, which confounds the comparison with the weak arm. The matched control has since been run (RESULTS.md, "E1 matched control"). It uses identical grammars and priors, differing only in whether an application is simulated or provability-checked. Simulation peaks and falls to 0.009 at $N = 3\cdot10^4$; provability rises to 0.70, with exponent 0.48. Provability alone carries the effect: it removes the constant faker exit and also makes entry cheaper. At finite $\varepsilon N$, a well-mixed modal population of 6,400 holds P(C,C) = 0.99 from any start (RESULTS.md, "E3 finite-εN"). A standing defector fringe prunes the unconditional shadow, which the $\varepsilon\to0$ chain cannot represent. *Priced provers* (RESULTS.md, "Priced arm"). Charging proofs by work restores both obstacles in a well-mixed population:
- *An entry barrier,* exponential in $N$.
- *A price ladder:* cheaper on-path equivalents strictly invade, and ALLC, which pays nothing, invades FairBot.

Spatial structure removes the barrier: entry on the torus is $N$-independent even where the well-mixed value is $10^{-13}$. It does not remove the ladder.

*Lazy* pricing removes the ladder. It is free against constant opponents and exact copies, and pays only against non-trivial strangers. At $n = 6$ it reproduces the free arm. At $n = 8$ it reaches full cooperation through cost-based incumbency, which locks in a cooperative family that punishes ALLC and rivals FairBot's network instead of including it.

So the realizable condition reads: *no price against defectors, and no price advantage for the shadow*. The price that remains, against strangers, favours incumbents. Open: the 'only if' direction, and whether any pricing yields one cooperative network rather than rival ones.

*How fast must compute get cheap?* (RESULTS.md, "Price scaling paths"). This is stated as a proposition about the two-edge reduction (entry ρ(FB | D) against the ALLC exit ρ(C | FB), with explicit fixation integrals). Along c_N = c0·N^(−α), cooperation tends to 1 iff α > 1/2:
- the ladder takes the odds as N^(α−1+β), where β is the free arm's odds exponent;
- the barrier, exp(−Θ(c²N)), kills entry below 1/2.

Its empirical premises are (i) β → 1/2 and (ii) the full chain following the reduction. Both are checked through N = 3·10⁵, at n = 6 and n = 8: the local β is 0.49–0.50; every path's slope matched; d(slope)/dα = 1.08. At α = 1/2 the plateau is set by c0. At α = 1, cooperation still tends to 1, with a constant odds penalty of (1 − e^(−w c0 10³))/(w c0 10³). The iterated limit lim_N lim_c is the free arm. So the realizable condition gains a rate: *a price is harmless in the limit iff it vanishes faster than $N^{-1/2}$ of the stakes, or is exempted on the ladder.*

*Certificate pricing* (RESULTS.md, "Certificate pricing (decidability-based free set)"). Checking is free iff PA decides the opponent's action toward the reader. On the GL chain that criterion is almost exactly *monotonicity in box atoms*: box-positive cooperation certifies itself, and only cooperation conditional on non-provability pays. At $n = 8$ it gives one cooperative network containing FairBot (rival share ≤ 0.02, X ≥ 0.955 at $N \ge 10^4$), at the free arm's rate: 0.700 at $N = 3\cdot10^4$ and 0.792 at $10^5$, against 0.725 and 0.814. The controls separate three things:
- the copy subsidy is what produces lock-in, parochial under lazy (P\*) or universal when copies of PrudentBot are subsidized;
- the atom ladder, not the free set, removes the non-monotone rival block;
- an exact zero on certified checks is load-bearing.

So the open question above is answered for $n = 8$, well-mixed and ε→0: one network is achievable, but every mechanism seen so far that beats the shadow's $1/N$ exit is a *moat* (copy subsidy or clique), and a moat chooses its family by prior and exit rate, not by how broadly it cooperates. They cannot be obtained together from game payoffs, or from prices with free constants, without incumbency (RESULTS.md, "Universality against drift-closure"). The setting is deterministic programs in the PD under the chain's fixation rule.

*Lemma 0 (box soundness at the stable world; RESULTS.md, "The scramble lemma"):* the evaluator's shared world chain stops at a global fixed point, so a box true at the stable world covers it, and a boxed pair really plays the boxed action; this alone, with no Löb, makes FairBot's pair unfakeable at every level. Löb buys self-cooperation; soundness buys unfakeability; the two are independent.

*Lemma 1:* from a cooperative world a mutant is a faker (fixing at an $N$-independent rate), an exactly neutral mutual self-cooperator ($1/N$), or deleterious ($e^{-\Theta(N)}$). So a program's neutral closure is its component in the graph of mutual cooperation among self-cooperators.

*Corollary 1:* ALLC lies in FairBot's component and D suckers ALLC, so every drift-closed class cooperates with nothing in FairBot's component. Universality and drift-closure are exclusive. This is the regress of §9.7 made exact.

*Proposition 2:* a non-closed network exits at $\Theta(1/N)$, so its odds are $\Theta(N^{1/2})$. The exponent $\beta = 1/2$ used above is derived, not empirical.

*Proposition 3:* with non-negative prices and free constants, a family beats $1/N$ only if newcomers pay more to read the incumbent than the incumbent pays to read itself, which is a moat.

*A legibility budget* (RESULTS.md, "Bounded provers: a semantic legibility gate"). Gating boxes by a stabilization cost prices out every sibling (cost ratio ≥ 2 at every budget) and still closes nothing at $n \le 8$: each unsuckerable class leaks through a neighbour *cheaper* than itself (ALLC for FairBot, `BOX(THEM(THEM))` for PrudentBot, `not(BOX(THEM(ME)))` for P*), and a reader whose legibility is monotone in opponent cost can never cut those. The budget is otherwise harmless: the $1/N$ leak, the $N^{1/2}$ odds and the ε = 0 lottery are unchanged at every budget, and the gate creates no fakers. A random gate at matched masking, which acts as a tag, does lock in P*. So in this proxy the proof-length route to closure fails for a reason other than siblings, and closure keeps coming from self-recognition. Proxy-specific: the cost does not charge the Löb step, and real proof length is unmeasured. *Measured proof length* (RESULTS.md, "Proof length"). In a cut-free GL sequent calculus on the DSL's own syntax, the free arm's box tables are exactly GL provability (0 disagreements through $n = 8$ and on the level-2 ladder with its siblings), with certified lengths: FairBot cooperates in 4 sequents and 1 Löb step, PrudentBot in 24 and 5, the prudent ladder in 22–54. The cost is set by boxed obligations re-proved in every Löb branch (cut-free GL shares no lemmas), not by program size, and the stabilization proxy is uncorrelated with it. Siblings cost about 2× to read for readers that probe the opponent against D and +1 for the rest; no sibling is cheaper than self. A *sound bounded calculus* K exists, with a joint bounded Löb rule witnessed by its own instance, in which a budgeted program's play is a function of the budgets with no fixed-point selection: FairBot budgets are symmetric with a threshold on $\min(b_x, b_y)$, copies are cheaper than distinct budgets (3 vs 4), so budgets just above the copy threshold are soft cliques; at $n = 6$ K closes nothing at any budget and keeps the chain's cooperation and the lottery from $b = 4$ on, while a random change of as many plays per stratum destroys cooperation, so **soundness, not masking rate, is what makes bounded provers harmless**. K's incompleteness (no reasoning under a box from boxed hypotheses) removes the Gödel-sentence faker of `BOX1(THEM(THEM))`, and K cooperates *more* than the free oracle (+0.06–0.08 at $N = 10^4$): a realizable prover need not be worse than the idealization. A per-match price on budget rebuilds the ALLC price ladder ($\pi(\text{all-D}) = 1$ at $c \ge 0.01$), so whether compute can be charged depends on the price's form, not on proof length. *K at n = 8* (RESULTS.md, "K at n = 8"): the cheap sound prover keeps and improves the whole $n = 8$ free arm from $b = 4$ (+0.05 at $N = 10^4$, odds slope 0.47–0.52 against 0.44, neutral $1/N$ exits, nothing closed at any budget or across budgets, 20/20 lottery), and the entire gain is faker removal, mostly Gödel sentences (deleting the 37 classes K disarms reproduces K to $10^{-4}$). K's incompleteness is selective: it removes exactly the cooperation that depends on Con or on the 4-axiom (P\*, P2, PB2, the level-2 ladder) and keeps PrudentBot from $b = 11$, cheaper than its GL length, so **the realizable cooperative core at $n = 8$ is FairBot's family plus PrudentBot**, and PrudentBot fills P\*'s place in π. Lemma sharing needs weakening-aware certificates (exact-identity DAG shares nothing; analytic cut helps only where a boxed lemma is reused across Löb branches; subsumption saves 30–40% for prudent provers), and siblings stay ≥ 1.96× for prudent readers under every measure. Amortized and cache-accounting prices are the α = 1 path as schedules: $N\Delta = cb$ is $N$-independent, the ALLC exit is weakly selected with fixation ratio $1 + wcb/2$, the exit slope is −1 and cooperation rises with $N$; the per-match price rebuilds the ladder; lazy (copy-subsidy) pricing becomes an absorbing copy-clique exactly when an ALLC punisher exists (PrudentBot@16 at $n = 8$, even at $c = 0.01$), so the copy subsidy is the moat to avoid. *K with the 4-rule* (RESULTS.md, "K with the 4-rule"): a 4-rule is sound for K (proved by induction with the Nec witness) but on the DSL the literal form never fires (Lemma V), and its monotone closure only shortens finite-budget proofs, vanishing by $b = 54$ with the chain unchanged. The Con/4-axiom cooperators stay out because a guard at the reader's own budget asks K to certify its own bounded consistency, which no sound rule can supply within that budget (Lemma G, the second incompleteness theorem in bounded form); read one budget up, PB2 joins at PrudentBot's cost ($b^* = 11$) and no faker returns, while P\*, P2 and the ladder need distribution under a box. K's faker removal is structural (every lost atom underivable up to $2L + 10$) and no single-derivation trigger reproduces it (recall 0.42), so the faker set is a property of the calculus, not of a proof trace: a realizable reader must be the bounded prover itself. Across islands the same incompleteness removes the bridge-less rivals: P\* self-defects in K, so it cannot hold an island, and at $b \ge 12$ every rival of FairBot's pair is bridged (RESULTS.md, "Rivals under the bounded prover K"). The budget then has a lower bound set by compatibility, not cost: below a pair's distinct-budget threshold provers become soft cliques, and soft cliques are bridge-less rivals that make permanent patchworks (0.80 of runs at $b = 4$); the budget that is best in the well-mixed chain is worst across islands. *Traps* (RESULTS.md, "The modal arm on two-player divide-the-dollar"): the working conjecture must be stated per inefficient recurrent state: for each trap some efficient-supporting program must enter it neutrally and be unfakeable. In bargaining P′ is such an entrant for the greedy polymorphism and nothing is one for the P/S5 trap or the Löbian hawk mixtures, and *resistance to profitable certified demands* is a property distinct from unfakeability (P has the second, not the first). *Method:* chain claims about polymorphic traps need the seeded log-domain solver and an account of twin drift; the linear-domain lazy chain hides deep states behind underflowing flows. *The club: closure by declaration* (RESULTS.md, "The closed club"). A membership oracle, `CLUB(THEM)` true iff the opponent is in a fixed point K of "self-cooperates and cooperates only within K", closes a network *as a set* at exactly the clique's rate, $e^{-wN/4}$, with FairBot's entry and the clique's hitting times. It does not evade Corollary 1: its universality is 0, it excludes twice its own mass in unsuckerable programs, and the leak returns inside it as member-fakers, so where π lands is decided by which fixed point is chosen. Membership of a guarded program is self-fulfilling, so every subset of the maximal club is a fixed point and the choice does hidden work; "Black-magic fixed points" is re-closed for membership-level rules. Declared membership is a third route to self-recognition beside syntax and proof length, carrying no more information than a tag. *Proof-carrying contracts* (RS proposal, 2026-10-04; RESULTS.md, "Proof-carrying contracts v1"). A contract read as a bare truth table is self-referentially paradoxical; read through provability it is certified-sound and, among carriers, exactly the ungated free box, so it adds nothing to a reader that can already read source. Validity binds each contract to about one source, so transmission of contracts reduces to source selection and validity breadth is not a selector. Where legibility is lost (a gate under which only constants are legible from source), carried contracts restore cooperation from 0.001 to 0.95–0.99, by production at birth or inheritance from an established carrier population, not by swapping from a μ-drawn 1% seed, which dies by drift. A seed of *prover* carriers establishes where only constants are legible with probability ≈ 0.5 at 1% and ≈ 0.85 at 3% of N = 6,400, rising with N and mostly dying below 0.3% (RESULTS.md, "Prover-carrier seed at b = 0"); there is no carrier–carrier threshold, the barrier is drift through the scramble, and the early advantage came partly from a fringe of non-carriers that trust contracts while remaining unreadable. Under a symmetric gate, where non-carriers read carriers through the gate like anyone else, carriers still establish from 1–3% seeds and win the k = 1 lottery (0.975): the fringe carried most of the post-scramble per-generation advantage but only about 0.1–0.2 of establishment probability, because establishment is decided in the scramble by seed count and drift, not by the growth rate afterwards (RESULTS.md, "The symmetric gate"). Without the fringe the carrier's advantage is the linear carrier–carrier term plus a transient legibility shield, and mutation stripping opens a small deterministic threshold, f* ≈ 0.004. So carrying is heritable legibility in the strict sense: carriers are legible to each other and to nobody else, and that suffices. A contract read and a source read of the same carrier differ even where source is legible (b = ∞), one being the box over the representative's free trace and the other over the carrier's actual trace; they coincide only among carriers. *Amortized verification* (RS proposal, 2026-10-04; derived from RESULTS.md, "Price scaling paths"). If a program verifies an opponent's certificate once and caches it, the per-match price is c/N over the ~N matches of a lifetime. That is the atoms-pricing path α = 1, already measured: cooperation → 1 with exit slope −1, one universal network, and a constant odds penalty of $(1 - e^{-wc})/(wc)$ against free proofs, which is 0.9985 at $c = 0.01$. Both sides pay once, so caching makes no ladder and no moat. Proof-carrying cooperation with amortized checking behaves as free provability. A fixed background fringe acts as a price on constants. It can close families, but at $n \ge 8$ it ends on the parochial P\*.

*Theorem (formerly Conjecture 4; RESULTS.md, "Conjecture 4: the sibling theorem"):* in the modal language with boxes at every PA + Con^k level, no class is drift-closed. For every self-cooperating x that defects on D, the sibling y = `or(x, ψ_K)`, where ψ_K says "your play against D is undecided in PA + Con^K" and K is the latest settling world among x's probes, mimics x against everything x can observe, so it sits next to x in the mutual-cooperation graph, and it is suckered by z = `BOX_K(THEM(^D))`, whose play against D is undecided at level K. The sibling needs one more Con level than x; the fixed-level-cap case is open. No step uses Löb. The mechanism is *extensional*: a reader that sees opponents only through finitely many behavioural probes always has a sibling it cannot distinguish. So drift-closure needs a reader that can tell a sibling from a copy, by syntax (cliques) or by proof length (bounded readers with no budget slack), and both are self-recognition or incumbency. Checked by an independent evaluator on every self-cooperating class at n ≤ 11 (one level) and n ≤ 9 (two levels), and the static map is one component with no closed class through n = 13. FairBot remains leak/μ-optimal (92.2 at n = 13, limit ≈ 89–90, a 1/s² shell tail).

*Lazy pricing on graphs* (RESULTS.md, "Rival networks under lazy pricing on graphs"). Lazy incumbency is a well-mixed effect. On the torus a newcomer cluster's interior is all copies, so it pays only on its border, and P*'s own shadow then exits it at $O(1/N)$, or at an $N$-independent rate at $c = 10^{-1}$. In the ε→0 representative chain on the torus, the universal FairBot network holds 0.95 of the mass at $N = 4{,}096$, but because of its prior mass: with equalized priors, P* holds 0.95. As hypercube degree grows, the lock-in returns. So which cooperative network wins is decided by different things in different regimes:
- *well-mixed:* moats (copy subsidies);
- *torus:* prior mass;
- *finite ε:* the ALLC-punisher that eats the shadow (see 3 below).

*Certificates without provability* (RESULTS.md, "Certificates-only arm"). The grammar is the matched control's, with the same prior. Programs see only honest certificates, and only the semantics of self-reference changes between variants:
- *D-seeded* reasoning (least fixed point) gives no working self-reference. Only fakeable third-party probes cooperate, and the weak arm's faker limit returns: 0.009 at $N = 3\cdot10^4$.
- *C-seeded* reasoning (greatest fixed point, a stated selection rule that re-opens "Black-magic fixed points") makes FairBot a *mirror*: it plays against $y$ what $y$ plays against it. It enters neutrally and has no faker. It rises without a peak to 0.575, in one cooperative network.
- *Tags* absorb into one parochial clique, at P(C,C) = 1.

So what the conjecture's two conditions need is *outcome symmetry*: a short program whose action against $y$ equals $y$'s action against it, and which defects on D. Löb, coinduction and self-recognition are three ways to get it. Löb is the way that needs no selection rule. The C-seeded arm falls short of Löb (0.575 against 0.706 at this grammar) entirely through one fakeable probe, `cooperate iff you cooperate with yourself`. A reader of truth can be faked there by a program whose self-cooperation rests on a true negative fact. No consistent prover can be faked that way. Suppressing those edges closes the gap, and PA + Con(PA) equals PA, so the gap is truth against provability, not logical strength. Certificates alone are not a mechanism; the semantics of self-reference is.
3. **Standing variance — answered.** The shadow survives finite $\varepsilon N$: the conditional cooperator's share peaks at $\varepsilon N \approx 1$ (1.6%) and falls to its $\mu$-weight at $\varepsilon N = 10$; standing variance keeps unconditional cooperators alive as mutants, which raises mean payoff only because exploited pairs beat mutual defection in this game (§5; RESULTS.md, "Standing variance"). *Shadow as food* (RESULTS.md, "Rival networks", R3b and R4; finite-ε approach results, not π). Mutation keeps an ALLC load inside reciprocator domains, x ≈ 0.2 at ε = 10⁻³ on the torus and the hypercube. A program that exploits ALLC and cooperates with itself eats that load at its domain border and overtakes the reciprocator within a few thousand generations at c ≤ 10⁻². Removing ALLC from the mutation supply reverses this. The winning punisher can be outside the universal network (P*) or inside it (PrudentBot).
4. **Spatial structure — what remains.** The $\varepsilon \to 0$ object is measured (§3): $\rho_{\text{enter}}$ local and $N$-independent, $M_{\text{exit}} \propto N^{0.5\text{–}0.8}$ over one doubling of side. Open: (i) whether that exponent persists, so that the $\varepsilon \to 0$ cooperative share tends to 1 slowly or saturates; (ii) the $\varepsilon \to 0$ route into the FairBot sea by drift inside a `THEM(^C)` sea, and $M_{\text{exit}}$ of the combined state; (iii) why the exit under serial single mutants is the shadow drifting to majority (137 of 200 trials) rather than a subsidized D front, and whether that changes the exponent when fronts are allowed (two mutants alive). In the free and lazy modal arms on the torus, the ε→0 object has $N$-independent entry and a $1/N$ shadow exit, so π(D) falls toward $N^{-1}$ (−1.02 over the last doubling of side), against $N^{-1/2}$ well mixed (RESULTS.md, "Rival networks", R2).
5. **Island model — what remains.** At ε = 0 both conjectures are tested (§3, Islands). A holds as a set: all three absorbing families appear under every seeding. As a distribution it fails for placement, not composition: clustered seeding absorbs cooperatively in 6 of 80 PD runs against 50 of 240 shuffled, while filling every spare slot with D changes nothing. B′ fails in the PD and, narrowly, in Chicken with `ROLE`. (i) *answered for $w_g = 0$* (§3, Ergodic islands): the faker sets the exit once $M = IN \gtrsim 860$, and the order of limits matters. With ε→0 first, islands help (π_R rises to a plateau of 0.067 at $N = 100$). With $I \to \infty$ at fixed per-island ε, islands hurt (R-dominant time falls to 0.008). Open: (ii) *answered:* the cooperative absorption probability goes to 0 as the number of islands grows. The ε = 0 lottery concentrates on mutual defection: 100% at 1,024 islands, with none cooperative from 256 (RESULTS.md, "ε = 0 islands vs island count"). That held for uniform-over-programs seeding in L6R; under iid seeding from μ the lottery rises with the island count in every arm, to 1.00 in the modal arm (§3, Almost all seeds), so the canonical ε = 0 answer depends on the seeding law; (iii) whether the Chicken mutual-Swerve islands survive a runtime rule other than divergence → minimax.
6. **Effective tail.** Prove the fraction of length-$n$ mutants that tip any attractor decays geometrically, or find the counterexample.
7. **Drift stability / the regress.** A variant that punishes ALLC but tolerates ALLC-tolerators is itself a leak. The fixed point is the fully self-referential reciprocator resolved by grounding, and grounding is what makes its entry probability fall below $1/N$ (§3). *Made exact* (§9.2, Corollary 1): universality and drift-closure exclude each other in the pure game. Each added order of prudence costs one Con level (orders 1 and 2 together need PA + Con²). Per unit of prior mass, FairBot leaks least of any unsuckerable class. The sibling theorem (§9.2) closes the regress: each added order of prudence has a sibling exactly one Con level up, so no amount of prudence escapes it. A third route to closure, declared membership (the club), is a clique over a predicate and leaks internally (§9.2).
8. **Which $\lambda$ in multi-action separable games, and in fixed-role games.** Reciprocators at every rate are mutually neutral under best-reply; imitation with finite matching should select $\lambda\to 1$ (Robson–Vega-Redondo). Fixed roles select the subgame-perfect split in the ultimatum (§3); which outside options or which grammars restore bargaining is open. Fixed-role multi-player bargaining selects the asymmetric split, not the Nash or fair one, as the fixed-role ultimatum game did: in both, the selected outcome is the one whose deviations are generous rather than self-punishing (RESULTS.md, "Three-player majority divide-the-dollar").
9. **$k$-player generalization.** Programs become $(k{-}1)$-ary; payoff object is a $k$-tensor; attractor chain unchanged; behavioral characterization scales; DSL enumeration does not. The original coalitional question is answered by the process: only single mutants, by construction. *First k = 3 result* (RESULTS.md, "Three-player majority divide-the-dollar"): with fixed roles and separate slot populations in the majority divide-the-dollar game, the ε→0 object at n = 6 is a rotating-pair chain: pairs hold ≈ 0.98 of π, unfair against fair about 0.62 : 0.37, N-independent from N = 10³; the grand coalition gets 0.003–0.017. The pair exit is the excluded slot's neutral drift to a better offer followed by the pivot's strict switch (∝ 1/N), pivots only ratchet up, and the net current runs fair → unfair → wasteful → fair. Source reading adds nothing: modal equals simulation equals constants to three decimals apart from the grand coalition. The new obstacle for k ≥ 3 is that an exit can be a *partner's* strict defection to a third party's offer, which no program reading its partner can prevent; unfakeability does not cover it. The constant grand coalition is drift-closed at n = 1 and loses closure at n = 6 to one-atom "accept a pair offer" bridges, a k = 3 instance of the sibling theorem's lesson. *The union game* (RESULTS.md, "The union game"): with a boss slot and two worker slots under fixed roles, π sits at zero wage (0.96–0.99) and equal division gets ≤ 0.005; source reading and a quorum atom add nothing, since the quorum is FairBot's handshake on strikes with an untagged twin. Two mechanisms: refusal is neutral only where it is free (zero wage), where it is swamped by the mass-heavy constants, so the prior decides the fair share (0.31 uniform over named programs, 0.001 length prior); and a *polarity dilemma*: in PA alone a strike pact needs the positive box of the *low* wage to resolve by Löb, and that box is fakeable by a boss that pays fair only at the bottom world, while the unfakeable polarity, "provably fair", cannot carry a strike pact. Unfakeability is the binding issue for distribution as it was for the dilemma. Island selection on bosses spreads cheap exploitation and deterrence, not realized repression, and cheap repression raises efficiency by suppressing strikes. *Enforcement* (RESULTS.md, "Incentive-compatible enforcement in the union game"): making the stage-2 moves ex-post rational inside the per-world evaluator, with boxes on implemented actions (Lemma 0 survives), shows that the ε→0 chain scores committed policies without executing them, so a *non-credible* committed threat deters: the boss's strike-targeting threat, worth −c if called and never called on path, holds 0.53 of π, and removing it raises the uniform-prior fair share from 0.31 to 0.55. The workers' commitment is necessary for any wage above zero (rational workers never strike into a committed threat, CR ≈ 0.998 zero wage) but not sufficient. With both sides rational the chain selects the smallest positive wage, the ultimatum game's subgame-perfect logic: a rational strike is credible only where it is free, and in the full language under the length prior the heavy constant striker becomes a free enforcer of the 1/4 wage (intermediate 0.75, N-independent, workers 0.19 against 0.005, boss 1.62 against 1.36), which Pareto-dominates the committed game. So "the prior decides" was partly an artefact of committed play, and distribution results must be stated relative to both the prior and the commitment structure. A replacement pool (credible, profitable repression) drives the wage to zero in every arm; efficiency is maximal exactly where workers get nothing. The polarity dilemma survives in the two-level language: the unfakeable "¬□fair" pact's handshake can activate only on a world-0 low wage (every box is vacuously true at world 0) and so inherits a world-0 faker, "fair only while provably struck"; lifting the wage check to BOX1 kills activation altogether. *Method:* a per-world best-response override changes what the chain tests; any compute price that makes commitment costly is also a selection on which threats get tested.
10. **Formal regime for $\varepsilon$.** "Small but $\gg e^{-cN}$" as a joint asymptotic.
11. **Runtime as a selection pressure.** Re-opened, not adopted. The case for it: in a Turing-complete language with source access, compute cost is what separates the ways of meeting both conditions of the characterization (§9.2). Those ways are self-recognition (`eq`, cheap), proof search (`BOX_k`, expensive) and certificate checking (cheap to read, paid in length). A free-compute instrument cannot rank them. The case against, from REJECTED.md: runtime depends on the opponent, so a program can impose cost on its reader, and a price on runtime favours CliqueBot-shaped short-circuits. The one form tried, a finite budget with timeout to the minimax action, failed and stays rejected. What form a compute price should take, if any, is open. Nothing is assumed about it. Two constraints from the priced runs (RESULTS.md, "Price scaling paths" and "Certificate pricing"): a price must vanish faster than $N^{-1/2}$ of the stakes or be exactly zero on the ladder's rungs, since any positive charge on certified checks rebuilds the ladder; and exemptions for copies create moats. Any compute price that beats the shadow's $1/N$ exit is incumbency (§9.2, Proposition 3). A background fringe is a price on constants.
