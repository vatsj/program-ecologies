*Step 0: why bullies beat firm readers.* Exact invasion assays (one invader copy, other slots monomorphic, A_f charges included; rho * N, 1 = neutral; the full Moran process was also run: 10^4-trial jump chain on 420 assays max |z| 3.4, the real kernel on 24 non-neutral assays max |z| 2.0, neutral twin 0.00513 +- 0.00016 against 0.005). *What it says about the long-term goal.* Readers that refuse the bully (P), offer to its victim (O) or both do not change the outcome on this grid: the unequal island (bully on 2/3 + a constant on 1/3) and the fair island both strongly resist single-slot entry, and most islands' outcome is settled in the founding scramble. Among counted spellings, P and O are far rarer than the fair reader (10^-13 even with a probe primitive; lower bounds of a grammar, not class-mass ratios). The RE's model-dependent hypothesis: the stable-set target for a length prior over L_T needs a mechanism acting on the founding basin or on two slots at once; this test excluded only third parties of these kinds (literal-only probes, no punishment or side payments, exploiter mass kept at 0.01·δ/0.09 in every arm).

*Caveats.* One 1/3 constant as the probe; O does not punish; the idealized table freezes 16% of profiles at the null action; masses are lower bounds of a grammar (R0's two-reader search carried a label bug, negligible); R2's enumeration stops at size 4; finite schedules; E10 and the decomposition are exploratory; the F2 control differs from F in fallback.
