# Kovalenkov and Wooders, "Epsilon cores of games and economies with limited side payments" (Warwick WP 536, rev. May 1999; GEB 2001)

Read in full from the PDF the RS supplied (not committed). Reading note for the leader-value core thread.

**Setting.** NTU games in characteristic form (N, V): V(S) closed, comprehensive, superadditive, depending on S only.
No strategic game, no outsiders' responses, no alpha/beta distinction.

**Parameters of a collection G((δ,T),(β,B)).** T approximate player types, players of a type being δ-substitutes
(swapping them moves payoff sets by at most δ). β-effective B-bounded groups: restricting to coalitions of at most B
players loses at most β per player. This form of small-group effectiveness excludes games with unrestricted side
payments.

**Theorem 1 (nonemptiness).** Given T, B, a per-capita payoff bound C and thickness ρ (every type is at least a share ρ
of the players): for every ε > 0 there is a size η(ε,T,B,C,ρ) such that every game in the collection with V(N) convex,
ρ-thick, per-capita bounded and at least η players has a nonempty equal-treatment (ε+δ+β)-core. Convexity of V(N)
and thickness cannot be dropped (Examples 2, 3); small-group effectiveness cannot be dropped (Example A1: coalitions
need two thirds of the players, and the 1/7-core is empty at every size). The paper notes lotteries as a way to get
convexity.

**Theorem 2 (equal treatment).** If in addition coalitions of at most B players can transfer payoff at rate q > 0
(q-comprehensive), and a type has more than B players, then any ε-core payoff gives two players of that type payoffs
within (2B/q)(ε+δ+β). The bound is tight (Example 4), fails with unrestricted side payments (Example 5) and fails at
q = 0 (Example 6).

**Not in the paper.** Convergence to competitive prices as such (it treats the core as a stand-in for competitive
equilibrium and proves equal treatment); it points to Wooders 1994b "Equivalence of games and markets" (Econometrica)
for that, and to Kaneko and Wooders 1989 (JET) for a continuum economy with widespread externalities and finite
coalitions.

**For us (the RE's reading).**
- Majority division is Example A1's kind of game: no small effective groups, so no approximate core at any size.
- Pairwise-matched population games (the PD in pairs) have strictly effective 2-bounded groups and fit the framework.
- The leader-value core does not fit directly: V must be superadditive, and leader values with first-mover advantage
  are not (the existence sweep's empty cases were singleton claims that do not add up).
- V(N) convex is what the grand coalition's coin supplies.
