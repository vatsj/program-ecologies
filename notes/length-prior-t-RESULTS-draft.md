*What it says about the long-term goal.* Realizable source-reading cooperation needs readers, and a length prior over a Turing-complete syntax gives them 10^-10 of the constants' mass or less (R1, with a read primitive) and about 10^-23 (R0, reading written out), in a prior that is 72-88% programs that forfeit. Reading cost matters by 13 orders (R0 against R1), but even the cheap machine is 5 orders below any reader share at which arm T has been seen to work (delta* >= 1e-4 anywhere on the grid, and none at the measured bully ratio). The result does not say the Solomonoff prior fails: it says that the *hand-set* library prior of arm T was carrying the finding (a 9% reader share and no bullies), and that a length prior over this syntax puts the weight on junk, on constants whose mass is set by the Elias code, and on conditionals that are not the library's.

*Caveats.* s0 = 6 / 5 against the spec's 9; the reader lower bounds are of a grammar, not complete; lib / libsrc / sim terms are sampled, not enumerated; the machine is two points on one axis (the cost of reading); the join is a projection onto T+ (other conditionals, 15% of R1's finite mass, are omitted); finite schedules.
