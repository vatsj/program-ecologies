## 1. Facts used in the write-up

**F1 (the matched weak arm has no reader shadow).** In the weak arm an atom reads the opponent's action in the
current encounter by simulation from bottom. If both programs have an essential atom, both stay bottom at every
iteration (each atom reads the other's bottom), so both diverge and play the minimax action S1. Hence every reader
earns 1/6 against itself and against every other reader, and no reader is a neutral neighbour of S3 (a neutral
neighbour must earn 1/2 against itself). S3 is a strict best reply to itself among constants, and every reader that
plays S3 against S3 loses 1/2 − 1/6 against its own copies: every exit from all-S3 is deleterious with a barrier
linear in N. (In the published weak `dollar5` arm the accommodating shadow was a *probe* reader,
`flip(THEM(^S3))`, which never simulates itself; this grammar has no probes.)

**F2 (P′'s fixation into the greedy polymorphism).** G = S5 2/3 + A5 1/3 (A5 = `if(BOX(S5), S1, S3)`), mean 5/18.
P′ = `if(BOX(S1), S5, S3)` earns 0 against S5 and 5/6 against A5 (certified below), so its first-order payoff is
5/18 = the mean. Under the chain's lumped resident, u(P′,P′) = 1/2 and the residents earn 1/18 against P′, so the
fitness gap at k copies is w·(4/9)·k/N and
ρ(P′ | G) ≈ (Σ_k exp(−2wk²/(9N)))⁻¹ ≈ √(8w/(9πN)) = 9.2·10⁻³ (N = 10³), 2.9·10⁻³ (N = 10⁴) at w = 0.3, matching
the chain's numbers. First-order neutral, positively frequency-dependent, N^(−1/2): the modal PD's FairBot-into-all-D
rate, for the same reason (self-play beats the residents' payoff against the entrant).

**F3 (P helps the greedy).** P = `if(BOX(S1), S5, if(BOX(S5), S1, S3))` plays S3 against S3, itself and P′ (no atom
provable), so it is a neutral neighbour of S3; it plays S1 against S5 (BOX(S5) provable in 2 sequents), so S5 invades
all-P strictly (5/6 vs 1/2); on {S5, P}: u_P = 1/6 + x/3, u_S5 = 5x/6, the mixture is x_P = 1/3 (sol's number),
and in the reduced subsystem neither P′ (P never provably concedes, so P′ plays S3 and earns 1/2 only against P) nor
S3 can re-enter it: S5/P is a polymorphism whose every exit is deleterious.

**F4 (the Löbian hawk).** X = `if(BOX(S1), S1, S4)` demands 2/3 unless the opponent provably demands 1/6. Against
itself, ⊢ P^{S1}[X,X] by Löb (certified below), so two copies settle on (1/6, 1/6) instead of clashing at (2/3, 2/3).
Against Z = `if(BOX1(S3), S3, S2)` (a PA + Con shadow of S3 that concedes 1/3 to anyone not provably fair) it gets
(2/3, 1/3); against V = `if(BOX1(S4), S2, S4)` also (2/3, 1/3). A hawk that coordinates with its own copies on the
meek split earns 1/6 rather than 0 in self-play, which is what makes hawk–dove mixtures such as X 4/7 + Z 2/7 +
V 1/7 (efficiency 0.653, mean payoff 0.381) resistant to every single mutant at first order. S4 itself cannot do
this (S4 vs S4 clash), which is why the S4–accommodator mixtures of N = 10³ are shallow.

**F5 (deep states and their twins).** A state is *deep* if every outside mutant is deleterious at first order or
first-order neutral with a frequency penalty under the chain's lumped resident; every exit then has a barrier linear
in N. At modal n = 7 there are 47 deep states with ≤ 3 types (14 two-type, 33 three-type; none monomorphic; encounter
efficiency 0.27–0.85), all hawk–dove mixtures of Löbian hawks with S2/S3 doves; the matched weak arm has 27 (S3 and 26
inefficient mixtures). **Every one of the 47 modal deep states has a support twin** (a program with identical payoffs
against every resident and itself, e.g. Z = `if(BOX1(S3), S3, S2)` and its PA twin `if(BOX(S3), S3, S2)`) **whose
relabelled state is strictly invadable.** In a finite Moran population a twin drifts neutrally inside its type's
compartment (fixation 1/(x_r N)), so the true exit from such a state is two-step and polynomial (the shadow mechanism
inside a polymorphism); the chain's lumped-resident fixation charges the twin a frequency penalty and calls the state
deep. `src/modal_dollar.py` (`twin_of`, `edge_logweights(twins=True)`) adds the twin moves at rate μ_q/(x_r N) as a
correction.
