## The RS's framing (verbatim, registered first)

> I think the interpretation of these certificates as "genes" ... helps make sense of commitment races as a sort
> of co-evolutionary arms race in a competitive game. Some threats are fit ... I'd like to confer the union the power
> to special-case when it sees a quorum, but don't love the idea of letting the bosses special-case a threat.
> ... I propose that certain classes of credible deviation get encoded as a gene on the spot. This suggests an
> interpretation of a gene as a deviation from an equilibrium condition on the underlying program. I'm excited about
> this as a proof device: no credible deviations (say, pareto improvements) exist, bc if they did they would've been
> adopted as a fit gene. I propose we stress-test this for existing equilibrium notions ... mb bayes-nash? The
> strategies take some closed parametric form (... a single-digit number of parameters. The point is we can't j
> hard-code genes) and genes are flexible enough to express anything strategies miss.

*Scored where it makes a claim:* (a) **the asymmetry** ("the union may special-case on a quorum; the boss may not
special-case a threat"): whether any credibility filter derives it, and what declaring it (G-asym) does to the fair
share against G-all; (b) **the proof device** ("no credible deviation, say a Pareto improvement, exists, else it would
have been adopted as a fit gene"): at the chain's top states, whether an admissible single gene is a strict
mutant-program improvement, and whether the top states are Pareto-efficient (a Pareto-dominated top state with no
strict gene improvement is a counterexample to the device as stated).
