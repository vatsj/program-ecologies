## Measure

- *Program IDs*: canonical boolean functions of atoms (the DP's finest unit; programs with the same canonical function
  play identically against everything under every K). Behavioural classes (identical payoff row and column under P_K,
  `modal.ModalProvider`) are formed only after K is solved. A class is "in K" if its canonical members are.
- *Prior*: the length prior of the modal arm over the language in question (bits = log2 a(s) + 2 log2 s + 1).
- *F(K)* = {x : x plays C against itself under P_K, and against no program of L_n outside K}.
- *Suckerable*: some y in L_n with x(y) = C and y(x) = D under P_K. *Neutral entrant into all-a*: y with u(y, a) = u(a, a),
  u(a, y) = u(a, a) and u(y, y) = u(a, a).
- *Chain*: ε→0 chain (`chain.Chain`), PD, w = 0.3, `eager_poly=False`, θ = 10⁻⁶, on the club arm at the chosen fixed
  point; controls: the free modal arm (`modal.build`) and the clique arm (`modal.build_priced(n, 0, m_cliques=1)`) at
  the same cells. Exit and entry rates from `ergodic_islands.log_fixation` (log domain). A log-domain monomorphic
  embedded chain (GTH elimination with logaddexp) gives π at N where the linear chain underflows; residuals of both are
  reported.
- *Top club state*: the monomorphic K state with largest π; club-FairBot's state is reported as well.

## RE predictions (Fable, from the spec)

1. **K is empty in the base language** at n ≤ 9, and non-empty with the `CLUB` atom, containing club-FairBot
   `and(BOX(THEM(ME)),CLUB(THEM))` and its `BOX1` and `THEM(THEM)` variants. At n = 8, K has between 3 and 30
   classes and μ-mass below 0.01. *Falsifier:* K non-empty in the base language, or K at n = 8 with μ-mass above 0.05.
2. **K is drift-closed:** no neutral entrant into all-club-FairBot outside K, no suckerable member. *Falsifier:* either
   found by enumeration.
2b. **The joint operator has a unique maximal fixed point** at n = 6 and 7, reached from the full set, and the empty set
   is the only other fixed point; no cycles. *Falsifier:* two incomparable maximal fixed points, or a cycle.
3. **Residence in K is exponential.** Every exit from the top club state is deleterious with rate below e^(−0.2·N); the
   fitted exit slope is steeper than −3 over N ∈ [10², 10³]. Conditional on entry, π(K) ≥ 0.99 at every N ≥ 10² at both
   n. *Falsifier:* any strict or neutral exit, or π(K) < 0.9 at any cell.
4. **Entry equals FairBot's exactly.** The 2×2 payoff matrices of club-FairBot and FairBot against D coincide, so
   ρ(club-FairBot | all-D) = ρ(FairBot | all-D) to numerical precision. Hitting time from all-D into K is within 2× of
   the clique's at m = 1, scaled by the μ-ratio. *Falsifier:* the matrices differ, or the rates differ by more than 10⁻⁶.
5. **Collateral is large:** the unsuckerable-but-excluded mass (FairBot's family, PrudentBot) exceeds K's own mass by at
   least 10× at n = 8. The club's old-sense universality is exactly 0. *Falsifier:* excluded unsuckerable mass below K's
   mass.
6. **Span is small but not a clique.** The within-K mutual-cooperation graph at n = 8 has a component containing at
   least 2 behaviourally distinct members; but K holds fewer than 10% of the self-cooperating classes. *Falsifier:* the
   within-K graph is a disjoint union of singleton behavioural classes, or K exceeds 10%.
7. **Cutoff dependence.** At most 20% of the members at n drop out at n + 1, and every non-guarded member (if any) drops
   out by n = 9. *Falsifier:* more than half of the members at n = 7 gone at n = 8.

## Subagent predictions (made from the definitions, before any evaluation)

S1. *F is deflationary: F(K) ⊆ K for every K.* Proof: x ∈ F(K) plays C against itself and against no program outside
   K, so x ∉ K would contradict x(x) = C. Hence iteration from any K₀ is a decreasing chain that stops at a fixed point,
   and **no cycle is possible**; the "no cycles" half of 2b holds by proof. (A run that reports a cycle means a bug.)
S2. *Many fixed points.* Any guarded program g = `and(CLUB(THEM), ψ)` cooperates only inside K whatever K is, so its
   membership is self-fulfilling: if g ∈ K and ψ holds of g against itself, g ∈ F(K); if g ∉ K, g defects on itself.
   So {`CLUB(THEM)`} alone is a fixed point, as is {club-FairBot}, and so on. I predict **2b fails on "the empty set is
   the only other fixed point"**: at n = 7 the 20 random starts reach at least 5 distinct fixed points. I predict the
   maximal fixed point is unique (the limit from the full set contains every fixed point found, and the union of the
   fixed points found is itself a fixed point), but with low confidence.
S3. *`CLUB(THEM)` itself* ("cooperate iff you are a member", 3 nodes) is in K at every n with the atom, carries the
   largest μ in K (equal to FairBot's), and is not behaviourally club-FairBot from n = 8 on.
S4. *Prediction 2 fails on suckerability at n = 8.* `and(CLUB(THEM),not(BOX(THEM(ME))))` (8 nodes) self-cooperates
   (C at every world from 1 on), so it is in K, and it defects on `CLUB(THEM)` because `CLUB(THEM)` provably cooperates
   with every member. So `CLUB(THEM)` is suckerable by a member. At n ≤ 7 I expect no suckerable member. The
   neutral-entrant half of 2 holds by construction (a neutral entrant into a K world is cooperated with, so it is in K).
S5. *K is closed as a set, but not every K state is.* Every member defects on every non-member, and a non-member mutant
   earns at most −1 against a resident earning 0, so every exit from K to outside K is deleterious. Exits *within* K
   are possible: strict (S4's faker of `CLUB(THEM)`) and neutral (members that mutually cooperate). So prediction 3's
   "every exit from the top club state is deleterious" fails whenever the top state is `CLUB(THEM)` or has a
   mutually cooperating member as a neighbour, which I expect at both n.
S6. *Exit rate out of K.* The cheapest way out is a self-cooperating non-member that defects on the residents (FairBot
   in an all-member world): a symmetric coordination game with gap 1, so log ρ ≈ −w·N/4 = −0.075·N, slower than the
   e^(−0.2·N) of prediction 3, which therefore **fails numerically**. Its log-log slope over N ∈ [10², 10³] is about
   −25, so the "steeper than −3" half holds. π(K) ≥ 0.99 at every N ≥ 10³ at both n; at N = 10² I expect ≥ 0.9.
S7. *Entry.* Every member defects on D (D is not a member), so every member has FairBot's 2×2 matrix against D and the
   same ρ, not only club-FairBot. 4 holds exactly. The hitting time into K is shorter than the clique's at m = 1 (K's
   mass exceeds μ(FairBot) because it contains `CLUB(THEM)` plus more).
S8. *Mass.* μ(K) at n = 8 between 0.004 and 0.02 (`CLUB(THEM)` alone is about 0.003), so the μ-mass half of
   prediction 1 holds; the class count is the uncertain half.
S9. *Positive control.* The 'pos' grammar still admits S4's faker: its `not` sits above a box, not above `CLUB`. So
   I predict the control keeps a suckerable member at n = 8. Empirical monotonicity of F (K ⊆ K' ⇒ F(K) ⊆ F(K') on
   random nested pairs) I expect to hold in 'pos' and fail in 'full'.
