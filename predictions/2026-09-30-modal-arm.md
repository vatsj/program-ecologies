# Predictions: the modal (Löbian) arm in the PD, 2026-09-30

Written and committed before any chain cell of the modal arm was run. Executions so far:
- a reference check of the evaluator (`tests/test_modal.py`): all 1,040,400 pairs at n = 6 agree with direct
  Kripke recursion on syntax trees;
- validation against the known modal-agent outcomes: FairBot/FairBot C, FairBot/DefectBot D, PrudentBot/
  CooperateBot D, PrudentBot/FairBot C, and so on;
- the single-edge static rates quoted below.

## The arm (`src/modal.py`)

**Grammar.**

    A ::= C | D | not A | and A A | or A A | BOX(App) | BOXD(App) | BOX1(App) | BOXD1(App)
    App ::= THEM(ME) | THEM(THEM) | THEM(^A)

- `BOX` means provably C; `BOXD` means provably D.
- `BOX1` and `BOXD1` are the same, but provable in PA + Con(PA).
- **Why a second level is needed.** PA cannot prove that FairBot defects against DefectBot, since that
  needs Con(PA). LaVictoire et al. (2014) use PA + 1 in PrudentBot's second clause.
- **Node cost.** The box is the application node, so FairBot `BOX(THEM(ME))` costs 3 nodes, like `THEM(ME)`
  in the weak arm. PrudentBot `and(BOX(THEM(ME)),BOXD1(THEM(^D)))` costs 8.
- **Excluded.** There is no X and no `ROLE`: the arm is deterministic, and `ROLE` is inert in the PD. There
  is also no unboxed simulation: these are pure modal agents of rank 0.

**Semantics.** Provability logic GL on the linear Kripke chain.
- At world n, `BOX` holds iff its statement holds at every world m < n. `BOX1` holds iff it holds at every
  m with 1 ≤ m < n.
- Values stabilize, and the stable value is the outcome.
- Every self-reference is guarded by a box, so fixed points are unique (de Jongh–Sambin).

**Re-opened REJECTED entry: "Black-magic fixed points".** The objection there was that non-unique fixed
points need a selection rule that does hidden work. Guarded self-reference has a unique fixed point, so no
selection rule is involved. The arm is an instrument: a free box idealizes proof search at zero cost, so
it is an upper bound.

**Prior and sizes.** Same length prior as the weak arm. The language has 1,020 / 4,542 / 19,544 / 89,842
programs at n = 6 / 7 / 8 / 9, which form 51 / 172 / 471 / 863 behavioural classes.

## Static facts at w = 0.3

- **Nothing strictly invades FairBot or PrudentBot.** The maximum over all q of u(q, FB) − u(FB, FB) is 0,
  and likewise for PrudentBot.
- **FairBot's exits are all neutral drift.** The neutral classes of all-FairBot carry μ ≈ 0.485, of which
  0.480 is exploitable once fixed, mostly ALLC with μ(C) = 0.466. The exit rate is about μ/N:
  4.8·10⁻³ at N = 100 and 1.6·10⁻⁵ at N = 30,000.
- **PrudentBot's exploitable neutral mass is 0.005,** because ALLC is not neutral against it. But
  μ(PrudentBot) ≈ 1.5·10⁻⁶.
- **Entry.** FairBot into all-D has ρ = 0.042 / 0.014 / 0.0044 / 0.0025 at N = 100 / 1,000 / 10⁴ / 3·10⁴.
  That is ∝ N^(−1/2), since FairBot is neutral at one copy.
- **The ratio of cooperative to all-D mass.** Roughly μ(FB)·ρ(FB | D) / (μ(C)/N), which is 0.05 / 0.15 /
  0.5 / 0.8 at N = 100 / 1,000 / 10⁴ / 3·10⁴. It grows like N^(1/2).

## Verdicts

Cells: n = 9 at w ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1}, and n ∈ {6, 8} at w = 0.3. N ∈ {100, 300, 1,000, 3,000, 10⁴, 3·10⁴}.

1. **No peak.** π-weighted P(C,C) rises monotonically in N at every w and n. At w = 0.3 and n = 9 it is
   within a factor of 1.5 of 0.04 / 0.12 / 0.3 / 0.45 at N = 100 / 1,000 / 10⁴ / 3·10⁴. The weak arm instead
   peaked at N ≈ 10³ and fell.
2. **No strict exits.** Every exit from all-FairBot is by a neutral mutant. The faker share is exactly 0,
   and the total exit rate scales like N^(−1), with a fitted slope in [−1.1, −0.9].
3. **Entry.** ρ(FairBot | all-D) has a fitted slope in [−0.55, −0.45] over N ∈ [10³, 3·10⁴].
4. **ALLC is the main destination** of all-FairBot's exits, at least 0.9 of exit flow.
5. **PrudentBot is present but negligible.** π(all-PrudentBot) < 0.01 at n = 8 and 9, since its μ is
   ~10⁻⁶. The cooperative mass at n = 9 is within ±20% of n = 6 at every N. PrudentBot's exclusion of ALLC
   does not matter at these N.
6. **Against the weak arm.** At N = 3·10⁴ modal P(C,C) is at least 10× the weak arm's 0.0044.

**Falsifier.** Either of the following refutes the claim that the modal arm is efficient in the limit:
- π(cooperative) at N = 3·10⁴ below its value at N = 10³ for any w, meaning a peak;
- any strict-invader exit from all-FairBot.

**What each outcome would mean.**
- *If 1–4 hold:* the modal arm meets both conditions of the characterization conjecture. It is the first
  instrument with cooperation rising toward 1 in lim_N, like 1 − c·N^(−1/2). The remaining obstacle is only
  the shadow's μ-weight, which sets how large N must be. The ratio at 3·10⁴ puts 90% cooperation near
  N ≈ 10⁶.
- *If 5 holds:* PrudentBot's theoretical advantage, excluding the shadow, is priced out by its length at
  feasible N, and FairBot does the work.
