# Review of `specs/2026-10-25-pricing-rules.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds**
- **Unstated/mismatched $K$ across games:** Rule A charges $c \cdot K$ for a nested timeout, and Prediction 3 assumes $c \cdot K \ge 1$ at $c = 10^{-4}$ (implying $K \ge 10^4$). However, PD certificate checks take $2\text{--}4 \cdot 10^4$ steps (requiring $K \ge 5 \cdot 10^4$ or $10^5$), whereas RPS and Inspection in `lt_tape` likely use a smaller cap (e.g., $K = 10^3$ or $10^4$). If $K$ differs across games, "one price level $c$ delivers every game's target" is confounded: under Rule A, the effective penalty $c \cdot K$ is only uniform across games if $K$ is uniform. **Fix:** Explicitly state $K$ for each game in the spec; if $K$ differs across games, either equalize $K$ or parameterize Rule A directly by the timeout penalty $P_{\text{fail}} = c \cdot K$ so the joint band is evaluated on an invariant scale.
- **Ambiguity in Rule A for non-nested steps and multiple timeouts:** Rule A states "steps of reads that finish cost nothing." What about root-level evaluation steps outside of any nested read, or steps *before* a nested read is called? What if a program executes two sequential capped reads that both time out—does it pay $2 c K$ or $c K$? **Fix:** Specify whether root steps are free under A, and whether $c \cdot K$ is charged *per timed-out simulator call* or *per encounter* (and what happens when a nested simulation's inner simulation times out, which the outer handles and finishes).
- **Rule B cache-key and endogenous production in PD:** In World K with S-nonself production, a carrier's held certificates change over its lifetime. If B's cache is keyed only on `(reader bare source, read bare source)`, a first encounter before certificate production (which might fail or take a slow path) locks in the cache, or makes later verification of newly produced certificates free without ever paying for the check. Conversely, if keyed on `(source, certificate)`, how does that map to RPS/Inspection? **Fix:** Specify whether the cache key includes the carried certificate/gene and whether failed/timed-out reads populate the cache under B and A+B.

2. **Predictions I think are wrong**
- **Prediction 4 (B does not rescue RPS at any $c$ where F does not):** Wrong. Under B, the cache is keyed on `(reader source, read source)`. Even if the private tape changes the *action* per encounter, the *sources* are identical on repeated encounters within an island. If B caches only the *cost* (making subsequent simulations of the same opponent source free on that island) while still executing the read against the fresh tape, then on an island of size $N=100$ run for thousands of encounters, simulators pay the simulation cost only once per opponent type, dividing the effective cost per encounter by $\sim 50\text{--}100$. Alternatively, if B caches the *output* of the read, simulators become blind to the fresh tape after encounter 1 and lose to `U` even at $c=0$! Either way, B's behavior in RPS is mischaracterized. (Clarify whether B caches *cost only* or *evaluation result*, and adjust Prediction 4 accordingly.)
- **Prediction 5 (A+B has a wider joint band than A):** Likely wrong (they will tie). Under A, finished reads already cost $0$, so amortizing finished reads (adding B to A) changes nothing for successful checks. B only changes A if *timeouts* are also cached (so mutual simulators in RPS only pay $c \cdot K$ on their first encounter per island). If timeouts are cached under A+B, A+B will actually *hurt* RPS by letting simulators amortize their mutual-timeout penalty!

3. **Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Log the actual step counts and timeout counts per game:** Since F and A can be computed from stored per-encounter step/timeout records for RPS and Inspection, generate the continuous response curve over $c \in [10^{-6}, 10^{-1}]$ for RPS and Inspection at zero extra simulation cost, rather than just 4 grid points.

4. **Alternative explanations**
- **Inspection game survival under A:** In the Inspection game, if the responsive inspector's read of the inspectee finishes within the cap, its cost under A is literally $0$ at *every* $c$. Thus, A meeting the Inspection target across a wide band of $c$ does not test robustness to nonzero verification cost; it just reflects that Inspection has no timeouts on the equilibrium path.
