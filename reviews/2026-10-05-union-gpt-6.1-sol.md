# Review of `specs/2026-10-05-union.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

### 1. Design flaws/confounds and fixes
- **`QUORUM` is not operationally defined outside monomorphic states.** Does it inspect the sampled opponent, a resident genotype, or population frequencies? “Resident” is ambiguous during fixation; a population-wide switch upon fixation invalidates ordinary pairwise-payoff fixation formulas. Define executable semantics for mixed populations and use the same semantics in both objects. Unit-test every intermediate mutant count before reusing the chain solver.
- **Fairness is not Pareto efficiency here.** With both workers working and no whacking, *every* wage yields total payoff 2 and is Pareto-efficient, including zero wages. Report efficiency separately from equal division; this experiment principally tests bargaining distribution, not selection toward efficiency.
- **The proof/source interface is underspecified.** Specify the formal proposition proved by `QUORUM`, how mutually conditional programs establish it, how boss conditionals resolve, and whether “contains a union clause” means syntax or certified behavior. Otherwise the oracle may supply coordination by fiat, while repression may merely target a syntactic tag.
- **Boss-only emigration is not an ablation of myopia.** It selects on realized spatial payoff differences, not necessarily longer-horizon consequences. Specify emigrant weighting, replacement, timing, and payoff averaging. Either rename this spatial selection or add an explicit payoff-horizon comparison.

### 2. Predictions likely wrong
- **Prediction 2’s neutral-entry argument is conditional, not established.** The second union’s neutrality depends on the missing mixed-population semantics and on absence of repression. Under repression it can be strictly disadvantaged; with resident-dependent activation, fixation may change everyone’s behavior discontinuously. I predict neutrality at zero wages in the encounter-based, no-repression subgame, but no justified N-independent fair share in the full grammar.
- **Prediction 6’s reason is false:** an unwhacked lone militant at zero wages earns exactly as much as a scab. Militants can enter neutrally without a quorum and induce wage increases. I predict some wage bargaining without `QUORUM`; intermediate-wage offers may prevent equal division.
- **Prediction 4’s direction is not robust.** At positive c, repression is costly, while a boss facing scabs earns most without repression. Migration could spread cheap exploitation rather than preemptive repression. I predict dependence on migration timing, worker composition, and payoff horizon—not a guaranteed 0.15 reduction.
- The numerical fair-share and first-passage bounds lack supporting rate calculations. Predict these only after accounting for mutation-prior mass and competing exits, not merely counting fixations.

### 3. Missing controls/cheap additions
- Compute complete payoff/fixation tables for scab, union, militant, and every constant boss policy first.
- Add a matched no-quorum grammar preserving mutation mass, plus a reduced canonical-strategy chain.
- Separate source targeting from strike targeting; test semantically equivalent union implementations.
- Make outcome bins disjoint or report their joint distribution. “Repression” currently overlaps fair, low-wage, and scab-split.
- Report support, transition structure, mixing diagnostics, and uncertainty. Specify whether dwell times use mutant proposals or generations.

### 4. Alternative explanations not ruled out
Grammar multiplicity and mutation priors could determine fair shares; oracle-supplied coordination could explain apparent union success; population-state discontinuities could manufacture drift races; transient seeding and slow mixing could explain island differences. Three seeds cannot distinguish these mechanisms.

### 5. Beyond this experiment
Separate **credible collective withdrawal** from **credible costly repression**. Here repression is a committed program policy, with no within-encounter incentive check. Testing which institution survives when enforcement itself must be incentive-compatible would connect source-level commitments to actual bargaining power.
