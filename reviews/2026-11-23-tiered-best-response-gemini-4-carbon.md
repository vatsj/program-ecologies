# Review of `specs/2026-11-23-tiered-best-response.md` by gemini-4-carbon

**1. Design flaws or confounds**

* **One-population symmetry in Arm P (`src/bridgeless_rivals.py`):** In modal PD, islands are typically a single well-mixed population, not fixed slots. Your definitions ("for each island and slot... one other slot's resident is replaced") assume multi-population/role-separated islands (like Arm T/M). In a one-population island, an invading variant $p'$ also plays *itself* at frequency $x$, so on-path equivalence requires $u(p', p') = u(p', p) = u(p, p)$, not just matching actions against other slots. Furthermore, migrants arrive in the *same* pool, not a "partner slot." **Fix:** Explicitly write separate definitions of $E(p; k)$ and migrant/prior payoff for one-population symmetric arms vs. multi-slot arms before running.
* **Source-reading breaks action-based "on-path equivalence":** You define $p' \in E(p; k)$ as playing the same action against native majorities and leaving native payoffs unchanged. For open-source readers, native partners inspect $p'$'s source code; changing $p \to p'$ off-path can flip a native reader's action *on-path* (e.g., breaking a `BOX` proof or syntactic check). Your clause "leaves every slot's payoff there unchanged" implicitly catches this if evaluated via the full game table, **provided** you also check $p'$ against *itself* in one-pop games and verify native-vs-$p'$ payoffs, not just $p'$'s action. State the exact table algebraic condition.
* **Weighting by $m_q$ vs. $m_q T_q$ in Regret:** P10's selection gradient $\sigma \propto \sum_q m_q T_q \Delta_q$ weights testers by arrival rate *times lineage lifetime* $T_q$ on island $k$. Your "migrant payoff" weights migrants solely by island counts ($m_q$), implicitly assuming all migrants have equal residence time $T_q$. In Arm P, a defector or incompatible rival migrant dies in $O(1)$ steps ($T_q \approx 1$), whereas a neutral bridge migrant drifts for $O(\ln N)$ steps! Measuring unweighted $m_q$ regret tests a mis-specified target and may falsely show positive regret where $\sigma$-weighted regret is zero. **Fix:** Compute both raw arrival-weighted regret (testing the RS's literal words) and $T_q$-weighted regret (testing P10's mechanism, using single-mutant mean sojourn time against island $k$'s native profile).
* **Majority share threshold:** If an island is mid-transition or polymorphic (majority share 35%, especially at $mN=10$), defining $E(p; k)$ and native best-response against the plurality program alone misclassifies Tier 1 selection as Tier 2. **Fix:** Restrict primary regret shares to islands where native majority share in every slot is $\ge 0.8$ (report excluded polymorphic islands separately), and explicitly report the fraction of residents failing Tier 1 (native best-response) itself!

**2. Predictions I think are wrong**

* **RE 5 (Dose up to $mN = 10$):** Likely **falsified** (either vacuously or by reversal). At $mN = 10$, islands are swamped well above the patchwork boundary ($x = mN \cdot T_{\text{nuc}}/N \lesssim 0.3$ from Queue 2/8), either collapsing diversity completely (leaving 0 diverse worlds in Arm T/M and possibly breaking Arm P's patchwork) or turning islands into mixed migrant soups where Tier 1 ("native monomorphic profile") breaks down and raw regret rises. **My prediction:** Diversity collapses at $mN=10$ in T/M; if Arm P stays diverse at $mN=10$, majority shares drop and measured monomorphic-equivalent regret plateaus or worsens relative to $mN=1$ or $mN=3$. Add $mN=3$ to catch the non-monotone interior.
* **RE 7 ($\Lambda = 1,000$ in consensus worlds):** In an annealed tail where mutation decays over the run, most of $\Lambda$ arrives early during or just after the scramble, not as isolated late testers on a settled island, **and** at $N=200$, neutral-on-path variants still need $\sim N$ generations to fix once seeded. Unless the horizon is very long and the tail is flat/late, $\Lambda=1,000$ could still show near-zero effect *or*, conversely, in Arm P where PrudentBot beats FairBot against tail suckers by $\Delta > 0$ (Queue 47 showed A=156 raised prudent share +0.11–0.19), exceed your $<0.10$ bound. Check the exact annealing schedule of Queue 74 before locking RE 7's numbers.

**3. Missing controls or cheap additions**

* **Tier-1 (Native) Regret:** Measure whether the resident actually best-responds to its *own* island's majority profile first! Lexicographic order fails at step 1 if drift/hitchhiking leaves suboptimal natives at $mN=10$ or small $N$.
* **Founder-weighted supply control:** Your supply control draws *uniformly* from $E(p; k) \cap \text{Founders}$, ignoring that some founders had 500 copies across 64 islands and others had 1. Weight the supply control by actual founder counts in that run (or seed prior restricted to founders); uniform over unique founders severely underweights abundant shadows.
* **Stage 0b parameter clarity:** Specify what happens when a tester fixes in the partner slot in 0b! If the tester is advantageous against the resident (or neutral and drifts to fixation with prob $1/N$), the "resident background" is destroyed. Either condition/reset on tester extinction or restrict 0b to testers that are deleterious/neutral against natives, and state which.

**4. Alternative explanations the brief does not rule out**

* **Differential island survival / colonization (differential emigration of winners), not P10 within-island replacement:** In diverse worlds, programs with zero migrant regret (like PrudentBot or bully-resistant readers) are *also* harder for rival migrants to invade and overthrow, or better at colonizing other islands! Thus, residents may exhibit low migrant regret because low-regret profiles *survive longer as island majorities*, even if no $p \to p'$ Tier-2 replacement within $E(p;k)$ ever occurred on an island. **Rule it out cheaply:** In Stage 1/2, log island lineage histories (was the end-state resident present on that island at $t=25\%$, or did an on-path equivalent $p' \in E(p;k)$ actually replace $p$ on the *same* island?).
