# Review of `specs/2026-10-17-legitimate-detectors.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

## 1. Design flaws/confounds and fixes

- **The grudge mechanism is not yet a defined realizable intervention.** Observing X defect does not prove “I defect against X”; conditional self-reference alone supplies no derivation. The existing checker’s soundness cannot automatically transfer to a new self-hypothesis rule. **Fix:** make an executed, sound derivation a go/no-go prerequisite. Bind certificates to the exact program, inherited state, opponent identity, and evaluation budget. Test adversarial non-readers and negated readers, not just detectors.

- **Inheritance and identity determine whether retaliation works.** Does one victim’s grudge protect only its descendants, every same-source copy, or the entire network? Does appending a gene change the source that opponents recognize? Population-wide propagation could covertly supply the coordination being tested. **Fix:** specify the state/source distinction and transmission rules; default to lineage-local inheritance and log protected versus unprotected detector mass.

- **The cause labels do not identify causes.** A predator reaching 0.5 is neither necessary nor sufficient for causing replacement. Inefficiency, third-party exploitation, and demographic advantage can coexist. “None of the above” does not establish abundance. **Fix:** retain descriptive event labels, allow overlap and “unresolved,” and use matched counterfactual replays around removals: suppress the suspected exploitative payoff edge or third-party prey while holding arrivals fixed.

- **The large-population interpretation is unsupported.** A tests two sizes, while B/C mainly test fixed geometries; lineage memory, recurrent arrivals, and merge timing can change with size. **Fix:** frame conclusions as finite-size mechanism tests; add a cheap invasion/retaliation scaling diagnostic before making asymptotic claims.

## 2. Predictions likely wrong

- **Prediction 1 conflates retaliation with unexplo itability.** A surviving predator can exploit unmarked lineages; source variants can evade exact-source grudges. Even perfect retaliation leaves first-strike gains. I predict reduced establishment against repeated identical sources, but protection dependent on inheritance coverage, encounter rate, and source diversity—not a justified universal ≤0.05 bound.

- **Prediction 2 uses the wrong definition of “false.”** Within-network defection can be a legitimate response to incompatibility, budget failure, or an earlier grudge. Conversely, an across-network grudge can be unjustified. Predict zero only after proving within-network cooperation invariant under every reachable gene state. Score false triggers against actual encounter traces separately from within-network grudges.

- **Predictions 3–4 overstate symmetry and causal attribution.** Equal initial mass does not equal equal establishment, verification costs, bridges, or cross-play. Tail predators removing detectors would not make PrudentBot itself a predator. I predict path-dependent advantages; a coin flip requires a demonstrated symmetry or neutral matched control.

## 3. Missing controls/cheap additions

- Sham gene writing with identical costs and inheritance but disabled checking; paired random seeds.
- Exact-source versus fresh synonymous-predator arrivals; measure repeat-encounter frequency and grudge coverage.
- Prewritten valid grudges as an upper-bound control, separating certificate efficacy from production/transmission failure.
- Preregister establishment threshold, censoring horizon, assay replication count, and binomial intervals. Twenty runs cannot sharply establish near-coin-flip probabilities.
- Report support and transition structure, including unresolved coexistence, not only winners.

## 4. Alternative explanations not ruled out

Detector survival could reflect predator exhaustion, finite observation windows, memory-induced source fragmentation, neutral demographic shielding, or collateral benefits from other certificate-reading families—not evolved resistance.
