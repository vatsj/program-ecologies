# Review of `specs/2026-10-05-k-at-n8.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

### 1. Design flaws/confounds and fixes

- **Analytic cut is not automatically lemma sharing.** In tree proofs, cut can shorten derivations while still duplicating lemma proofs; DAG sharing changes the cost object. Define whether budgets count tree sequents, distinct DAG nodes, certificate-checking work, or search work. Keep analytic-cut and DAG-sharing results separate. Finite cut-formula availability alone does not certify minimum-length search: specify finite sequent states, cycle handling, and the lower-bound/completeness argument.

- **The amortized price assumes the amortization it should test.** Charging \(cb/N\) presumes one verification serves \(N\) encounters. Source-reading requires opponent-specific verification, and cache misses depend on distinct sources, turnover, and cache ownership. Label this an imposed pricing schedule unless you measure verification events and cache lifetime. Add a simple opponent-keyed cache accounting control.

- **Global-budget closure is not closure under budget evolution.** A family may have no leak within a fixed-\(b\) catalogue yet leak to a cheaper-budget mutant. Run the leak test on the cross-budget catalogue too. Define whether “copy” means identical source, identical budgeted program, or extensionally identical behavior; these produce different subsidies.

- **Finite-\(N\) agreement cannot establish the large-population result.** Three sizes and a plateau through \(b=48\) establish neither asymptotic efficiency nor prover saturation. Report “unchanged over tested budgets,” plus unresolved GL atoms and recurrent-class/exit-rate scaling. Do not infer completeness from a plateau.

### 2. Predictions likely wrong

- **Prediction 5’s amortized exit is not neutral.** If ALLC is uncharged while the incumbent pays \(cb/N\), the exit is strictly advantageous at every finite \(N\). Moreover, \(N\Delta\) remains order \(cb\), so fixation can retain a nonvanishing multiplicative bias. My prediction: potentially high cooperation, but a **weakly selected**, not neutral, ALLC exit.

- **Prediction 4 conflates two mechanisms.** Analytic cut need not deliver the savings of reusable DAG certificates, and a quadratic coefficient’s interval containing zero is not evidence of linear scaling over a short, dependent sample. My prediction: heterogeneous savings by obligation structure; no justified universal linearity or sibling-ratio threshold.

- **Predictions 1–2 are too confidently support-based.** Programs outside stationary support can control entry, escape, and bottlenecks. Removing prudent programs can therefore matter despite their negligible free-arm mass. My prediction: preserving FairBot’s core is plausible; the numerical closeness bounds and “beats free” do not follow from that fact.
