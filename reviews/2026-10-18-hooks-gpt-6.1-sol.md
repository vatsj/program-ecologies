# Review of `specs/2026-10-18-hooks.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

## 1. Design flaws and fixes

- **The evolving object is underspecified.** List lengths, tag allocation, key domains, write triggers, update timing, inheritance of memories, and expiry are undefined. “Uniform over expressible fills” need not exist for source/history keys. These choices determine founder abundance and learning dynamics. **Fix:** specify a finite sampling procedure or normalized generative prior, list limits, and encounter/write/inheritance semantics before predictions.

- **Hook lookup may be circular; certificate validity may change after filling.** Behaviour keys include play against self and probes that can themselves invoke hooks. Source-key identity is ambiguous when hereditary fills change but “bare source” does not. Checking a fill alone does not establish soundness of the mutable composite program. **Fix:** define probe worlds, recursion/fuel handling, and identity under updates; demonstrate compositional validity or recheck affected certificates after every write. Test adversarial interacting fills.

- **Ablations confound mechanism with founder abundance and representation.** Adding grammar nodes and renormalizing priors changes the supply of establishers, shadows, and rivals; removing a scope may redistribute that supply. Equal hook-node cost does not equalize key storage, scripts, or lookup computation. **Fix:** retain identical base-program founders and coupled fill draws; disable a scope without reallocating its mass. Report both this intervention and any renormalized-prior comparison, plus total representation and execution costs.

- **The stated design cannot attribute wing differences to source access or support a large-population conclusion.** Wings differ in grammar, prover/checker, population size, production, and social-game execution model; removing source/root access does not match these. **Fix:** use a shared executable subgrammar at identical parameters for the causal contrast. Add a small paired population-size sweep, or explicitly restrict conclusions to finite-size lotteries.

## 2. Predictions likely wrong

- **Prediction 6:** removing source scopes should not generally align the wings within 0.1. Verification, production coverage, and base-language differences remain. I predict residual differences unless those are matched.

- **Prediction 2:** history need not increase gentle-holder share by 0.1. Grudges require repeat encounters after a surviving victim learns; high-priority behaviour/source fills may mask them. I predict benefits conditional on encounter recurrence, successful writing, and execution—not a universal marginal effect.

- **Prediction 1:** behaviour fills being most-held is not evidence of commitment. Broad keys can confer more applicability, while inactive copies hitchhike. I predict held-share rankings will be sensitive to the prior and list-retention rules; efficiency preservation is uncertain.

## 3. Missing controls or cheap additions

- Add **inert hooks/fills** with identical prior and storage overhead; separately measure held, applicable, selected, and executed fills.
- Separate **history disabled**, **writes shuffled across partners**, and **history learned but not inherited**. This distinguishes reciprocity from inherited identity labels.
- Log certificate rejection, key coverage, founder establishment, and transitions between networks; report persistence beyond the fixed horizon.
- Define all denominators and paired uncertainty intervals. Twenty runs poorly resolve 0.1 differences. Align success criteria and falsifiers: currently several leave broad unclassified gaps.
- Preserve at least one fill-source control before dropping all of them; otherwise seeded commitment and endogenous acquisition remain inseparable.

Apparent cooperation could reflect extra cooperative founder mass rather than commitment; source-associated islands could be certificate-coverage components rather than identity discrimination; rare threat execution could mean no relevant challenges rather than effective deterrence; fission–fusion success could reflect merge-time abundance rather than robust compatibility.
