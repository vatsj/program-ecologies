# Review of `predictions/2026-10-02-ergodic-islands.md` by fable

(Saved from the fable subagent's report, verbatim.)

## 0. Checks you asked for

- **Numerics.** `log_gr` matches (1 − 1/r)/(1 − r^−I) for lr ∈ {−3, −0.5, ±10⁻⁶, 0, 0.5, 3} and I ∈ {1, 4, 64}, including the r < 1 branch through `_logexpm1`. `log_fixation` matches `chain.fixation` to 4 digits on all four key games at N = 10, 100, 1000. `gth` matches a direct linear solve on a random rate matrix with entries spanning e^±10. `classify` is correct for the three named categories (see 1(f) for what it misses). Reduction table: I reproduce every cell I checked, (10,1) 0.0010, (10,4096) 0.128, (25,64) 0.081, (100,1) 0.0077, (100,64) 0.062, (400,16) 0.033, (1000,4096) 0.024. o(N) for the modal arm: 0.151 / 0.204 / 0.366 / 0.527, as stated. The faker sum is 3.8·10⁻⁴ at (N = 10, I = 1), not 3.3·10⁻⁴; the brief's N = 10 number is the large-I value.
- **Φ₂ and the island walk.** Correct for this migration rule on a *regular* graph: a death on island i picks a parent on neighbour j with probability m/d, so a discordant edge (i = a, j = q) flips i at rate Nm·ρ_N(q|a)/d and j at rate Nm·ρ_N(a|q)/d, and the number of q islands is a birth–death chain with constant ratio r. On an irregular graph the per-edge ratio is (ρ(q|a)/d_i)/(ρ(a|q)/d_j), so regularity is load-bearing; say so. The migrant does arrive as one copy and the within-island process is the uniform-death, fitness-birth Moran whose up/down ratio at count k is f_q(k)/f_a(k), so `chain.fixation` is the right ρ_N. The neutral value 1/(IN) is exact at *every* m on a regular graph (the replacement matrix is symmetric, hence isothermal), so the shadow check is a code check, as the brief says. The "m → 0" label understates the formula's domain: for the entry edge what matters is immigrant pressure on the lineage (`rho_dilute`), and at mN = 0.1 that is 0.99× ρ_N even though about ten immigrants arrive during the lineage's drift phase.
- **The faker is structure-invariant for a sharper reason than branching.** This PD has T + S = R + P, so every faker-vs-R subgame is additive: the payoff gap is exactly (T − R)(N + 1)/(N − 1) at every within-island count for `THEM(^D)` and half that for `THEM(^X)`. That is constant selection, not frequency-dependent selection. And on a regular island graph each island is the source of exactly N births per generation at any m (N(1 − m) + d·N·m/d), so every faker individual has birth rate r and death rate 1 wherever it sits. Φ(faker) = 1 − 1/r + O(r^−N) for every m, I, graph, with r = e^{w(T−R)(N+1)/(N−1)}: 0.264 at N = 100, 0.278 at 25, 0.315 at 10, exactly the table. Disagree with astra on this point: local saturation and replacement competition do not enter, because there is no frequency at which the faker's advantage changes. For a general PD the gap x(P − S) + (1 − x)(T − R) is positive at every x, so the branching constant still holds at large N; constancy in N is special to T + S = R + P.
- **Order of limits.** ε → 0 at fixed (I, N, mN) is physical here: no pair of weak-arm classes with μ > 10⁻⁴ is bistable at N = 100 (the smallest max(ρ, ρᵀ) is exactly 1/N), so the slowest island-level flip is neutral and ε ≪ m/N suffices. State that instead of the two justifications given; it answers astra's "eventual absorption" point. Fixing mN is the right scaling. "Exit split depends only on M = IN" is right for N ≥ 25 given the two facts above, and it is right at every m, not only in the rare-migration limit.
- **`_meta_fix` vs `islands._run` (K = 2).** Same process: parent drawn by count·exp(w·mean payoff over the other N − 1) on the source island with the victim still alive, victim uniform on the target, no change when victim and child agree, m applied per death with the source a uniform neighbour. Running to global absorption is right for the entry edge because r = ρ(R|D)/ρ(D|R) is 10⁶ at N = 100, so an established R island never loses. One caveat: the seed `1000003·seed + 7919·b + 31·I + N` does not depend on game, graph or mN, so complete and hypercube at the same (I, N, mN) use common random numbers. Fine for comparing them, but the Wilson-overlap test in verdict 7 assumes independent samples.
- **ABM anchors.** See 1(c), 1(d), 2.
- **REJECTED.** See 5.

## 1. Design flaws and fixes

**(a) The Monte Carlo stopping rule cannot resolve the verdict bands.** At 40 successes the Wilson half-width is 26–31% of Φ for every edge (entry N = 100: [0.031, 0.057] around 0.042; faker-D: [0.201, 0.341] around 0.264). Verdicts 5 and 6 ask for ±10%. With ~24 faker cells and ~16 entry cells the chance that every point estimate lands inside ±10% is about 0.5⁴⁰. Worse, the falsifier of the brief's central claim (faker-D below 0.18 or above 0.35) fires by noise with probability ≈ 1.6% per cell at n ≈ 150, about 30% over the faker cells. Fix: `min_succ` = 400 for the faker edges (failures are cheap and a success is one strict sweep; a few minutes per cell), ≥ 150–200 for entry, and write the verdicts as "the Wilson interval contains the prediction in ≥ 90% of cells", not point-in-band. Report cells that stopped on the time budget or trial cap separately.

**(b) Verdict 4 is wrong as written, and the fix is more interesting than the claim.** The modal n = 6 cooperative family is not faker-free. `BOX1(THEM(THEM))` is strictly invaded by `not(BOX(THEM(ME)))` and `not(BOX(THEM(THEM)))` (μ 1.2·10⁻³ each); `BOX(THEM(^C))` and `BOX1(THEM(^C))` are third-party probes faked by `BOX(THEM(^D))` and `BOX1(THEM(^D))`, exactly as `THEM(^C)` is by `THEM(^D)`. Their strict exit rates at N = 100 are 7.0·10⁻⁴ and 7.8·10⁻⁴ per mutation event, I-independent. At I = 1, N = 100 they hold 0.050 of the 0.169 cooperative mass. On islands their mass saturates while the three unfakeable provers grow ∝ I. The chain gives odds = I·o_u(N) + o_f(I, N) with o_u(100) = 0.133 (constant in I to three digits), o_u(1000) = 0.430, and o_f saturating at 0.47 (N = 100) and 0.16 (N = 1000). The ratio of the chain's odds to the brief's I·o(N) is 0.88 / 0.76 / 0.68 / 0.66 / 0.65 at I = 4 / 16 / 64 / 256 / 1024, so the ±15% bullet fails from I = 16. P(C,C) at N = 100 is 0.418 / 0.712 / 0.899 / 0.972 / 0.993 against the brief's 0.45 / 0.77 / 0.93 / 0.98 / 0.995; the I = 256 falsifier (below 0.9) does not fire; the log-odds slope over I ∈ [16, 1024] is 0.96 and holds. The third bullet, "no faker exits", is false, and the code would not show it: `faker_share` is computed relative to FairBot alone. Fix: restate verdict 4 as the two-term law, report exits from the whole cooperative block split neutral/strict, and add the prediction that is actually new: π(fakeable provers)/π(unfakeable provers) falls like 1/I, from 0.45 at I = 1 to 0.002 at I = 1024 (N = 100). Islands select for unfakeability inside the family.

**(c) The finite-ε comparison in "What it would mean" mixes two statistics.** "About 0.13 against 0.06 at I = 64, N = 100" compares the ABM's P(C,C) with the chain's π_R. In the anchor run (`multilevel`, w_g = 0) `THEM(^C)` was dominant on 0.03 of island-time, fakers on 0.17 (self-play PCC ≤ 0.25), D on 0.71. R-dominant islands plus faker self-play account for at most ~0.07 of the 0.129; the rest sits in islands with no dominant class, i.e. polymorphic islands, which the monomorphic chain cannot represent. Astra gave this as a one-line alternative; the anchor's own numbers make it the main account. Fix: decompose ABM P(C,C) by island dominant class (R / C / faker / none) from `dom_time` and `isl_cc_hist`, which are already recorded, and compare R-dominant island-time to π_R. Verdict 10 should predict that decomposition. On the comparable statistic the finite-ε regime is likely *below* the chain (0.03 against 0.06), the opposite of the brief's reading.

**(d) Verdict 11's statistic is dominance flips sampled every 20 generations, not per-mutant exits.** A consistency check against the anchor: 11,209 pooled exits over an R-dominant island-time of ~0.03 gives a mean R dwell of roughly 400 generations; immigrant fakers alone, mN × 0.17 faker-island share × ρ ≈ 0.14, would give a dwell near 50. So the dominance statistic and the chain's exit split are not the same object by an order of magnitude. Before interpreting the faker : shadow ratio, count exits only from islands that were ≥ 90% R and record whether the invader was mutation-born or an immigrant.

**(e) Hypercube vs complete at finite ε is the one place graph can matter, and the brief predicts it will not.** Faker islands are born from R islands; on a hypercube their exports fall on 6–8 neighbours, which are the R cluster they came from, rather than on 63–255 islands. "Within 0.05 of complete" is a real prediction, not a safe one; I would give it about even odds.

**(f) `classify` files weak fakers under "other".** A class with U[q, R] = U[R, R] and U[R, q] < U[R, R] enters R neutrally and then exploits it at every positive frequency. Numerically the "other" exit sum is negligible (7.9·10⁻⁷ at (100, 64)), so f is unaffected. But `and(X,THEM(^D))` (μ 1.2·10⁻⁵, U[q,R] = 0, U[R,q] = −1.5, D-neutral) is such a class, and its state is a sink: fed only from R, left only by drift ∝ 1/(IN), so its mass grows ∝ I: 0.0029 / 0.0078 / 0.0114 at I = 4096 for N = 100 / 25 / 10. Verdict 3's "nothing else above 0.01" fails at (10, 4096). Add a weak-faker category and state the sink.

**(g) The plateau is not monotone from N = 10.** The reduction at I = 4096 gives p* = 0.054 / 0.100 / 0.111 / 0.123 / 0.128 / 0.128 / 0.117 at N = 3 / 5 / 6 / 8 / 10 / 16 / 25: a maximum near N = 10–16, falling below because ρ_N(D|R) grows and "other" exits return. "At most about 0.13" stands; say where the maximum is, and add N = 5 and 16 to the static grid, which is free.

**(h) Cells stopped on the 25-minute budget.** The I = 256, N = 100, mN = 0.1 entry cell had 3 successes in 80 trials; 40 successes is ~1,000 trials of thousands of generations each. Several cells may stop on time with wide intervals and still be scored. Report the stopping reason per cell.

- **Verdict 10 at I = 16:** at risk on the low side. The multilevel scaling run had I = 16 about 0.04 below I = 64 with large replicate spread; with R on 0.03 of island-time, 16 islands hold half an R island on average.
- **Verdict 11, second bullet:** agree with astra that the direction is contingent; the reason is that both supplies are first order in ε (faker stock in D islands ∝ ε, exported at mN; ALLC mutation ∝ ε), so the ratio has no first-order ε dependence.
- **Verdict 12:** below even odds. At N = 25, ρ_25(D|R) = 2.6·10⁻³ against 1.7·10⁻⁸ at N = 100, so D immigrants take R islands at mN × 0.7 × 2.6·10⁻³ ≈ 1.8·10⁻³ per island-generation, comparable to the mutation-borne faker exit. The two-level chain suppresses this through the island-level walk, which assumes R islands can form a majority; at finite ε they are a 3% minority among 70% D islands and the walk argument does not apply. Add the 0.54× entry dilution and N = 25 can come out lower.
- **Verdict 13:** fine. At εN = 0.01 the D-island phase is ~4% of island-time (ALLC drift at μ_C εN/N per island, then D at 0.0047 per generation × 0.26, then recolonization at mN × 0.9 × 0.042), so ≈ 0.96. At I = 16, εN = 0.01 the first FairBot mutant arrives about every 1,250 generations and fixes with 0.042, so the approach from all-D takes ~3·10⁴ generations: first half depressed, second half fine.

## 4. Alternative explanations

- **The modal island gain is selection for unfakeability within the family, not a property of provability.** The ∝ I law holds for the three provers with no strict invader at n = 6. At n ≥ 8 `BOX(THEM(THEM))` acquires strict invaders (RESULTS, "Modal arm"), so on islands its share of the family should fall ∝ 1/I. A grammar whose provers are mostly fakeable would show little island gain.
- **The finite-ε cooperation is polymorphic-island cooperation**, not monomorphic R islands with faster entry (1(c)). This is the mechanism the chain cannot represent, and it is the one that makes the ABM regime worth running.
- **"Islands raise cooperation up to 10×" is the well-mixed chain at size N with the shadow exit deleted:** p*(N) = μ_R ρ_N(R|D)/f. Stating it that way makes clear that nothing about islands acts on entry beyond choosing N.

## 5. Beyond this experiment

- **Put the faker invariance in THEORY as a proposition, with its scope.** For this PD (T + S = R + P), on a regular island graph with this update rule, Φ(faker) = 1 − e^{−w(T−R)(N+1)/(N−1)} + O(r^−N) for every m, I and graph. The levers against it are then explicit: the prior ratio μ_R/Σμ_F, the temptation T − R, unfakeability, or between-island selection (w_g), which changes the per-individual birth rate and is why the multilevel rescue works. Non-regular graphs weight source rates by degree and are outside the statement.
- **The plateau bound.** Even with ρ_enter = 1, π_R ≤ μ_R/(μ_R + f) = 0.64 within the reduction. The weak arm's ceiling on any island family is set by two prior masses and one exponential; that is the number to quote against "structure rescues the dilemma".
- **REJECTED entries the brief should flag.** (i) 2026-09-18 "Fakers as the spatial/island spoiler, refuted on the lattice; carry into island predictions" and its 2026-09-23 reversal at ε = 0: this experiment adjudicates between them under mutation, and the brief's §9.5 (i) framing should say so. The 09-18 entry also says "do not re-propose faker-resistance as the fix", which THEORY §9.2 has since re-opened; cite the chain of re-openings. (ii) "Standing variance (finite εN) as the rescue, closed": verdicts 10–12 and the "distinct regime above the ε→0 value" reading re-open it with island structure, and 1(c) suggests it will not survive on the comparable statistic. (iii) "Plain islands with mutation stay below 0.1" (09-30) and "the multilevel threshold is flat in island count" (the I = 16 anomaly) bear on verdict 10. The brief flags Fudenberg–Imhof and the migration game correctly.
- **Budget.** Experiment A is ~50 seconds of compute and is nearly determined by two facts that are exact or near-exact; B's faker cells test code; the one open number in B is entry at fixed mN, and it is under-powered. The ABM is where the mechanism not in the chain lives. I would shift time from B's faker cells and A's large-I cells to more entry successes and to the ABM decomposition.
