# Review of `specs/2026-11-07-majority-current-object.md` by gemini-4-argon

**1. Design flaws or confounds**
- **V1 validation horizon mismatch:** Queue 64 ran for $10,000$ generations ($100N$ at $N=100$). This spec runs to $120N$ ($12,000$ generations at $N=100$). V1 asks to reproduce queue 64 at $10,000$ generations, which corresponds to the $100N$ snapshot, not the primary $120N$ endpoint. *Fix:* Explicitly specify that V1 checks the $100N$ snapshot of the $\Lambda=0, N=100$ cell against queue 64's endpoint, and ensure the RNG stream for births/migrations is strictly divorced from the Poisson arrival RNG stream so $\Lambda=0$ is draw-for-draw identical up to $100N$.
- **RE 7 statistical power and independence:** RE 7 pools fair-consensus runs across $\Lambda \in \{0, 10, 100\}$ at $mN=1$ to get $\ge 20$ runs for a $\chi^2$ uniformity test. Because founders are shared across $\Lambda$ via CRN, runs with the same index across $\Lambda$ are heavily correlated—if founders pick the winning fair pair early, pooling violates independence and inflates false positives (or masks a lack of independent trials at $N=400$, which only has 20 base seeds). *Fix:* Test uniformity on $\Lambda=100$ alone (or whatever single $\Lambda$ is primary), and if pooling is used for count reporting, compute the $p$-value via permutation/bootstrap respecting the CRN seed blocks.
- **Debounce window scaling (Measurement 4):** The transition debounce is fixed at 25 generations across $N \in \{100, 200, 400\}$. Because Moran drift and fixation times scale with $N$, a 25-generation debounce filters different dynamical regimes at $N=100$ vs $N=400$. *Fix:* Scale the debounce window as $0.25N$ generations (or report both fixed 25 and $0.25N$).

**2. Predictions I think are wrong**
- **RE 8 (D1 clean tail at $N=100, mN=1, \Lambda=100$ raises M's $V^*$ by $\ge 0.15$):** I predict this will fail ($< 0.10$ gain, and specifically fail at $N=200$). In queue 71, the clean tail's critical dose was $\Lambda^* \approx 35\text{--}55$ in the $I \to \infty$ rare-migration ODE. Here, at $mN=1$ and finite $I=64$, inter-island migration delivers 1 migrant per generation per island, consolidating the founding unequal majority across the metapopulation by $g \approx 10N\text{--}20N$. Because the annealed tail decays as $(1 + (g-2N)/(N/2))^{-1}$, over half of the $\Lambda=100$ arrivals occur *after* $g = 10N$, when migration has already locked the metapopulation and a single clean arrival in one slot is swamped by $mN=1$ immigrant re-supply of the unequal convention.

**3. Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Add $N=200$ to RE 8 / D1 scoring:** D1 is already being run at $N=100$ and $N=200$, and queue 31 showed the DSL's fair-to-unequal phase transition happens between $N=150$ and $N=200$. At $N=100$, M's $\Lambda=0, mN=1$ baseline is already $0.70$ (leaving little headroom and high variance). Scoring D1 at $N=200$ (where M's baseline collapses to $U$) is a much sharper test of whether the clean tail can flip the unequal basin at finite $N, I$.
- **Track arrival timing of successful establishments (Measurement 6):** Split Measurement 6's establishment rate into early ($g \in [2N, 15N]$) vs. late ($g \in (15N, 100N]$) arrivals. This costs zero extra compute and directly tests whether late tail arrivals are inert against $mN=1$ migration.

**4. Alternative explanations not ruled out**
- **M vs. T is confounded by seed-law support and dose, not just evaluability/Trojan bullies:** Arm T is a 27-program hand-picked library with 9% fair conditional readers and no Trojan bullies; Arm M is a 1,305-class length-prior DSL where Trojan bullies exist and reader mass differs. If T succeeds and M fails, D1 rules out the tail's re-supply of bullies/victims in M, *not* their presence in M's founding seed. Without a diagnostic seeding M without bullies/⅓-constants at founding, you cannot distinguish "the tail failed to flip M's founding basin" from "finite-$I$ dynamics under M's grammar cannot sustain $V^*$."
