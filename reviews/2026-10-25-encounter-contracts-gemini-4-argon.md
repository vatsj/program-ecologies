# Review of `specs/2026-10-25-encounter-contracts.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds**
- **Stage 2 semantics in the weak (simulation) arm is underspecified and risks circularity.** In Stage 2, "every non-carrier re-evaluates its program against the others' stage-2 actions (the responders see the contract)." In the simulation DSL, a program does not take opponent *actions* as input; it simulates opponent *programs*. If the responder simply re-runs its simulator on the bare Stage-1 programs, its action will not change unless the simulator is given the updated `(program, contract)` objects—which would trigger recursive Stage-2 evaluations inside the simulator. **Fix:** Specify the exact Stage-2 evaluation semantics before running. Either (a) responders evaluate their conditional branches by substituting the carriers' Stage-2 fills for the results of `SIM(...)` atoms (a behavioral override), or (b) responders simulate the full `(program, contract)` pair with a recursion depth cap of 1.
- **Genesis baseline mismatch ($p$ vs. Stage 2).** Genesis keys the new contract to $p$ (the Stage-1 bare-program action profile), draws new actions that differ from $S$'s *Stage-2* actions, and compares payoffs to "before" (presumably the current Stage-2 outcome). If the encounter triple already carries firing contracts for key $p$, $S$ overwrites its own active fill for $p$; furthermore, if a *complement* member ($i \notin S$) carries a contract for $p$, does it play its contract fill or re-evaluate its bare program as a responder during Genesis and Stage 2? **Fix:** Explicitly state that "non-carrier" in Stage 2 means "an individual without a firing contract for key $p$" (or "not in $S$" during Genesis), and clarify how existing contracts on $p$ Held by $S^c$ behave when $S$ mutates its contract on $p$.
- **Jump-chain relaxation vs. ongoing $\nu > 0$ genesis.** Under the rare-migration jump chain, islands are relaxed to their attractors between migrants. If $\nu > 0$ runs continuously during relaxation, neutral or off-path contracts can drift and prevent strict absorption. **Fix:** Specify the stopping criterion for island relaxation when $\nu > 0$ (e.g., fixation of on-path behavior for $T$ generations, or turning off $\nu$ after a fixed post-migrant window).

2. **Predictions I think are wrong**
- **Prediction 4 ($\alpha > 0$ with an interval excluding 0 on fair islands).** I predict $\alpha$'s interval will include 0 (or be $< 10^{-3}$). On a resident $(1,2)$ fair island, a single migrant from $(2,3)$ has abundance $1/N$. Even if an encounter between the migrant and resident slot 3 forms a contract for the third pair $(1,3)$, that contract requires a coordinated change between slots 1 and 3. As your own Prediction 3 notes, a contract installed on *one* individual in slot 1 and *one* in slot 3 only fires when those two specific individuals meet (probability $1/N^2$), giving each a negligible $\sim 1/N$ fitness advantage that almost certainly dies by drift before fixing in both slots.

3. **Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Lineage/Slot-broadcast control (cheap addition):** Because of the $1/N$ coordination barrier on $|S| \ge 2$ contracts (Prediction 3), add a diagnostic arm (or sub-test on dominated islands) where a successful genesis installs the contract on $k$ random copies in each signing slot (or the whole slot, i.e., encounter-tested but slot-installed). This cleanly separates whether failures to clear dominated islands come from the *responder test* or from *single-individual installation dilution*.

4. **Alternative explanations not ruled out**
- **Silent-contract hitchhiking:** A contract formed during a transient migrant encounter goes silent once the migrant dies, sitting in the "reservoir." If it drifts to high frequency in both partner slots *while silent*, a later migrant presenting key $p$ will trigger a synchronized slot-wide response. If $\alpha > 0$ or delayed conversions occur, track whether the firing contracts were newly minted or reservoir relics; otherwise, reservoir drift will be misread as direct single-encounter invasion.
