# Review of `predictions/2026-10-01-cert-pricing.md` by gpt-6-astra

### 1. Design flaws/confounds and fixes

- **The certificate rule is an oracle, not yet an implementable certificate market.** `hc/hd` establish semantic decidability in your evaluator; they do not supply proofs, bound proof length, or specify what happens when a provable certificate is withheld. Also, completeness on a particular linear GL chain does not automatically establish the claimed equivalence with arithmetic provability for each interpreted program. **Fix:** state the exact correspondence and assumptions; otherwise describe this as *semantic certificate eligibility with free provision*. Keep implementation claims separate.
- **Connected components do not measure universality.** One low-mass bridge can merge mutually hostile families; the π ≥ 10⁻³ cutoff can instead split them. FairBot membership certifies neither pairwise compatibility nor universality outside the enumerated language. **Fix:** make threshold-free X primary, report excluded cooperative mass and cutoff sensitivity, and call the result “compatibility within the sampled language.”
- **Large-population efficiency is not identified by this grid.** Four N values at fixed n cannot establish convergence to one or a persistent power law. The object is also the ordered limit ε→0 before N→∞, not general large-population evolution. **Fix:** restrict conclusions accordingly; report local log-slopes and add a larger-N point if cheap. Evaluate Pareto efficiency using net payoffs alongside the mandated P(C,C).
- **Rare transitions can dominate stationary weights.** An absorption error below 10⁻⁶ need not bound stationary-distribution error when relevant exits are 10⁻²⁰. **Fix:** distinguish structural zeros from underflow/pruning, and test tighter exploration thresholds and numerical precision in the stickiest cells.

### 2. Predictions I think are wrong

- **“The new rung … cannot hold mass” does not follow.** Exploitability of the cheaper intermediate does not determine its stationary mass, destination flux, or return time. Repeated P*→intermediate→D excursions could materially reduce cooperation. **My prediction:** P* residence falls, but the narrow cooperation band requires full-chain evidence.
- **The claimed falsifiers do not identify the stated mechanisms.** Lazy-like outcomes cannot falsify the already-established fact that certification differs from sameness. Surviving incumbency would not show that *any* self-free, ALLC-punishing family locks in. **My prediction:** qualitatively similar outcomes can arise from different bottlenecks; classify failures by transition structure rather than outcome alone.
- **“Free against defectors should not be load-bearing” is overbroad.** certC retains free checks of constant D, precisely the exemption supporting neutral entry. **My prediction:** certC may match cert0 while free access to D remains essential.

### 3. Missing controls or cheap additions

- Add two diagnostic hybrid masks: change lazy’s **off-diagonal exemptions only**, and change its **self/copy exemptions only**. The current comparison jointly rewards cross-program legibility and removes P*’s copy subsidy.
- Compute hitting probabilities and expected return times from P*’s strict-exit destinations to D versus the FairBot family.
- At one decisive cell, add a small certificate-verification charge; measure whether the result relies discontinuously on exactly zero cost.
- Audit cert0/certC payoff differences on visited states: aggregate agreement may simply mean their distinguishing encounters are rare.

### 4. Alternative explanations not ruled out

- FairBot dominance may reflect mutation-prior inflow and neutral accessibility, rather than selection for compatibility.
- n=6 versus n=8 changes many programs and prior weights, not just P*.
- Apparent universality may result from truncating the language before another self-certified, incompatible family appears.
