# Review of `specs/2026-10-21-coalition-proof-auctions.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds:**
- *Bid-resolution order in Part B is missing from the note.* The spec states bids are resolved "in a fixed order of conditional clauses stated in the note," but `notes/coalition-proof-auctions.md` states no order. If `RING` bids $b_A + b_B = v_P + 1 = 21$ assuming a truthful $P$, and `XPOL` bids $(b_A+b_B) + 1$ against `RING`, `XPOL` bids 22 on a value of 20 (a net loss under first-price, and it doesn't even beat `RING` if `RING` targets $v_P$). **Fix:** Explicitly define the exact integer bids for every `(P, A, B)` triple in `src/auction_islands.py` before running. For `RING` to be an *underbidding* ring in the threshold instance ($20, 15, 15$), it must target $P$'s shaded bid (e.g., $b_A=b_B=6$ to beat `SH` at 10) or coordinate a low bid in first-price; against truthful $P$ ($b_P=20$), any winning bid sums to $\ge 20$, leaving zero underbidding margin below $v_P$ for `XPOL` to exploit profitably.
- *R1 simultaneous best-reply definition.* Under T-weak or T-strict, each responder $j \in N \setminus S$ has a *set* of best replies to $(d_S, v_{-S})$, not a unique bid. "Not jointly consistent" is undefined unless a single reply is selected first, and iterating independent T-weak selections can miss jointly deterrent best-reply profiles. **Fix:** Define R1 as the set of pure Nash equilibria of the complement's subgame (holding $d_S$ fixed) reachable from $v_{N \setminus S}$ via best-reply dynamics, then apply T-weak (worst equilibrium for $S$) or T-strict ($L_1$-nearest to truth) over that equilibrium set.
- *First-price truth baseline.* In Part A, testing whether the *truthful* profile is notion-robust under first-price is vacuous (truthful bidding gives 0 payoff to winners, so singletons always profit by shading). **Fix:** Restrict the robustness table under first-price to the Bernheim–Whinston core-equilibrium check, or test robustness of the BW equilibrium profiles rather than truth.

2. **Predictions that are wrong:**
- *Prediction 2 (VCG, AM, R1, T-strict):* Wrong at the boundary. If $v_P = 10$ and $v_A + v_B = 9$, item bidders overbidding to $(20, 20)$ win and pay $10 - v_B$ and $10 - v_A$, summing to $20 - (v_A + v_B) = 11 > 9$, a net loss. Losers' collusion under VCG is strictly profitable on integer grids iff $v_A + v_B < v_P$ **and** $2(v_A + v_B) > v_P$ (since total VCG payment when $b_A, b_B \ge v_P$ is $2v_P - (v_A + v_B)$ at truth-anchored responses, or 0 only if both $b_A > v_P$ and $b_B > v_P$, which requires bids up to $2G$—check whether $b_A = b_B = v_P + 1$ is allowed when $v_P = 2G$). Actually, at $b_A = b_B = 20 \ge v_P$, VCG payments are $\max(0, v_P - 20) = 0$, so they pay 0! Wait: if $b_A = b_B = 20$, they always pay 0 and profit whenever $v_A + v_B > 0$ and they lose at truth (which includes ties $v_A + v_B = v_P$ since ties go to more winners—so at $v_A + v_B = v_P$ they *already win* at truth!). My prediction: truth fails on $v_A + v_B < v_P$ (strictly, about 38.5% of AM).
- *Prediction 3 (Surcharge, AM, R1, T-strict):* Fails at integer grid boundaries. When the surcharge is 1 tick ($v_P - (v_A + v_B)$ is odd or small), discrete tick shading can either overshoot (losing to $P$) or split the surcharge without strictly exceeding $10^{-9}$ gain for a singleton. Expect singleton breaks on ~80–90% of positive-surcharge instances, not $\ge 95\%$.
- *Prediction 10 (Revenue with audit $\ge$ without audit under VCG):* Wrong in the *collusion* instance ($20, 5, 5$) if `COLU` (unconditional overbidder, untouched by the audit) survives or if `POL` + `COL` cycles raise revenue without the audit. More importantly, in the *threshold* instance ($20, 15, 15$), VCG revenue at truth is $10$, whereas if `COL` (which overbids to 40) fixes without the audit, VCG revenue is $0$; with the audit, if `SH` (bids 7) fixes in A or B, P wins and pays $\le 15$, potentially lowering revenue below truth.

3. **Missing controls or cheap additions:**
- *Add an unconditional-spite / noise-guard control in Part B.* In open-source settings, `POL`'s spiteful bid (19) is only safe because `COL` is verified to bid 40. Add a trembling/execution-error check or a `SH` + `COLU` cross-check in the static tensor to show `POL` loses $-\infty$ (or $-19$) if one item bidder fails to overbid.
- *Report seller revenue + bidder welfare (total efficiency) separately in Part A* for the BW first-price equilibria vs. VCG at truth.

4. **Alternative explanations not ruled out:**
- In Part B, `COL` requires *both* A and B to seed and coordinate (`COL` is truthful unless the other item is `COL`), making it a two-population coordination game (quadratic in prior mass), whereas `POL` in P is a unilateral neutral shadow of `T`. If `POL` beats `COL`, it may be purely demographic (one-slot vs. two-slot founding bottleneck, as in the union/boss asymmetry in RESULTS "The gene arm"), not deterrence. Pairing the uniform prior (U) with a matched founder-mass prior where `COL` has $\sqrt{p_{\text{POL}}}$ weight would separate demography from selection.
