# Review of `specs/2026-10-06-carrier-populations.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

## 1. Design flaws/confounds and fixes

- **Source-sensitive lumping needs a stronger correctness criterion.** Equal behavior against retained representatives does not establish equivalence against discarded sources: certificates can depend on the opponent’s exact skeleton. Require identical action **rows and columns against every original spelling**, including self/cross-twin cells, before merging. Aggregate prior mass explicitly; verify that twin-expanded transitions reproduce an unlumped small-catalogue chain.

- **The cutoff is selected using outcomes of enumeration and computational cost.** Targeting 100–300 classes can exclude CBP, bridges, or leaks; lowering it after timing changes the scientific object. Prespecify a cutoff containing the named mechanisms and a smaller fallback. Report both catalogues’ structural diagnostics, and treat fallback results as a different experiment—not confirmation of the original prediction.

- **The lottery’s endpoint is ambiguous.** “Verified closed cooperating class” could mean mutation/drift closure, which the context says generally fails, or absorption under the actual ε=0 migration process. Define closure for that process, specify the horizon and treatment of polymorphic islands, and distinguish failures from right-censoring. A horizon snapshot cannot establish permanent coexistence.

- **This is a finite template ecology embedded in a Turing-complete evaluator**, with tactic-generated certificates supplied externally. It tests executable verification, not unrestricted program evolution or endogenous certificate discovery. State that scope; separately measure certificate-production effort and specify the length prior completely, including list and entry weights.

## 2. Predictions likely wrong

- **Prediction 3 conflates soundness with evolutionary protection.** Soundness excludes false certified actions, not exploitation of every self-C, D-on-D program. Arbitrary Boolean templates can cooperate for reasons other than certified opponent cooperation. Predict no false accepted atoms, but potentially exploitable establishers. Restrict the unfakeability claim to a hand-proved subclass.

- **Prediction 5’s “no bridge” premise is unsupported.** If entry labels vary per atom, `or`/`if` can combine checks from both entries; even with one entry per program, other templates may bridge behaviorally. Predict that pairwise incompatibility of the named carriers need not imply catalogue-wide bridge-lessness. Enumerate bridges before predicting separated islands.

- **The numerical transfer predictions are overconfident.** Neither the exponent [0.3, 0.6], ≥0.9 lottery success, nor the optional-production ≤0.15 loss follows from pairwise realizability. Certificate availability changes establishment mass and transition topology. Predict qualitative transfer only if the same neutral-entry/shadow mechanism survives; otherwise no justified sign prediction. Forty runs also poorly resolve a 0.9 threshold.

## 3. Missing controls or cheap additions

- Run a **matched ideal-verification table on exactly the same sources, certificates, cutoff, and priors**. This isolates executable verification from grammar changes.
- Recompute decisive cells at a lower and higher K, with V/K fixed; separate false certification, failed certification, and timeout.
- Report prior mass of establishers, compatible establisher pairs, shadows, and bridges—not just class counts.
- Add one larger N or derive the decisive fixation-rate asymptotics. Three nearby points establish a local slope, not a large-N exponent.
- Include L×O in the lottery: optional production could principally affect initial establishment rather than stationary weights.

## 4. Alternative explanations not ruled out

Cooperation could reflect frozen-tactic coverage, free certificate provisioning, cutoff exclusion of spoilers, or syntax-dependent prior mass. Apparent entry separation could reflect finite-horizon metastability; apparent N-scaling could reflect crossover rather than asymptotics.
