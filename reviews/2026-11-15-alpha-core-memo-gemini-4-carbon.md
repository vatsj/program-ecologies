# Review of `notes/2026-11-15-alpha-core-memo.md` by gemini-4-carbon

**1. Derivation check & Question 1 (Step 3 and literature)**
Step 3 is **wrong as stated** and conflates α with β, and syntactic checking with behavioral simulation. 
- **Syntactic (clique) vs. Behavioral:** The complement $N \setminus S$ does not even need to resolve $S$'s behavior (contra C5) to hold $S$ down. Fixed programs can simply check *exact source equality* against the equilibrium profile: if $S$'s text changes at all, $N \setminus S$ plays a pre-computed punishment $a_{N \setminus S}$ (Tennenholtz 2004, *Program Equilibrium*). 
- **α vs. β and Pure vs. Mixed:** If $S$'s programs are deterministic and $N \setminus S$ simulates them (unbounded fuel), $S$'s action $a_S$ is known before $N \setminus S$ acts, holding $S$ to its pure **α-worth** ($\max_{a_S} \min_{a_{-S}}$). However, if $N \setminus S$ merely checks syntax (avoiding halting/fuel issues) OR if $S$ has a private random tape (DEFERRED 16), $N \setminus S$ sees the *deviation* (or mixed distribution $\sigma_S$) but cannot condition on the *realized action*. Then $N \setminus S$ chooses $\sigma_{-S}$ knowing $\sigma_S$ (or blindly punishing), which in mixed strategies yields the **β-core**, not the α-core! Note: In 2-player (or $S$ vs. monolithic $N \setminus S$) zero-sum/TU games, mixed α and β coincide by minimax, **but in NTU games (your setting!) mixed α-worth $\neq$ mixed β-worth** (Aumann 1961; Scarf 1971 proves non-emptiness for the NTU **α-core** of balanced/convex games, whereas the NTU β-core can be empty or smaller).
- **Uncorrelated vs. Correlated punishments:** In $|N| \ge 3$, independent private tapes only let $N \setminus S$ play *independent* mixed punishments $\prod_{j \notin S} \sigma_j$, whereas standard α/β-cores assume $N \setminus S$ can *correlate* ($\Delta(\prod_{j \notin S} A_j)$). Without a shared correlation device for $N \setminus S$, the strong program-equilibrium set is strictly *larger* than the standard β-core.
- **Missing Literature:** Aumann (1959, *Acceptable Points in General Cooperative n-Person Games* — proves strong Nash of repeated games = β-core); Forges, Mertens & Vohra (2002, survey on incentive-compatible/ex-ante cores); Monderer & Tennenholtz (2009, *Strong Mediated Equilibrium*); Oesterheld (2019, *Approval-directed agency and the decision theory of Newcomb-like problems* / open-source folk theorems). Kalai et al. (2010) is only 2-player Nash (unilateral), not coalitional.
