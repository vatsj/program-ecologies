# Review of `specs/2026-10-05-k-cut.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

### 1. Design flaws or confounds, and fixes

- **Ordinary cut soundness does not establish bounded distribution under a box.** The missing step is transporting □A and □(A→B) into a single admissible boxed witness, including hypothesis contexts, budget indices, and interactions with joint bounded Löb. The recurrence \(1+s_A+s_{A\to B}\) is valid only if both premises are usable in that witness context. **Fix:** exhibit a minimal distribution derivation with explicit indices and contexts; prove witness composition and the Löb/cut interaction before implementation. A zero-violation table is not this proof.
- **“Finite reachable set” needs a precise definition.** Recursive source unfolding can generate infinitely many syntactic formulas unless definitions are finitely represented; cycle handling can also accidentally admit circular proofs. **Fix:** specify finite graph representation, admissible cut formulas, and a strictly budget-decreasing search invariant. Verify pruning on derivations containing cut, not merely unboxed conclusions.
- **The grafts do not identify separate causal contributions as written.** Cooperators and exploiters can overlap; changed plays alter support and invasion relations. Single-factor grafts omit interactions and may produce inconsistent hybrid payoff tables. **Fix:** freeze the labelled sets on the full source catalogue, specify collision rules and exactly which ordered interactions are replaced, and run the full \(2\times2\) restoration design. Report its interaction term.
- **The chain conclusion depends on the ongoing solver audit.** New deep states could reverse an apparent K/free comparison. **Fix:** require seeded log-domain solves, independent state discovery, twin drift, residual checks, and rate-level validation for every chain arm.

### 2. Predictions likely wrong

- **Prediction 1’s cost estimate is unjustified.** Tree-size cut charges repeated premises repeatedly; GL lengths or lemma-sharing measures need not predict K_c witness size. I predict some targets may exceed 54 even if distribution is the correct missing mechanism. Failure at 54 distinguishes neither structural obstruction nor missing distribution from excessive proof cost.
- **Prediction 2’s “then” conclusion is wrong.** Few restored fakers or chain proximity to K does not imply restoration without fakers: fakers may return but have negligible prior mass, lack accessible victims, or be offset by new cooperators. I predict restoration of logical capabilities will be a better signal than aggregate \(P(C,C)\) proximity.
- **Prediction 3’s factor-of-two dominance lacks a mechanistic basis.** Stationary weights depend nonlinearly on competing entry/exit paths. I predict a material interaction, with no reliable ordering of the isolated graft effects.
- **Prediction 5 is not warranted by PB2’s isolated effect.** Restoring P* changes its ecological context. I would not predict noninteraction without the factorial control below.

### 3. Missing controls or cheap additions

- Cross **K/K_c × ordinary/one-up guard** at the same budget and N; one extra cell completes the interaction test.
- Include hand-checked positive and negative distribution examples, witness replay by an independent checker, and tests that K derivations remain valid in K_c.
- Report both source-count and **μ-weighted invasion hazards**, against a fixed victim panel as well as endogenous supported victims.
- Predefine lottery success and stopping/censoring rules. Twenty seeds cannot resolve small differences reliably.
- Make falsifiers exhaustive: 5–9 Gödel fakers, 17/20 lottery successes, and intermediate guard differences currently lack clear verdicts.

### 4. Alternative explanations not ruled out

Budget thresholds, source-prior mass redistribution, solver omissions, and finite-N crossovers can all mimic “incompleteness improves cooperation.” Fixed n and three N values do not establish the large-population claim, much less robustness as program expressiveness grows.
