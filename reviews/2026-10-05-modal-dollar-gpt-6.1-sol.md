# Review of `specs/2026-10-05-modal-dollar.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

### 1. Design flaws/confounds and fixes

- **The resource fallback changes the experiment.** Adding the three-way program and selected duplicates at fixed mass changes both expressivity and mutation supply. Any rescue could be a prior effect. Treat augmentation as a separate arm; pair modal and weak augmentation with identical syntax masses, sweep the added mass, and retain a same-cutoff comparison without augmentation.
- **Lazy exploration can conceal the relevant escape routes.** Tiny omitted flows can dominate stationary weights when retained exits vanish with N. Report stationary-weight bounds or convergence under successively tighter exploration thresholds, not just cut flow. Explicitly retain transitions through named shadows, greedy constants, and their polymorphisms.
- **The primary comparator risks never running.** Matched weak comes after both modal cutoffs. Move paired modal/weak cells immediately after static validation. The published weak result, with another grammar, cannot identify the effect of provability.
- **Finite-N success is not a large-N conclusion.** Predefine entry/exit-rate scaling tests and identify the barriers controlling stationary odds. Two high-efficiency observations at large N do not establish rescue in lim_N.

### 2. Predictions likely wrong

- **Prediction 2 misses direct greedy invasion.** Let P be the three-way program. The proposed payoffs are P–P = 1/2 each and S5–P = 5/6 to S5. Consequently, **S5 strictly invades a monomorphic P population**. No accommodating shadow is needed. Soundness certifies greed; P then rewards it.
  
  In the isolated P/S5 subsystem, if x is P’s frequency,
  \[
  u_P=1/6+x/3,\qquad u_{S5}=5x/6.
  \]
  The stable equilibrium is x = 1/3, with encounter efficiency 5/9 and mean payoff 5/18. My prediction: P can invade the old polymorphism without producing a stable efficient endpoint; greedy/prover mixtures or further replacement pathways remain important.
- **The claimed non-neutrality of P’s accommodator is unsupported.** The existing conditional accommodator can still demand 1/2 against P when greed is not provable, while conceding against S5. “Conceding to it costs 1/2 − 1/6” assumes the concession actually triggers. Compute the modal encounter before assigning that cost.
- **Prediction 1’s quantitative modal–weak agreement is too strong.** Matching syntax and prior does not match recursive encounter behavior. I expect the leak mechanism to remain plausible, but not necessarily weights within 0.1 at every N.

### 3. Missing controls / cheap additions

- Make the static table a **go/no-go checkpoint**: include every efficient candidate’s invasion by all five constants, plus its neutral neighbors.
- Validate representative recursive encounters independently using proof certificates or the existing proof checker. “Neither antecedent is provable” needs verification, not an informal fixed-point argument.
- Run the exact reduced subsystem {S3, S5, accommodator, P}; compare its stationary weights and transitions with the full chain.
- For the lottery, include a matched weak cell with identical initialization and stopping rules; distinguish verified closure from merely long residence.

### 4. Alternative explanations not ruled out

- Improved efficiency could reflect changed mutation supply or missing exploiters, rather than sound reading.
- Apparent rescue could be a finite-N barrier or metastable window.
- Efficient lottery outcomes could arise from risk dominance and founder composition, independently of provability.

### 5. Ideas beyond this experiment

Separate **unfakeability** from **resistance to profitable certified demands**. In bargaining, correctly recognizing an opponent’s commitment can make exploitation easier. Candidate rescue programs need an invasion-resistant acceptance rule, not merely sound source recognition and efficient self-play.
