# Review of `specs/2026-10-14-dominance-contracts.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds**
- **Menu G (D4) has a fatal combinatorial bottleneck.** A contract $\kappa = (S, a_S)$ is a specific pair of demands. There are $\binom{3}{2} \times 7^2 = 147$ possible contracts (98 naming a given slot). At $p_c = 0.1$ and $N = 100$, each slot receives $\sim 10$ contract copies drawn uniformly from 98 options. Further, for $\kappa$ to install in *both* slots of $S$, how is the island menu defined? If $\kappa$ is on the menu whenever *at least one* individual in *either* slot carries it, say so; if it requires carriers in *both* slots of $S$ to be present simultaneously, the probability a specific dominating move exists on an island is near zero ($\sim (10/98)^2 \approx 0.01$). **Fix:** Define the island menu in G as the union of contracts carried across all three slots, and restrict the contract universe to the $\binom{3}{2} \times 5 = 15$ *efficient* winning-pair splits (or increase $p_c$ to $0.5$) so D4 tests genetic extinction rather than initial sampling starvation.
- **Installation into non-refusers at $\beta = 1$ (R1) is ill-defined when $f_r > 0$.** If consent draws two non-refusers and passes, $\beta = 1$ attempts to overwrite the *entire* slot of $N=100$ individuals, but "drawn among non-refusers" cannot fill $N$ individuals if refusers exist in the slot. **Fix:** Specify that at $\beta = 1$, either (a) all non-refusers in $S$ get $\kappa$ while refusers keep their old demand (so $\beta=1$ is actually $\beta = 1 - f_r$, creating a polymorphic island and huge fitness selection), or (b) $r$-blind installation is the *default* in R1 (consent checks 1 individual per slot; if passed, all $\beta N$ individuals are overwritten regardless of $r$, keeping $r$ inherited). Actually, if $\kappa$ overwrites the individual's demand but *keeps* their $r$ bit, installing only into non-refusers immediately purges $r$ from the $\beta$ fraction *ONLY IF* you replace refusers with non-refusers. If you just modify non-refusers in-place, $r$'s frequency doesn't change at the event—it only changes afterward because non-refusers now play $\kappa$ (payoff 4 or 3) while refusers play the old demand $y$ (causing disagreement or lower payoff!). Clarify the exact in-place vs. replacement mechanics of installation.
- **Contract override persistence.** Once an individual has $\kappa = (S, a_S)$ installed, it overrides their underlying program. When a *later* contract $\kappa' = (S', a'_{S'})$ is installed on overlapping slots, the excluded player from $S$ (say player 3) still has $\kappa$ installed from the previous step! Player 3's constant action from $\kappa$ is no longer part of the active coalition $S'$, which may cause unintended demands. **Fix:** Specify whether a new contract on $S'$ clears old contracts in the complement $-S'$, or if contracts just overwrite the base constant action permanently.

2. **Predictions you think are wrong**
- **Prediction 7 ($\beta = 0.25$ fixes in $\ge 0.5$ of events).** Likely **wrong**. In `dollar3`, moving from $(3,3,0)$ to $(4,0,2)$ via $S=\{1,3\}$ requires slot 1 to demand 4 and slot 3 to demand 2 (while slot 2 demands 3). At $\beta = 0.25$, 25% of slot 1 demands 4 and 25% of slot 3 demands 2. A slot-1 invader demanding 4 only gets 4 when paired with a slot-3 invader demanding 2 (probability $0.25$); when paired with the $0.75$ resident of slot 3 (demanding 0) and resident of slot 2 (demanding 3), demands are $(4,3,0) \to$ disagreement (0) for the invader, whereas the resident gets $(3,3,0) \to 3$ with probability $0.75 + 0.25 = 1$! The invaders suffer a massive coordination penalty at $\beta = 0.25$ and will be crushed by Moran selection before fixing. **My prediction:** Fixation probability at $\beta = 0.25$ is $< 0.05$, and D3 will look identical to the no-contract baseline, not D1.

4. **Alternative explanations**
- If D1 at $mN = 1$ has *higher* V\* occupancy than $mN = 0$, it may not be because contracts "recover" V\*, but because contracts rapidly break wasteful/disagreement traps into unequal pairs, which then single-slot drift + migration convert into V\* (since unequal pairs have 121-gen residence vs 776-gen for V\*). The static interleaved walk in §1 is essential to separate "contracts select V\*" from "contracts accelerate entry into the P-drift basin of V\*."

5. **Ideas that matter beyond this experiment**
- **Farsighted refusal via source-reading:** Here refusal $r$ is a hardcoded guard on $y \in V^*$. In the Turing-complete/modal arm, can a program prove that accepting $\kappa$ leads to a state from which another contract will exclude it, thus deriving farsighted stability (Anesi) endogenously without a bespoke $V^*$ bit?
