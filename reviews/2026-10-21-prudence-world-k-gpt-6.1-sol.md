# Review of `specs/2026-10-21-prudence-world-k.md` by gpt-6.1-sol

### 1. Design flaws or confounds, and fixes

- **Arrival exposure is underspecified.** With \(t_0=6,400\), the first 10,000 post-merge generations deliver only \(10,000/(10,000+6,400)\approx61\%\) of the schedule’s infinite-horizon integral. Define whether A means delivered or extrapolated exposure; normalize the discrete schedule accordingly. Specify arrival sampling, replacement, and timing relative to reproduction, and test realized counts as well as expected exposure.
- **Higher PrudentBot share need not mean prudence was selected.** Fresh founders directly introduce PrudentBots, rivals, and predators; increasing A also increases demographic turnover. Record immigrant versus resident ancestry and decompose frequency changes into immigration and reproduction. Add a matched sucker-depleted arrival control, preserving non-sucker arrival rates and replacement counts.
- **The proposed premium confounds mechanism with composition.** K changes both encounters and which programs qualify as certified suckers. Compute PrudentBot–FairBot payoff differences against a fixed, paired list of opponent sources in both worlds, alongside realized encounter-weighted differences. Also report total population-level selection advantages: a positive sucker-only advantage can be outweighed elsewhere.
- **The object is a finite intervention, not persistent protection or a large-population result.** A summable tail supplies finite total immigration; the chase removes its premium entirely. Keep the chase rather than dropping it first, distinguish tail-end from chase-end outcomes, and restrict conclusions to finite-horizon persistence at \(N=12,800\). A scaling claim requires another N with explicitly matched exposure and observation-time scaling.

### 2. Predictions likely wrong

- **“The tail stops target drift” is too strong.** Renewed immigration introduces uncertified variants as well as selection against them, and that selection weakens through the tail and disappears during the chase. I predict reduced off-target residence time where exploitable suckers remain, not reliably zero terminal failures.
- **Monotonic PrudentBot share is doubtful, particularly in K.** Certified cooperators may reward FairBot equally; arrivals can also support anti-punishers and competing networks. I predict a clearer response to *uncertified-sucker exposure* than to total A, with weaker or nonmonotone responses in K.
- **Merging does not itself preclude clique invasion.** It removes spatial beachheads, not positive selection against susceptible holders. K’s certificate convention is the stronger reason to expect no tested identity takeover; world L remains vulnerable.

### 3. Missing controls or cheap additions

- Align each falsifier with its claim: “zero failures” is contradicted by one failure, not only by as many failures as baseline; the ≥0.1 effect and ≥0.9 universality thresholds likewise need corresponding verdicts.
- Twenty runs cannot establish elimination of a roughly 12% failure mode: zero failures still gives a one-sided 95% upper bound near 14%. Report paired effects and uncertainty; use trajectories and off-target person-time rather than terminal counts alone.
- Report \(P(C,C)\), support, transition structure, losses unconditionally, and outcomes both before and after the chase. Predefine target membership independently of the measured outcome.

### 4. Alternative explanations not ruled out

Direct immigrant replenishment; predator-mediated removal rather than prudence; increased turnover; K’s removal of identity interactions rather than a changed prudence premium; and slow transient retention inherited from the merge rather than stable protection.
