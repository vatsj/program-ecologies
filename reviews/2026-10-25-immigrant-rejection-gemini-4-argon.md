# Review of `specs/2026-10-25-immigrant-rejection.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds**
- **Symmetry of W in Candidate 2 (Status-quo tie-breaking):** In symmetric three-player majority division, the two fair pairs that include the pivoting player (e.g., player 1 in (1/2, 1/2, 0) vs. (1/2, 0, 1/2)) give the pivot the exact same payoff (1/2) and are structurally symmetric under the default Konishi–Ray table. Therefore, their discounted values $W$ are identical ($W_A = W_B$). A tie-breaker that "keeps the outcome with the higher discounted value $W$" will still face an exact tie and revert to drift. *Fix:* Either drop Candidate 2, or explicitly define the farsighted tie-breaker via a non-zero switching/transition cost $\epsilon$ in the KR recursion so the *current* state has strictly higher continuation value than a symmetric alternative.
- **Ambiguity of "modal outcome" in Candidate 3 (Conformity):** An island's state is a profile of slot distributions, not a single outcome. If the excluded slot has drifted to offering 1/2, encounters on the island are split 50–50 between two members (a 3-against-3 tie). The "modal outcome" is undefined or tied right at the critical pivot step. Furthermore, reading an island-wide summary statistic is an environment oracle, not open-source program reading. *Fix:* Define the signal as a lag-1 public history of the island's modal completed coalition, or replace it with a local sample (e.g., observing one random encounter on the island before acting) and specify tie-breaking when the sample is split.
- **Step 0 / Predictions circularity:** The brief says Step 0's decomposition is recorded before any derived arm is built and "the derived arms target it," yet the three derived arms and their exact predictions are already hardcoded in the spec. *Fix:* Make Step 0 a genuine gate: if Step 0 falsifies Prediction 1 (i.e., if gV actually acts through contract refusal rather than tie-breaking/immigrant loss), halt and revise the derived candidates before running the main panel.

2. **Predictions I think are wrong**
- **Prediction 3 (Status-quo tie-breaking extends lifetime $\ge 3\times$):** Wrong for the reason in §1. Because $W((1/2, 1/2, 0)) = W((1/2, 0, 1/2))$ for player 1, the KR valuation will not break the tie between two fair members. *My prediction:* It will perform identically to baseline `gKR(0.99)` (within noise, $<1.2\times$ lifetime).
- **Prediction 2 (Loyalty $\ge 0.8$ at $mN = 1$):** Over-optimistic at $mN = 1$. Loyalty only conditions on the *immediately preceding* encounter. At $mN = 1$ ($N=100$), immigrant density is high enough that a resident pivot will frequently have just interacted with a mismatched immigrant or an unaligned partner, scrambling its 1-step memory right before the tie encounter. *My prediction:* Loyalty meets the target at $mN = 0.1$ ($\ge 0.8$), but falls below $0.6$ at $mN = 1$.

3. **Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Excluded-slot drift control:** Member-to-member transitions require two steps: (a) the excluded slot drifts to offering 1/2, and (b) the included pivot accepts it at a tie. Add a diagnostic or control that freezes *only* the excluded slot's offer vs. *only* the pivot's tie-breaking. None of your three candidates constrain the excluded player (who gets 0 and has no loyalty to the current pair). Knowing how much of `gV`'s stability comes from the excluded slot versus the included pivot will sharpen the mechanism.

4. **Alternative explanations the brief does not rule out**
- **Hitchhiking vs. direct selection in Prediction 5:** If a candidate trait rises in frequency under migration, it might not be selected for its refusal/tie-breaking behavior; it may simply hitchhike on whichever island happens to resist invasion longest and therefore exports the most successful emigrants. Compare trait frequency conditional on island member-state stability, or track within-island frequency changes following immigrant arrival.
