# Review of `specs/2026-10-24-inspection-shadow.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds**
- **Lumping vs. twin-drift confound in the chain:** The solver audit (Queue #12) proved that the lumped chain ignores twin drift between payoff-equivalent classes, which directly alters trap stability. Here, the core mechanism is neutral twin drift between `cr(THEM(ME))` and `N` against `C`, and between `C` and reading inspectees against `cr`. If `src/mixed2*.py` uses the unexpanded lumped solver or θ-pruned exploration (which Queue #57 notes distorted `mixed2` from −0.009 to −0.47), the exit rates and stationary weights will be wrong. **Fix:** Explicitly require `chain_log` with full closure/whole-space exploration and the **twin-expanded distribution** (IMPLEMENTATION 4) for all chain cells.
- **Unscaled generation horizon on the lottery path:** Generation 2,000 is held fixed while $N$ scales from 100 to 800. Neutral twin drift of `N` into `cr(THEM(ME))` among co-seeded survivors takes $O(N)$ generations. At $N = 800$, $T = 2,000$ is only $2.5N$ generations—stopping the run mid-drift and confounding the asymptotic island state with finite-time slowing. **Fix:** Scale the horizon with $N$ (e.g., $T = 20N$: 2,000 / 8,000 / 16,000), or record the trajectory up to $20N$ while also reporting the $T = 2,000$ snapshot.
- **Ambiguity in "Prudent inspectees":** Multiplying all reading inspectees (`cr` and `if`-readers) by 10 lumps exploiters of `N` (which comply with `cr` and cheat `N`) together with perverse readers (which cheat `cr` or comply with `N`) and mutual-reading forfeiters. **Fix:** Restrict the $\times 10$ prior boost specifically to the behavioral class of inspectees that comply against `cr(THEM(ME))` and cheat against `N`, or report the behavioral breakdown of the boosted set in `notes/` before running.

2. **Predictions you think are wrong**
- **Prediction 3 (Lottery path falls below 0.5 at (800, 8) at $c = 0$):** Likely **wrong** (it will stay $\ge 0.5$, or even rise). In the $\varepsilon = 0$ lottery without mutation, `N` can only displace `cr` if `N` survives the founding scramble on the same island while `K` is already extinct. As $N$ grows, the deterministic scramble duration grows as $\ln N$, giving initial `K` founders more generations to decimate `N` before `K` dies out, while neutral fixation of any surviving `N` remnant slows as $O(N)$. Unless migration ($mN=1$) imports `N` from other islands and then imports `K`, larger $N$ purges the shadow more thoroughly during the scramble.
- **Prediction 2 (Chain P(efficient) odds slope $< 0.5$):** In the PD, the 3-step cycle (`cr, C` $\xrightarrow{1/N}$ `N, C` $\xrightarrow{O(1)}$ `N, K` $\xrightarrow{O(1)}$ `cr, K` $\xrightarrow{O(1)}$ `cr, C`) has exit rate $O(1/N)$ and O(1) return, which by standard trees gives $P(\text{efficient})$ growing with $N$ unless there is an $O(1)$ strict-invasion exit (a faker) or return fails (the chain gets trapped in a mixed commitment polymorphism). If Prediction 2 holds, it will be because return paths bypass `(cr, C)` into committed/mixed inspector states, not because of the $1/N$ shadow exit alone.

3. **Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Asymmetric reading control (Inspector-only reading) in the lottery:** RESULTS #55 noted that when only the inspectee reads, commitment appears. Adding the inspector-only reading arm at $(100, 64)$ and in the $N=10^4$ chain is nearly free and isolates whether inspectee reading is helping (by punishing `N`) or hurting (via mutual-reading deadlocks/fakers).
- **Record return-path destination shares:** In Design 1, don't just record paths back into the efficient set; record the branching ratio from `(N, K)` into the efficient set vs. into committed/mixing inspector traps. That branching ratio determines whether the $1/N$ exit is fatal in $\lim_N$.

4. **Alternative explanations the brief does not rule out**
- **Mutual-reading / forfeit exits at $c=0$:** When a reading inspector meets a reading inspectee, evaluation can hit the recursion/forfeit default. Depending on the default action, reading inspectees may strictly invade `(cr, C)` as fakers ($O(1)$ exit) rather than acting as remedies. The exit table in Design 1 will test this only if strict invasions by reading inspectees are tracked separately from constant `K`.
