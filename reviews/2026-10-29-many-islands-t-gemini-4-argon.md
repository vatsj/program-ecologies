# Review of `specs/2026-10-29-many-islands-t.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds**
- **Two-type constant-fitness Moran formula is invalid for self-reading mutants when the charge or action depends on whether the partner is a mutant or resident.** Wait—the game is 3-player fixed-role (`dollar3` with 3 slots, $N=100$ per slot). A migrant enters *one* slot $s$, while the other two slots $s', s''$ are monomorphic! Because players only interact *across* slots in a 3-role game, a mutant in slot $s$ only ever faces the fixed residents of slots $s'$ and $s''$. Its payoff and compute charge are indeed constant during fixation! However, verify that `dollar3` fitness in `many_islands` has no within-slot interactions. Assuming across-slot play only, the constant-fitness Moran formula holds *exact*.
- **Symmetry breaking in ODE from empirical founding pools:** In Question 1 & 3, the ODE is initialized from a finite pool of 16,384 simulated founding islands. Because of sampling noise ($O(1/\sqrt{16384}) \approx 0.8\%$), $x(0)$ is *not* exactly rotation-symmetric. If the symmetric state is a knife-edge (positive eigenvalue in the non-invariant subspace, as predicted in P4), integrating the ODE from the raw empirical $x(0)$ to $t = 10^5$ or "convergence" will amplify the sampling asymmetry and break symmetry to one fair pair, confounding the symmetric-chain analysis (and the Jacobian at the "symmetric limit"). **Fix:** Explicitly symmetrize $x(0)$ across the 3 cyclic role permutations before running the ODE for the symmetric trajectory and Jacobian analysis, and run the raw (unsymmetrized) empirical laws separately to report sampling-induced tilt.
- **Definition of tilt initial conditions ($x(0)$):** Question 1 and Predictions 2–3 rely on "a fair tilt, an unequal tilt with unequal pairs $\ge 0.6$, a wasteful/disagreement tilt", but the brief does not specify how program triples (constants vs readers) are weighted inside those tilts. In arm T, if an unequal tilt is constructed purely by reweighting constant triples, or if reader-containing triples are scaled down, you may artificially starve the population of readers. **Fix:** Specify the exact reweighting rule (e.g., scale outcome classes of the symmetrized post-anneal law $x(0)$ while preserving within-class relative frequencies, or reweight only the settled states while keeping reader marginals $q_s(\text{reader})$ fixed).

2. **Predictions I think are wrong**
- **Prediction 5 ($V^* \ge 0.95$ at $P_{\text{fail}} = 0.1$, dose `main`).** I predict this will fail or sit on the knife-edge ($V^* < 0.90$ from the unequal tilt, and potentially from the natural law). Reason: When an island is settled by a reader in one slot and constants in the other two (which occurs neutrally since a reader reading a constant finishes and pays $0$), a second reader migrating into another slot creates a reader–reader encounter. That encounter regresses, times out (paying $P_{\text{fail}}$ for failed reads plus $P_{\text{fail}}$ for forfeit), and plays the timeout action `(ALL, 2/3)`, which breaks the fair division into disagreement! Thus, resident readers actively repel migrant readers in other slots under $A_f = 0.1$, and fair islands drifting to one-reader states can be destabilized or block multi-slot reader conversion.

3. **Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Separate failed-read charge from forfeit charge ($A$ vs $A_f$) in the exact ODE:** Since the $27^3$ table is tiny and the ODE is instantaneous once $x(0)$ is known, add a cheap ODE-only control (re-using the $P_{\text{fail}}=0.1$ founding law or the $P_{\text{fail}}=0$ law) with failed-read charge only (no forfeit charge) and vice versa. This isolates whether any degradation at $P_{\text{fail}}=0.1$ comes from taxing the recursive read or double-taxing the timeout action.
- **Report reader retention along the ODE trajectory:** Track the marginal share of readers $\sum_{y \in \text{readers}} q_s(y)$ at $t = 0, 10, 10^2, 10^3, 10^4, 10^5$. If readers die out early under $P_{\text{fail}} = 0.1$ before conversion finishes, this immediately explains why.

4. **Alternative explanations the brief does not rule out**
- **The timeout action `(ALL, 2/3)` as a mechanical disruptor:** When two readers meet and time out, they play `(ALL, 2/3)`. Even at $P_{\text{fail}} = 0$, this timeout action alters the game payoff compared to the DSL arm $R$ (depending on $R$'s fallback convention). If arm $T$ differs from arm $R$, it could be due to the timeout *action* on reader–reader triples rather than Turing-complete fuel limits. The $27^3$ static table analysis in `notes/` should explicitly compare the $T$ table at $P_{\text{fail}}=0$ to the DSL $R$ table.
