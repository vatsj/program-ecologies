# Review of `specs/2026-10-21-behavioural-hooks.md` by gemini-4-argon

**1. Design flaws and confounds**

* **Tautological keys drown out discriminative keys under the length prior.** In `beh-max`, a written fill draws a key from the length prior conditioned on being true of the partner. Under a geometric length prior, short tautologies or near-tautologies (e.g., $\Box c \lor \neg \Box c$ at 5 nodes, or $\neg \Box \neg a$, which is true of any partner at world 0) have high prior mass and are true of *every* partner. A thanks write by `HOOK(t, D)` that draws a tautological key turns the carrier into an unconditional cooperator (`ALLC`), not `FairBot`. **Fix:** Deduplicate keys by GL-equivalence *before* assigning the length prior (assigning each equivalence class the weight of its shortest member), and explicitly exclude constant-true and constant-false keys from the pool.
* **Trigger contradiction in M2 and the "Why" section.** The brief states a default-defector (`HOOK(t, D)`) writes thanks after *received* cooperation ("thanks after mutual or received cooperation"). In `notes/hooks-w2.md` §1.1, a program writes thanks only if *it* cooperated ("a program that cooperated writes... a thanks if both cooperated"). If the code follows `hooks-w2`, an unfilled `HOOK(t, D)` never cooperates, never triggers thanks, and cannot be converted. **Fix:** Verify the trigger condition in `hooks_w2_kern.py`. If thanks requires mutual cooperation, the conversion of `HOOK(t, D)` requires an initial seeded C-fill or contract, and the M2 hypothesis as stated is impossible.
* **Undefined atom removal in `beh-max-noA`.** "Every key mentioning $\Box a$ removed" is syntactically ambiguous when depth is $\le 2$ (e.g., $\Box(a \land c)$ or $\Box \neg a$). **Fix:** Specify "every key containing the atom $a$ is removed."
* **Computational blowup in M1 and contracts.** Evaluating the GL fixed point on the closed node set for all pairs with arbitrary depth-2 modal keys, and classifying every individual against the island's current population every generation (M1), will vastly exceed the 35s/run of `open-max` (which just looked up precomputed bit 2 of $\beta$). **Fix:** Precompute the static evaluation table over the deduplicated key equivalence classes, and sample M1 every 10 generations rather than every generation.

**2. Predictions I think are wrong**

* **Prediction 1 (Statics: Gödel predator cooperates with itself).** *Wrong.* The Gödel predator is $G \equiv \text{HOOK}(t, C) + (\Box a \to D)$, which plays $C \leftrightarrow \neg \Box a$. In a match $(G, G)$, let $p$ be the symmetric outcome "$G$ plays $C$ against $G$". By equivalence, $\text{GL} \vdash p \leftrightarrow \neg \Box p$. By Löb's theorem ($\text{GL} \vdash \Box(\Box \neg p \to \neg p) \to \Box \neg p$), Since $\neg p \leftrightarrow \Box p$, we have $\Box p \to \neg p$, i.e., $p \to \neg \Box p$, which gives $\Box \neg p \to \neg p$ when substituting $\neg p$... wait: $p \leftrightarrow \neg \Box p$ means $\neg p \leftrightarrow \Box p$. Thus $\Box \neg p \leftrightarrow \Box \Box p$. Since $\text{GL} \vdash \Box p \to \Box \Box p$, we get $\neg p \to \Box \neg p$, contrapositive $\neg \Box \neg p \to p$ (not Löb). Actually, in the standard Kripke frame Evaluation: at world 0, $\Box a$ is false, so $G$ plays $C$ ($a=1$). At world 1, $a$ was true at 0, so $\Box a$ is true, so $G$ plays $D$ ($a=0$). At world $\ge 2$, $a$ was false at 1, so $\Box a$ is false, so $G$ plays $C$ ($a=1$). **Prediction:** In the finite-world GL solver, $(G, G)$ stabilizes at $C$ at world 2 (while $\Box a$ evaluates to false). However, against `FairBot` ($F \equiv \Box a$), at world 0: $F=0, G=1$. World 1: $F$ sees $G=1 \implies F=1$; $G$ sees $F=0 \implies G=1$. World 2: $F$ sees $G=1 \implies F=1$; $G$ sees $F=0$ at w0 $\implies \Box a_F = 0 \implies G=1$. **Prediction:** `FairBot` *exploits* the Gödel predator (plays $C$ vs $C$ or $D$ vs $C$ depending on exact box reading), rather than being suckered by it, because `FairBot` does not cooperate at world 0, so $\Box(F \text{ plays } C)$ is false!
* **Prediction 5 (Prudence outnumbers FairBot in `beh-max`).** *Wrong.* Under a geometric length prior, `FairBot`'s key ($\Box a$, 2 nodes) has much higher prior mass than `PrudentBot`'s ($\Box a \land \Box \neg d$, 6 nodes). Because written keys are drawn from the prior conditioned on truth, mutual cooperation will overwhelmingly write $\Box a$ rather than $\Box a \land \Box \neg d$. **Prediction:** `FairBot`-equivalents will outnumber `PrudentBot`-equivalents at generation 200.
