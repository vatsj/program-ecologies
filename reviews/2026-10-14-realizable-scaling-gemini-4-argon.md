# Review of `specs/2026-10-14-realizable-scaling.md` by gemini-4-argon

**1. Design flaws or confounds**
- **Fixed horizons ($T \in \{2000, 10000\}$) confound $N, I$ scaling with mixing time:** Along the calibrated path ($mN \propto N^{0.7}$), neutral bridge-mediated patchwork resolution scales roughly as $O(I \cdot N / mN) = O(I \cdot N^{0.3})$. At $(400, 64)$ or $(100, 256)$, the time to absorb a rival island via a neutral bridge is $2\times$ to $4\times$ longer than at $(100, 64)$. Unresolved patchworks at $T=10,000$ will be misclassified as permanent universality failures when they are merely in transit. **Fix:** Either scale the extension horizon with $I \cdot N^{0.3}$ or explicitly separate *absorbing* patchworks (zero living bridge copies, or bridge strictly invaded) from *transient* patchworks (bridge alive and drifting) in the primary universality metric.
- **$mN$ vs $I$ confounding on P2:** Keeping $mN$ fixed while increasing $I$ from $32$ to $256$ under all-to-all island migration dilutes per-pair migration rate by $1/I$, slowing pairwise coalescent/absorption times linearly in $I$. **Fix:** When evaluating Prediction 4 and 5 across P2, evaluate at the absorbing limit (or bridge-survival status), not fixed $T$.
- **C-donor seeding scales with $I$, not $N\cdot I$:** "One donor per island" means donor initial frequency is $1/N$, which drops from $0.02$ ($N=50$) to $0.0025$ ($N=400$), while seeded TwinD copies per island grow as $\mu N$ (from $0.036$ to $0.29$ per island). Along P1, C-donor is simultaneously losing initial donor density and facing linear growth in per-island clique competition. This confounds donor-mechanism stability with initial frequency dilution. **Fix:** Document this explicitly or add a control where donor count scales as $1/100$ of $N$ on P1.

**2. Predictions you think are wrong**
- **Prediction 5 (universality vs. total seeded copies $\mu N I$ collapses onto one curve across calibrated cells):** Likely wrong across P1 vs. P2. The event "at least one island fixes on TwinD" depends on per-island establishment probability $p_{\text{est}}(c, N)$ where $c \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu N)$ is copies *on that island*. If $\mu N \ll 1$, copies are singletons and fixation scales as $\approx I \cdot (\mu N) \cdot \pi_{\text{fix}}(1, N)$. Because TwinD cooperates only with copies of itself, a singleton on an island of size $N=400$ gets zero self-play payoff advantage until it drifts or meets a twin, whereas at $N=50$ a singleton is at $2\%$ frequency and meets twins $8\times$ more often. Thus, $N=400, I=64$ and $N=100, I=256$ have the same $\mu N I \approx 18.4$, but per-island establishment of a self-recognizing clique will be suppressed at $N=400$ relative to $N=100$. **My prediction:** At matched $\mu N I$, universality will be higher along P1 (large $N$, small $I$) than along P2 (small $N$, large $I$).
- **Prediction 6 (C-donor stays $\ge 0.85$ universal along P1 up to $N=400$):** Likely wrong at $N=400$. At $(400, 64)$, expected seeded TwinD copies per run is $\approx 18.4$ (well past the $\approx 8$ copy threshold), while the single donor per island starts at $1/400$ frequency. Even if the donor spreads certificates fast, $18.4$ seeded TwinD copies across $64$ islands will establish on at least one island in $>15\%$ of runs. **My prediction:** C-donor universality at $(400, 64)$ drops below $0.75$ due to identity islands.

**3. Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Per-island copy clustering:** Log the empirical distribution of seeded TwinD copies *per island* (specifically, count of islands with $\ge 2$ copies at $t=0$). Since TwinD needs self-interaction to beat defectors/prudent-open in the scramble, multi-copy islands ($P(\ge 2) \approx (\mu N)^2 / 2$) should predict identity takeover much better than total copies $\mu N I$.
- **Absorbing-state check at $T=10,000$:** For any run not universal at $10,000$, compute statically from the current population support whether a bridge exists that can invade or drift through the remaining rival islands. Zero extra simulation steps.

**4. Alternative explanations the brief does not rule out**
- **Certificate adoption lag at large $N$:** Under S-nonself, endogenous production requires meeting a non-self partner, producing a certificate, and having it spread by copying along skeletons. If the time to first certificate production + copy-sweep scales faster than the scramble time $T_{\text{nuc}}(N)$, S-nonself will lose to seeded TwinD at $N=400$ not because $\mu N I$ crossed an abundance threshold, but because endogenous certificate bootstrapping lost the race on individual islands. C-donor vs. S-nonself on P1 tests this only if donor density is matched ($N/100$ donors per island).
