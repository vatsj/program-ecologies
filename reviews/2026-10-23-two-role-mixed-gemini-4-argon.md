# Review of `specs/2026-10-23-two-role-mixed.md` by gemini-4-argon

**1. Design flaws and confounds**

* **Unbounded class-table recursion for `cr`:** `cr` requires the opponent's *exact* action distribution, not a single simulated trace. A depth cap $D=12$ bounds simulation traces, but if an inspector's `cr` encounters an inspectee's `cr` (or `if (cr ...) ...`), exact distribution evaluation does not naturally advance a single trace's depth counter and will hang when building the static payoff matrix. **Fix:** Explicitly specify the depth-charging and base-case rule for `cr` (e.g., `cr` increments depth $d \to d+1$ and queries the opponent's exact distribution at depth limit $D-d$; at $d=D$ it forfeits and pays $c \cdot D$).
* **Undefined division-by-zero in Endgame II's `cr`:** If Endgame I never bets ($P(\text{BET}) = 0$, e.g., constant `CHECK`), the posterior probability that a bet is a bluff is $0/0$. Also, because this is a normal-form representation where II chooses `CALL` or `FOLD` unconditionally, if I checks, II's action is payoff-irrelevant ($0$ variance). **Fix:** Specify that when $P(\text{BET}) = 0$ (or when II is indifferent after a bet), `cr` breaks the tie with its own fair coin `X` (and ensure `cr` only pays the compute price $c$ when actually evaluated, or specify if it is an unconditional atom).
* **Grammar size $n=6$ blocks the Stackelberg grid in Inspection:** With binary `coin A B` (size $1 + |A| + |B|$) and constants/atoms of size 1: size $\le 3$ gives dyadic denominators up to 2 ($0, 1/2, 1$); size 5 gives denominators up to 4 ($1/4, 3/4$); size 7 is required to reach denominators of 8 ($3/8$). Thus at $n=6$, the *only* expressible blind probability $p > 1/4$ is $p = 1/2$ (`X` or `coin I N`). The brief's example of $p = 5/16$ requires size 9; even $p = 3/8$ (`coin I (coin I N)`) requires size 7. **Fix:** Either increase the cutoff to $n=7$ (or $n=8$) for Inspection so $p=3/8$ is in the grammar, or explicitly note in the spec and Prediction 2 that on the $n=6$ grid the Stackelberg commitment target is $p = 1/2$ (payoff $-0.5$, carried by the 1-node free coin `X`).

**2. Wrong predictions**

* **Prediction 2 (Inspection, exact arm, $c = 0.01$):** Likely fails both the reader share ($\ge 0.6$) and inspector payoff ($> -0.5$). At $n=6$, the commitment inspector is `X` ($p=1/2$, payoff $-0.5$). Against a committed `X` population, `cr` complies and pays $c=0.01$ (net $-0.01$), whereas constant `C` complies for free (net $0$). This is the **unconditional shadow** plus **price ladder**: `C` strictly invades `cr`. Once `C` replaces `cr`, non-inspectors `N` invade `X`, then cheaters `K` invade `N`, restoring the cycle. Furthermore, even if `X` held, its payoff is $-0.5$, which sits on the boundary of your $> -0.5$ prediction. **My prediction:** At $c=0.01$, the `C` shadow undermines `cr`, keeping `cr` share $< 0.5$ and mean inspector payoff below $-0.5$ (closer to $-0.7$ or cycling). Commitment only holds at $c=0$ (where `C` is a neutral shadow, not a strict invader) or if $n \ge 7$ allows $p=3/8$ (giving buffer above $-0.5$, though the `C` price ladder still erodes `cr`).
* **Prediction 4 (Inspection, sample vs exact):** Wrong if $n=6$. At $n=6$ the commitment inspector is $p=1/2$, not $5/16$. Even a 1-sample simulator (`if Q = I then C else K`) earns $-1/4$ against $p=1/2$ vs $0$ for `C`, while against $p=0$ (`N`) it cheats and earns $+1$. More importantly, because $c=0.01$ breaks exact commitment via the `C` shadow, `exact - matched` will be near $0$, falsifying $\ge 0.2$.

**3. Missing controls or cheap additions**

* **Promote $c=0$ in Inspection from "cut list" to required control:** Because $c=0.01$ introduces a strict price ladder where constant `C` beats `cr` against committed inspectors, $c=0$ is essential to separate "source reading cannot sustain Stackelberg commitment" from "the compute price $c$ kills the follower's reader."
* **Check grammar size $n=7$ for Inspection** (or add a single primitive bias coin) if you actually want to test a non-trivial commitment grid ($3/8$) distinct from the 1-node fair coin `X` ($1/2$).

**4. Alternative explanations**

* **Prior mass of `X` vs. commitment selection:** In Inspection at $n=6$, if inspector mass concentrates at $p = 1/2$, you cannot tell whether selection favored Stackelberg commitment or simply favored the 1-node free coin `X` by huge prior mass (as in RPS). Having $p=3/8$ in the grammar separates them: commitment prefers $3/8$ (payoff $-0.375$), prior mass prefers $1/2$ (`X`, payoff $-0.5$).

**5. Ideas for the research program**

* **The follower's shadow in open-source Stackelberg games:** In any Stackelberg game where the leader commits to the minimum deterring rate $p^+$, the follower's best response is a pure action (here `C`). If reading has *any* positive cost $c > 0$, the unconditional pure action strictly dominates the reader once the leader is committed, destroying the deterrent that kept the leader honest. Stackelberg commitment in priced open-source ecologies requires the leader to occasionally punish unconditional followers (or the follower's reading cost to be lazy/subsidized).
