# Review of `specs/2026-10-25-many-islands.md` by gemini-4-argon

1. **Design flaws or confounds**
- **Lumping over an evolving composition distribution in (2b):** The class-level ODE $dx_c/dt$ assumes the distribution of underlying slot compositions within outcome class $c$ is stationary and identical to the founding pool's distribution. In reality, islands converted via migration ($a, b \to c$) have different program compositions (e.g., enriched in flexible readers or specific excluded-player types) than founder islands in class $c$. Furthermore, neutral drift of the third (excluded) player's slot during single-migrant events that do not change the modal outcome ($a, b \to a$) alters an island's future convertibility. Thus, the Markov property fails at the outcome-class level, and the ODE can diverge from (2a) even if initial within-class variance is low.
  *Fix:* Make the composition-pool resampling Monte Carlo (population dynamics over sampled 3-slot compositions, where an event updates the recipient's actual composition in the pool) the primary (2b) solver, and use the class-level ODE only as a diagnostic summary.
- **Premature stopping in (2a):** Stopping when no outcome-class conversion occurs in $20\cdot I$ events will trigger almost immediately if single-migrant conversion rates are $< 0.05$ (which is likely at $N=100$ where a single migrant starts at frequency $1/100$). Also, 200 generations of unchanged *exact* composition is too strict if neutral variants drift in the excluded slot, pushing many events to the 5,000-generation cap.
  *Fix:* Define settlement by modal outcome stability + fixation/extinction of the migrant lineage (or a 200-gen stable composition *up to* excluded-slot neutral drift). Raise the (2a) stopping rule to $\ge 100\cdot I$ events without conversion, calibrated against the empirical max entry of $K$ from (2b).

2. **Predictions I think are wrong**
- **Prediction 5 (Readers at $A=150$ reach $V^* \ge 0.9$ in (2b)):** Likely wrong. In the $mN \to 0$ jump chain, only *one* migrant arrives in *one* slot. In `dollar3`, converting a dominated island (e.g., disagreement or unequal) to a fair pair usually requires coordinated shifts in two slots, or a reader invading a resident slot where it is initially mismatched and gets payoff 0 against the resident coalition. At $N=100, w=0.3$, a single migrant facing payoff disadvantage goes extinct before a complementary migrant ever arrives. The churn experiment needed $mN \ge 0.1$ (overlapping migrants) to get $0.89$–$1.00$. I predict $V^*$ stays below $0.75$ in (2b) even with readers.
- **Prediction 6 (Time to first loss of a fair pair in (2a) grows linearly in $I$):** Measured in *events per island* (the brief's stated clock), the coalescence/absorption time of a neutral voter model is $O(I)$ in *total* events goes as $O(I^2)$, which is $O(I)$ in events per island—so the scaling is right, *except* the run caps at $400\cdot I$ total events ($400$ events per island). At $I \ge 256$, if fair-to-fair conversion $K(f_1, f_2 \to f_2)$ is small (say $\le 10^{-2}$), almost *no* runs will lose a fair pair before the $400\cdot I$ cap, making the scaling of first-loss time unobservable (right-censored) for $I \ge 64$.

3. **Missing controls or cheap additions**
- **Two-migrant (pair-propagule) injection probe in (2b):** The RS explicitly noted: *"Anything needing several migrants is EVENTUALLY lost."* Cheaply add a diagnostic cell to the (2b) injection pool where a migration event sends *two* migrants (one each into two randomly chosen slots from the donor). If single-migrant $K$ fails to clear dominated islands (Prediction 5 fails) while pair-migrant $K$ succeeds, that directly proves the RS's conjecture about why the $mN \to 0$ single-migrant limit breaks efficiency.
- **Track excluded-slot drift ($K(a,b \to a')$):** In the (2b) injection experiments, record how often a failed outcome conversion ($a, b \to a$) nonetheless replaces the excluded third player's resident strategy, since that third player is the pivot for future coalitional deviations.

4. **Alternative explanations the brief does not rule out**
- If (2a) and the class-level ODE in (2b) disagree, the brief attributes it to finite-$I$ stochasticity or initial within-class spread, failing to rule out **cryptic state evolution** (the excluded player's slot changing via neutral migrant fixation during $a \to a$ self-transitions, priming the island for later conversion).
