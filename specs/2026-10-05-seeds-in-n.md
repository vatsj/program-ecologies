# Spec: almost all seeds, cutoff sensitivity in n, 2026-10-05

Status: reviewed by gpt-6.1-sol (`reviews/2026-10-05-seeds-in-n-gpt-6.1-sol.md`) and revised; changes marked [after review]. Committed before launch. To be run by an Opus subagent.

[after review] This is a *local cutoff-sensitivity test* at n = 6–9. Finite cutoffs cannot establish uniformity in n; that needs a theorem or a tail bound. The result is evidence about whether the per-island chance moves with n over this range, kept separate from the path in (N, I) that "almost all seeds" needs.

## Why

The seeding lottery is now the primary object (DEFERRED.md 1). "Almost all seeds" held for the modal arm at n = 6 in
every regime (RESULTS "Almost all seeds?"), and the open part is uniformity in the language cutoff: as n grows the prior
spreads over more classes, more fakeable provers appear, and the question is whether a sound core still survives the
initial scramble with a per-island chance that does not vanish in n. For the Turing-complete goal this is the whole
question, since the real language has no cutoff.

## Design

`src/almost_all_seeds.py` (`island_job`, iid seeding from μ, certification), modal arm at n ∈ {6, 7, 8, 9}, PD, w = 0.3,
horizon 10⁵ generations, Wilson intervals.

[after review] **The object, stated.** ε = 0 (no mutation); each slot seeded iid from μ at cutoff n; migration mN = 1
migrant per island per generation, a generation being I·N births, so per-capita migration is 1/N and the number of
migrants scales with I; complete island graph (every island exchanges with every other with equal weight, so enlarging I
changes the number of sources an island hears from, which is noted as part of the object, not a confound); a run is
*certified outcome-frozen* when every surviving class is pairwise payoff-identical, and is *unresolved* otherwise at the
horizon. Administrative censoring (cells stopped for time) is reported separately from dynamical non-resolution, with
completed generations and exposure, under a common generation budget predeclared in the predictions file.

## RE predictions (Fable)

1. **One step down, then flat.** The per-island chance p(100, n) is 0.07–0.08 at n = 6 and 0.045–0.06 at n = 7, 8, 9,
   tracking μ_core (ratio 0.69) to within ±25%; it does not fall further from 7 to 9. *Falsifier:* the Wilson interval
   of p(100, 9) entirely below 0.03, or the n = 7 and n = 9 intervals disjoint with n = 9 lower. Overlapping intervals
   are inconclusive. [after review] Sol's alternative: flat outcomes could reflect FairBot's flat prior alone rather
   than the whole core; the composition in 4 separates these.
2. **Run-level fractions approximately follow 1 − (1 − p)^I** [after review: approximate, since no-migration p measures
   establishment without migration and spread is assumed; the nucleation-then-spread control tests both parts]. At I = 4
   the efficient fraction at n ≥ 7 is 0.15–0.25 (N = 100), 0.30–0.45 (400), 0.55–0.75 (1,600); at I = 64 and 256 the
   point estimate is at least 0.95 at every n (40 of 40 gives a Wilson lower bound of 0.91, which is the resolvable
   claim). *Falsifier:* (100, 256) with a Wilson upper bound below 0.9 at any n, or (1,600, 4) with an upper bound below
   0.45 at n = 9.
3. **Post-ALLC losses are fakeable provers taken by probe-fakers; core losses, if any, are not strict invasions.**
   [after review: weakened; unfakeability excludes strict invasion of a monomorphic core island, not neutral replacement
   or displacement from a mixed island.] Islands lost after ALLC extinction are, at least 0.9 of them, held by fakeable
   self-cooperators and taken by probe-fakers; any loss of a core-held island is reconstructed as neutral or mixed-state
   displacement, never as a strict invasion; and the number of post-ALLC losses grows with n. Runs with losses still
   freeze efficient in at least 0.95 of cases. *Falsifier:* a reconstructed strict invasion of a monomorphic core island,
   which would contradict soundness.
4. **The frozen efficient states track prior mass × establishment, not description length as such** [after review:
   mechanism defined]. FairBot and `BOX1(THEM(ME))`, which carry most of the core's μ at every n and have the cheapest
   entry, hold at least 0.6 of the islands in frozen efficient states at every n; the share held by fakeable provers
   falls with n. Report island shares against μ × establishment probability per class. *Falsifier:* fakeable provers
   holding more than 0.3 of frozen efficient islands at n = 9, or island shares uncorrelated with μ × establishment
   (rank correlation below 0.3).
5. **Island-level ALLC extinction is n-independent** [after review: island-level, not global]: the island-level median
   extinction generation lies between 15 and 60 at every n and N, with the distribution at n = 9 within a factor of 1.5 of
   n = 6 at each N; the global time grows with I as the maximum over islands. *Falsifier:* island-level median above 120
   at any n.

**What it would mean.** If 1–3 hold, the per-island chance is bounded below uniformly in n (it tracks the unfakeable
core's prior, which is flat from n = 7), so "almost all seeds" has the uniformity the seeding programme needs at the
level of this language family, and the extra fakeable provers at larger n cost nothing. If 1 fails with p falling
steadily in n, the core's survival through the scramble is the term that fails in the limit, and the programme needs
either structure (islands, which multiply chances) or a prior that does not spread the core thin. 3 failing would mean a
new kind of spoiler appears at larger n, which would be the first strict invader of the sound core and would contradict
unfakeability by soundness.
