## Why

Stage 1's lottery stopped about 4 ticks short of the core price. The losing bidder P's honest threat was selected out
early, while P exploited the founding winners, and the winners then ratcheted down. A best-reply responder held the
price only when evaluated last, because two responders reading each other's resolved bids cycle.

The milestone-4 lever is to read checked claims instead of simulating. P may carry a **certified policy**: a claim
about its own bid as a function of the winners' bids, checked against its source once. Winners may be **followers**:
they read P's certified policy, never simulate it, and choose their bids from it. Followers read certificates and P
reads outputs, so evaluation is order-free by construction.

The RE's prediction is that this fixes the artefact but not the economics. Once followers hold the item roles, P's
threat is never tested, so its level is neutral and freezes wherever the scramble left it (the unconditional shadow
again). What keeps the threat honest is a supply of underbidders to exploit: the RS's prudence premium, in proportion
to the sucker supply that the mutation schedule sets.

[After review] Two limits are stated up front:
- The follower's rule "bid enough to beat the certified threat" hard-codes the core constraint's arithmetic. What the
  run tests is the selection of threats and of followers against constants, not the discovery of the constraint.
- Order-freedom here comes from an acyclic dependency architecture (followers read claims, P reads outputs), not from a
  general semantics for mutual source reading.

This is a mechanism experiment at N = 100, not limit evidence.

## Design

**Instances** (stage 1's): threshold (20, 15, 15), asymmetric (20, 16, 12), skew (14, 16, 8). The no-overbid space (bids
0..v per role) is primary; the unrestricted space (bids 0..40 for every role, stage 1's grid; so cap = 40 there) is
secondary [after second review]. First-price, best-offer seller, random revenue ties, and a zero bid is no bid, all
as in stage 1 (`src/core_auction.py`, reused by import).

**Programs.**
- *P role:*
  - constants c ∈ 0..cap;
  - certified threat-exploiters `TX(t)`, t ∈ 1..cap. The policy is: if the winners' total T satisfies T + 1 ≤ t, bid
    T + 1; otherwise bid t. The certificate states that policy and is checked against the program's source once. The
    checker is the stage's ground truth, and false claims are not admitted.
- *Item roles:*
  - constants;
  - followers `F(δ)`, δ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, …, 0.9}, the share of the joint payment this item offers. A follower reads the
    opposing P program. If P carries a certified policy with threat t, the required total is R = t + 1 (enough to
    beat t). If P is a constant c, R = c + 1. The follower bids ⌊δ·R + ½⌋ (half rounds up), raised to at least 1, if
    that is at most its value; otherwise it bids its value. [After review:] rounding makes a complementary pair's total
    R − 1, R or R + 1, so the statics are enumerated exhaustively over all (t, δ_A, δ_B) before the dynamics. Followers do not read their partner.
- *Resolution:* items first (they read P's source or certificate, never P's output), then `TX` (it reads the items'
  outputs). There is no cycle. As a check, every triple is also resolved in the reverse order, by independent fresh evaluation (no cached
  outputs): when `TX` is evaluated first, asking for an item's output triggers a fresh evaluation of that item, which
  terminates because followers read only `TX`'s certificate. Order-independence holds because the call graph is
  acyclic [after second review]. The two orders must agree.

**Priors.** Uniform over each role's constants, with total mass m on the certified or follower class (m ∈ {0.2, 0.5})
spread uniformly over t or δ.

**Arms.** ε = 0 (primary); constant rain at εN = 0.05 per role per generation over 20,000 generations, drawing from the
same prior, so rain supplies constant underbidders (suckers) as well as new threats; the annealed tail at A = 3; and
per-role migration (mN = 1), as in stage 1. The fixed-role island lottery is stage 1's: N = 100 per role, 64 islands,
w = 0.3, 40 runs, 5,000 generations (rain 20,000).

**Controls.**
- (i) `TX` without followers: items are constants only.
- (ii) Followers without `TX`: P is constants only.
- (iii) Rain without `TX` (stage 1's constants-only rain cell, rerun on these seeds).
- (iv) Sucker-depleted rain [redefined after review]: item-role rain draws only followers (no constants), while P-role
  rain is unchanged. Founding states and the timing and count of arrivals are paired with the full rain.
- (iv″) Safe-follower rain [after second review: in (iv), low-δ followers paired with a resident follower can still
  underbid and act as suckers]: item-role rain draws only followers with δ ≥ 0.5, so no two of them can underbid a
  threat. The arrival rates are matched to (iv). In (iv) the encounter-level `TX` win rate is logged, to see whether
  δ-mismatched followers do the work of constant suckers.
- (iv′) Rain split by role: P-only arrivals and item-only arrivals, each at the full per-role rate.
- (vi) Matched constants [after review]: the `TX(t)` mass is replaced by constants c = t. Followers answer both the
  same way, so this isolates the payoff from exploiting underbidders from the deterrence itself.
- (vii) N panel at ε = 0 in the primary cell: N ∈ {50, 100, 200}.
- (v) Bluffing, in the unrestricted space only: `TX(t)` with t above P's value.

**Outputs.** Per island:
- *primary* [after review]: the encounter-level core probability and efficiency; also island-mean core, edge and
  eroded as in stage 1, with the discrepancy between the two reported;
- P's final threat t and its distribution against the prior;
- the rate at which `TX` wins encounters (*tested*) before and after followers fix, conditioned on the fixed δ pair's
  actual bids;
- trajectories of the threat distribution, payoff differences between `TX` and matched constants, fixation times, and
  replacements by rain or migration [after review], so that selection in the scramble can be told apart from later
  drift;
- the followers' δ pair and the split class: Vickrey-nearest, equal surplus, equal payment, other. In skew these are A's
  payment 10, 11 and 7 on the revenue-14 reference edge. The edge middle (9.5) is unreachable on the grid and is
  reported only as a distance. Splits at revenue 15 are scored against the same references, with distances reported
  as well as exact matches [after review].

## Predictions (the RE's; falsifiers in brackets)

1. *Statics* [revised after review: rounding makes the exact edge claim false]: for every complementary pair
   (δ_A + δ_B = 1) facing `TX(v_P)`, the efficient outcome's revenue is within two ticks of the minimum-revenue core,
   and every triple resolves identically in both orders [any order difference, or an efficient complementary outcome
   more than two ticks off].
2. *ε = 0, threshold, no-overbid, m = 0.5:* the core fraction lies between 0.2 and 0.6, and P's final threat has a
   mean at least 3 ticks above the prior's mean (selection in the scramble, then freezing) [core above 0.8 or below
   0.1, or the shift below 1 tick]. The matched-constants control (vi) shows the shift is selection for exploitation:
   its shift is at most half of `TX`'s [at least as large].
3. *The shadow:* on islands where followers fix in both item roles **and their fixed bids sum above P's fixed threat**,
   `TX` wins under 1% of encounters after fixation, so its threat is untested from then on [≥ 5%]. [Conditioned after
   review: non-complementary δ pairs keep the threat tested.]
4. *The sucker supply:* with `TX` and followers in the prior, rain gives at least 0.8 of threshold islands in the core
   (stage 1's constants-only rain: 0.45–0.49), and the safe-follower rain (control iv″), which supplies no suckers,
   falls back below 0.6 [rain below 0.6, or the safe-follower rain at least as good]. *Counter-prediction
   (gemini-4-argon), registered:* the follower-only rain (iv) stays at or above 0.6, because mismatched low-δ followers
   are suckers too. *Counter-prediction (gpt-6.1-sol), registered:* rain also supplies
   low threats that followers accommodate at once, so the direction depends on relative invasion rates, not on the
   sucker supply alone.
5. *Order-free:* every cell's outcome is identical under the two resolution orders [any difference].
6. *The split:* the follower split is set by the δ coordination game (a threshold contribution game). In skew the
   modal split under migration is not the Vickrey-nearest one [Vickrey-nearest modal]. Low confidence. Competing
   readings are registered: equal payment (δ = ½, the symmetric focal point) and equal surplus (the Nash bargaining
   reading). *Counter-prediction (gemini-4-argon), registered:* in skew, B's value cap clamps every δ_B ≥ 0.5 to a bid
   of 7 or 8, so a capped B with A paying 7 or 8 will be modal by prior degeneracy, not by bargaining. The static map
   (δ_A, δ_B) ↦ (b_A, b_B) at t = 14, and the prior-weighted split distribution before selection, are reported beside
   the selected one.
7. *Bluffs (unrestricted)* [narrowed after review]: bluffs that lose money when tested are depleted. Among `TX`
   programs that were tested at least once, at most 10% at the end have t above P's value [above 30%]. Untested bluffs
   can survive neutrally; their share is reported without a prediction.
