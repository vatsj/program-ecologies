## The primitive

`HOOK(t, a₀)`: at this point the program plays the value of its fill for tag t under the current key, if a valid fill
exists, else the default a₀. A program may carry several tags. A **fill** is a gene (t, scope, key, action), carried in
a heritable list.
- **Scopes:** *none* (the fill applies everywhere); *behaviour* (keyed on the partner's play against a fixed probe set:
  ⌜C⌝, ⌜D⌝, and the program itself); *role*; *history* (written after an observed outcome with this partner's key,
  e.g. a grudge after a defection, a thanks after cooperation); and in Part R only, *source* (the partner's bare source,
  world K's key) and *root* (the per-root situation).
- **Fill sources:** seeding (founders carry fills drawn from a declared gene prior, uniform over (tag, scope, key,
  action) among those expressible); history-triggered writing; copying from partners (horizontal, along the existing
  skeleton machinery in Part R); and pair contracts (both parties write mutual fills under the pair rule, Part R; in
  Part W a two-slot joint fill).
- **Guardrails (not opinions about hooks):** every fill is checked (Part R: by the realizable checker with the Key and
  self-hypothesis rules; Part W: by construction), so soundness is unconditional; one checker-level **conflict rule**
  (the most specific scope wins: source > root > behaviour > history > role > none; ties by recency), with "recency
  only" as an ablation; **equal node cost** for every hook kind (`HOOK` is one node whatever its scope); results reported
  **per hook kind**, with one-kind ablations.

## Amendments after the first review (gpt-6.1-sol; these supersede the text above where they conflict)

1. **The evolving object, fixed before any code.**
   - A program has at most 2 tags and carries at most 4 fills (FIFO eviction beyond 4); fills are inherited by offspring
     and never expire otherwise; bare source excludes fills (as in world K), so a program's source key is stable under
     updates.
   - Key domains are finite: behaviour keys are the 8 classes of the partner's play against (⌜C⌝, ⌜D⌝, itself); role keys
     are the game's roles; history keys are the partner's behaviour class (Part W) or bare source (Part R); source keys
     (Part R) are drawn from the bare sources present among the island's founders; root keys (Part R) are the game's
     finite root classes.
   - The gene prior: each founder carries k fills, k ~ Poisson(1) truncated at 4; each fill draws its tag uniformly from
     the program's tags, its scope uniformly from the expressible scopes, its key uniformly from that scope's domain, its
     action uniformly; this distribution is normalized and stated per part.
   - Write triggers: after an encounter in which the partner defected while the program cooperated, it may write a history
     fill "defect" keyed on the partner (a grudge); after mutual cooperation, a "cooperate" fill (a thanks); one write per
     encounter at most; copying draws one fill from a partner's list with the existing skeleton rule (Part R) or uniformly
     (Part W); pair contracts write mutual fills when both parties' hooks accept (pair rule in Part R; a two-slot joint fill
     in Part W).
2. **Circularity and soundness.** Behaviour probes are evaluated with the probed program's hooks resolved by its own fills,
   under a recursion fuel stated per part (a probe that exhausts it returns the default); probes against self use the
   prover (Part W) or the checker's self-hypothesis rule (Part R). In Part R every certificate whose validity a write could
   change is re-checked after the write, and adversarial interacting fills (two fills whose keys overlap with conflicting
   actions; a fill that would make another false) are tested.
3. **Ablations without reallocation.** Base-program founders and fill draws are coupled across cells; disabling a scope or
   a fill source makes those fills *inert* (held, inherited, costed, but never applied) instead of removing them, so supply
   does not shift; a renormalized-prior comparison is reported beside it. An **inert-hooks control** (hooks and fills with
   identical prior and storage but never applied) is added; held, applicable, selected and executed fills are counted
   separately. History controls: disabled, writes shuffled across partners, and learned but not inherited.
4. **Scope of claims.** The cross-wing comparison is descriptive; causal attribution to source access is made only by the
   within-Part-R ablation. Claims are finite-size; Part R adds (400, 64) for the PD as a small size check.
5. **Reporting.** Certificate rejections, key coverage, founder establishment, network transitions and persistence beyond
   the horizon; paired run-level intervals with outcome regions predeclared; threat-execution rates reported with the
   number of challenges (a threat never challenged is untested, not effective).
6. **Drop order revised:** the conflict-rule ablation, then fission–fusion, then the (400, 64) check; at least one
   fill-source control (seeding-only against all sources) is never dropped.

## Amendments after the second review (gemini-4-argon; these supersede the text above where they conflict)

1. **No churn from repeated writes.** A write whose (tag, scope, key) matches a held fill updates that fill in place
   (action and recency) and evicts nothing; evictions per encounter are logged. A grudge-only arm (no thanks writes) runs
   beside grudge-plus-thanks in both parts.
2. **History tracks partners.** In Part W history keys are the partner's lineage identifier (its founder ID), not its
   behaviour class, so a grudge targets the partner's lineage; in Part R they remain the partner's bare source.
3. **Source keys.** Part R's source-key domain is the bare sources of the whole run's founder pool plus the token
   SELF (the holder's own bare source), each with equal weight; migrants' and seeded fills therefore keep meaning across
   islands, and self-keyed fills have prior mass.
4. **Three-player write triggers** (social organization game): a worker writes a grudge against the boss's key when it
   works and receives zero wage or is whacked, and a thanks when paid at least ¼ while working; a boss writes a grudge
   against a striking worker's key and a thanks against a worker who works at a positive wage; at most one write per
   encounter per program.
5. **Benchmarks.** Claims about hook semantics are judged against the inert-hooks control; the shift from the no-hook
   baseline to inert hooks is reported separately as the grammar-dilution effect.
6. **Identity routes separated (prediction 6).** In Part R the source scope, source-keyed history, and pair contracts are
   ablated singly and together, so endogenous identity through thanks or contracts among clones is distinguished from
   seeded source fills.
