# Spec: the union game, a population-state precondition against a boss with a monopoly on force, 2026-10-05

Status: reviewed by gpt-6.1-sol (`reviews/2026-10-05-union-gpt-6.1-sol.md`) and revised; changes marked [after review]. Committed before launch. To be run by an Opus subagent.

[after review] This experiment tests *distribution*, not efficiency: every state in which both workers work and nobody is whacked has total payoff 2 and is Pareto-efficient, at any wage. Efficiency (total payoff) and equal division (s = 1/2) are reported separately throughout.

## Why

RESULTS "Three-player majority divide-the-dollar" showed that stochastic stability over source-reading programs with
fixed roles selects a rotated dictatorship of the pivot, and that reading source does not help, because the binding
exit is a partner's own strict defection. The RS's proposal is a *union contract*: a worker program whose refusal of
an unfair wage is conditional on a provable quorum of fellow refusers, "hide your strength, bide your time". The RE's
analysis in chat (2026-10-04) predicted a drift race between union organizing and pre-emptive repression, with
alternation rather than a union victory, and the RS asked for island-level selection on the boss population as the
ablation of myopia (DEFERRED.md 8).

## The game

Three slots with separate populations and fixed roles: a boss B and two workers W₁, W₂. Per encounter:
- each worker chooses **work** or **strike**; a working worker produces 1;
- the boss chooses a wage s ∈ {0, 1/4, 1/2} paid to each working worker, keeping 1 − s per worker, and a **whack**
  policy: whack strikers (cost c to the boss per striker, loss L = 1 to the striker), whack nobody, or **whack by
  source**: whack any worker whose source contains a union clause, working or not;
- payoffs: a working worker gets s (minus L if whacked), a striker gets 0 (minus L if whacked), the boss gets
  Σ(1 − s) over workers who work, minus c per whack.

Fair wage = 1/2 (equal split of each worker's product). c ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.5}. All programs read the others' source
through the free box as in the modal arm; workers read the boss's s and whack policy and each other, the boss reads
the workers.

**Programs.** The modal grammar with 3-node atoms about the current encounter, as in the three-player run, plus one
atom for workers, `QUORUM` [after review: made encounter-level, so it has the same executable semantics in the chain,
in mixed populations and in the agent-based runs]: "the *other worker in this encounter* provably strikes whenever
s < 1/2". Formally it is the box `BOX(THEM_W = strike | s < 1/2)` read from the other worker's source; two union
programs resolve it mutually by Löb, as FairBot's pair does. There is no population-wide switch and no appeal to
residents, so ordinary pairwise fixation formulas apply at every intermediate mutant count (unit-tested). The union
program is `strike iff s < 1/2 and QUORUM`; the scab is `work always`; the lone militant is `strike iff s < 1/2`.
The boss's programs range over (s, whack policy) constants and one-atom conditionals on the workers' *play*
(boxes about their actions), plus **two repression policies kept separate**: *strike targeting* (whack whoever
strikes, a behavioural policy) and *source targeting* (whack any worker whose source contains the `QUORUM` atom, a
syntactic tag; semantically equivalent union implementations without the atom, if any exist at this n, are tested
against it). n is chosen as in the three-player run (the smallest cutoff with one-atom conditionals); report
language sizes and the mutation mass of each named program.

## RE predictions (Fable)

1. **The fair state is not drift-closed; it leaks through the scab.** In the fair state nobody strikes, so a scab
   (`work always`) is neutral in a worker slot and drifts in at 1/N; once one worker slot is a scab, the quorum is
   unprovable, the remaining union worker works at any wage, and a boss mutant with s = 0 is a strict invader. So the
   fair state's exit is ∝ 1/N followed by a strict wage cut, exactly the shadow mechanism. *Falsifier:* a fair state
   with no neutral exit, or an exit slope steeper than −1.3.
2. **The low-wage states leak back through the union, and the quorum matters only at the intermediate wage** [after
   review: sol's point that a lone militant at s = 0 earns exactly a scab's 0 is right, so the militant enters
   neutrally there too]. At s = 0 both militants and union programs are neutral in a worker slot; once both worker
   slots strike, the boss earns 0 and a boss mutant with s = 1/2 (or 1/4) is a strict invader. At s = 1/4 a lone
   militant is strictly worse than a scab (0 against 1/4) while a union worker is neutral (it works until the other
   slot provably strikes), so the quorum is what lets refusal pass through the intermediate wage. In the
   encounter-based semantics, the second union program's neutrality holds in the no-repression subgame; under
   source-targeting repression it is strictly disadvantaged. Prediction: in the myopic chain at c = 0.5 the chain
   alternates among fair, intermediate and zero-wage states with transitions ∝ 1/N, the fair share is N-independent
   within 2× over N = 10²–10⁴ and lies in [0.2, 0.6], and the intermediate state holds at least 0.2. *Falsifier:* fair
   share below 0.1 or above 0.8 at N = 10⁴, c = 0.5, or changing by more than 3× over N.
3. **Repression wins the drift race more often at lower c.** Whack-by-source is deleterious only in proportion to the
   member frequency, so its entry into the boss slot is near-neutral when members are rare; it needs one fixation
   where the union needs two. The fair share is monotone in c: at c = 0 it is below 0.15, at c = 0.1 below the c = 0.5
   value by at least 0.1. The repression state's share is the mirror image. *Falsifier:* fair share at c = 0 above the
   c = 0.5 value.
4. **Spatial selection on bosses lowers the fair share** [after review: a bet, with sol's alternative recorded]:
   with w_g^B = 10, time in the fair state falls by at least 0.15 relative to w_g = 0 at c = 0.5; with w_g on all
   slots the effect is smaller. Sol's alternative: at positive c repression is costly and a boss facing scabs earns
   most without it, so migration may spread cheap exploitation rather than pre-emptive repression; the joint
   distribution of (s, whack policy) across islands decides between the two. *Falsifier:* w_g^B = 10 raising the
   fair share by more than 0.05.
5. **Finite ε:** from the low-wage start, the first fair phase appears within 10⁴ generations in all seeds at
   c = 0.5, and the system alternates with dwell in each phase of 10²–10⁴ generations. *Falsifier:* no fair phase in
   10⁵ generations in 2 of 3 seeds.
6. **Without the quorum the chain settles at the intermediate wage** [after review: the earlier reason was false].
   In a matched no-`QUORUM` grammar (same mutation mass otherwise), militants still enter neutrally at s = 0 and push
   the wage up, but at s = 1/4 a lone militant loses to a scab, so the boss's intermediate offer is the attractor: the
   intermediate state holds at least 0.5 of π and the fair state at most 0.15 at every c. With `QUORUM` the fair share
   is higher by at least 0.2 at c = 0.5. *Falsifier:* the no-quorum arm's fair share within 0.1 of the quorum arm's, or
   its intermediate share below 0.3.

**What it would mean.** If 1–3 hold, "hide your strength, bide your time" gets the union in, by neutral drift, and
the same drift lets the scab in afterwards: the fair state is one phase of an alternation whose share is set by the
drift race and by the price of repression, not a stable outcome. [after review] Alternatives the design addresses:
oracle-supplied coordination (the quorum is an encounter-level Löb read, the same mechanism as FairBot's pair, not a
population oracle); grammar multiplicity and mutation prior deciding shares (the reduced canonical chain and the
named programs' mutation masses are reported); repression as a syntactic tag (source targeting is separated from
strike targeting). Beyond this run, sol's distinction between *credible collective withdrawal* and *credible costly
repression* points to the follow-up: here repression is a committed program policy with no within-encounter
incentive check; the institution that survives when enforcement itself must be incentive-compatible is the next
object. That would be the three-player lesson again: the
exit is in a worker's own slot (the scab), and nothing the other worker reads can prevent it. The ablation then says
whether longer-horizon selection on bosses tips the race toward repression (4). If 2 fails toward a fair share near
1, the quorum precondition is a genuine stabilizer, which would be the first democratic result in the program. The
RS's predictions, recorded in chat: the union works with private membership (not realizable here) and fails with
public membership; the RE's: alternation.
