## Why

RESULTS "Proof length" established that a sound bounded prover K keeps the n = 6 modal arm's cooperation from a
small budget up, closes nothing, and cooperates more than the free oracle because its incompleteness removes a
Gödel-sentence faker; that proof length is set by boxed obligations re-proved in every Löb branch, since cut-free GL
shares no lemmas; and that a per-match price on budget rebuilds the ALLC price ladder. Three things follow for the
realizable-program goal (CLAUDE.md):

1. **n = 8 is where the prudent provers live** (PrudentBot at 24 sequents, P\* at 22 under Con, the level-2 ladder at
   52–54). At n = 6 the family that matters is FairBot's pair at 4–5 sequents. Whether K preserves the n = 8 free
   arm (P(C,C) 0.73 at N = 3·10⁴, the N^0.44 odds, the fakerless pair) at budgets below the prudent provers' cost,
   and what P\*-type programs do when their Con-dependent cooperation is unprovable in K, decides whether a cheap
   sound prover is enough for the whole free-arm result or only for its FairBot core. [after review] Programs outside
   the stationary support can still control entry and escape, so "the prudent provers are not in the support" is a
   reason to expect preservation of the core, not a bound on the numbers.
2. **Lemma sharing.** [after review: two distinct cost objects, kept separate.] (a) *Analytic cut* in the tree
   calculus: cut formulas restricted to box contents reachable from the root by unfolding (finite), tree size still
   the cost; cut can shorten a tree while still duplicating lemma proofs. (b) *DAG sharing*: the cost is the number
   of distinct sequents in the derivation (a certificate with shared subproofs), computed on the same cut-free
   derivations and on the cut derivations. Both are reported; neither is assumed to be the other. The question is
   which, if either, removes the re-proving cost that puts PrudentBot at 24.
3. **A price that does not rebuild the ladder.** [after review: these are imposed pricing schedules unless
   verification events are counted.] The *amortized schedule* charges c·b/N per match, presuming one verification
   per lifetime; the *lazy schedule* (RESULTS "Lazy pricing on graphs") charges c·b per match only against opponents
   that are neither constants nor copies; the *cache-accounting* control charges c·b once per distinct opponent
   source class met in a lifetime, which in a chain state with one resident and one mutant class is at most two
   verifications per lifetime, so its per-match price is ≤ 2c·b/N and the control tests whether the amortized
   schedule's assumption holds where it is applied. "Copy" is defined as *identical budgeted program* (same source,
   same budget); the alternatives (same source at another budget; extensionally identical play) are reported as
   separate subsidy rules in the lazy cell at c = 0.1.

**Prices in K** (chain at N = 10⁴, budgets {2, 3, 4, 6, 10, 16} per program with μ split equally, cross-budget plays
