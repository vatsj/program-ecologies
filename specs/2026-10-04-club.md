# Spec: the closed club, semantic self-recognition without syntax, 2026-10-04

Status: reviewed by gpt-6.1-sol (`reviews/2026-10-04-club-gpt-6.1-sol.md`) and revised; changes marked [after review]. Committed before launch. To be run by an Opus subagent.

## Why

Corollary 1 (THEORY §9.2): any program that mutually cooperates with anything in FairBot's component leaks at 1/N,
because ALLC is in that component and D exploits ALLC. The sibling theorem adds that in the modal language, even
programs that refuse FairBot are not drift-closed, because a reader that sees opponents only through finitely many
behavioural probes always has an exploitable sibling it cannot distinguish. The transfer analysis concluded that closure
needs a reader that can tell a sibling from a copy, by syntax (cliques) or by proof length. The RS's objection: the
island programs of the long-term goal read source, so "extensional probes" are not their limit.

This spec tests the one construction that intensional reading makes available and extensional probing does not: a
**club** defined by a universally quantified property of the opponent's behaviour.

> club-FairBot: cooperate iff the opponent provably cooperates with me, and the opponent is provably a club member,
> where the club K is the largest set of self-cooperating programs each of which cooperates only with members of K.

It is self-referential (K is a greatest fixed point), it is not a clique (any program of any syntax that behaves
club-correctly is in), and it cannot be expressed in the modal grammar because membership quantifies over all opponents,
which is why it has no sibling. The RS is not a fan of it, and the experiment should also measure what is objectionable
about it: it defects on FairBot, whose only fault is tolerating ALLC.

**Re-opened REJECTED entry: "Black-magic fixed points".** Club membership is the greatest fixed point of "cooperates
only within the club". That is a selection rule, exactly as the C-seeded certificates were (RESULTS "Certificates-only
arm"): the least fixed point is empty, the greatest is the club. Inside the club, self-reference still resolves by Löb
through the `BOX` atoms. So the arm has one imposed fixed point, at the level of membership, and the brief must say so.
The justification for re-opening is Corollary 1: there is no selection-free route to a closed network that is not a
clique, so the question is what the imposed rule buys and costs.

## What this arm is, and is not [after review]

`CLUB` as built here is a **global semantic oracle** over the finite universe of enumerated classes. It is not an
intensional proof procedure, and universal behavioural properties are undecidable for Turing-complete programs in
general. So this arm is an *oracle benchmark*: an upper bound on what a source-reading program could achieve by
semantic self-recognition. The write-up must keep three things apart: semantic membership (computed here), provable
membership, and bounded-proof-search membership (neither built). Nothing in this experiment establishes that
syntax-free realizable closure exists; the follow-up that would is to degrade the oracle to bounded, sound membership
certificates with explicit proof costs (sol §5).

## The arm (`src/modal.py` extension)

- Grammar: the modal arm's, plus one atom `CLUB(THEM)`, which costs 3 nodes like `BOX(THEM(ME))`. It is true iff the
  opponent's class is in K.
- **The joint operator** [after review: defined precisely]. For a candidate set K of program IDs, let P_K be the play
  table obtained by evaluating every pair with `CLUB(y)` := [y ∈ K]. Define F(K) = {x : x cooperates with itself under
  P_K, and under P_K x cooperates with no program outside K}. A club is a fixed point K = F(K). Because `CLUB` atoms feed
  back into P_K (and may occur under negation), F need not be monotone, so existence, uniqueness and the meaning of
  "greatest" are not given. The subagent must, *before any chain run*: (i) iterate F from the full set of
  self-cooperators, from the empty set, and from 20 random initial sets at n = 6 and 7, and report every distinct fixed
  point and any cycle; (ii) check whether the union of the fixed points found is itself a fixed point; (iii) state
  which fixed point the chain uses and why. If F cycles or has several maximal fixed points, that is a result, and the
  chain runs on each maximal one separately. A restricted grammar in which `CLUB` occurs only positively is run as a
  control, since monotonicity is more plausible there; report whether it holds empirically.
- **Syntactic IDs** [after review]. Membership is solved over syntactic program IDs, not behavioural classes;
  behavioural equivalence is recomputed *after* the fixed point is solved, since quotienting can change under K and
  under the cutoff. Programs are tracked by ID across cutoffs.
- Sanity: in the base modal language (no `CLUB` atom) K is expected to be empty at n ≤ 9. Verify; if it is not, name
  the members.
- Classes are merged as usual. Record, for each self-cooperating class, whether it is in K, whether it is suckerable,
  and its μ.

## What to measure

1. **Composition of K** at n = 6, 7, 8, 9: number of programs and of behavioural classes, μ-mass, the members by
   name. [after review] Also the mutual-cooperation graph *within* K: closure plus self-cooperation does not imply
   members cooperate with each other. Report its components. The clique diagnostic is this graph, not the class count:
   a clique in disguise is a K whose members cooperate only with their own behavioural copies (the within-K graph is a
   disjoint union of singletons), while a club worth the name has members of different behaviour cooperating.
   Compare with the clique arm at matched node cost and prior, and with the P* family.
2. **Collateral:** the μ-mass of self-cooperating classes that are *unsuckerable* but outside K (FairBot, PrudentBot,
   …), and the mass of those that are suckerable and outside K (ALLC, …). Also report the club's "universality" in the
   old sense, the μ-weighted share of FairBot's component it cooperates with (expected: 0).
3. **Cutoff dependence:** which members at n drop out at n + 1, and why (name the new non-member they cooperate with).
4. **The ε→0 chain,** PD, w = 0.3, `eager_poly=False`, n ∈ {6, 8}, N ∈ {10², 10³, 10⁴, 10⁵}: π over states; support
   and transitions; P(C,C); exits from the top club state split into strict, neutral and deleterious, with their rates
   in the log domain and the fitted exit slope in N; entry ρ(club-FairBot | all-D); hitting time from all-D into K (as
   in the clique experiment, `priced_limN.hitting_time`); rival share with the block definition from "Certificate
   pricing". [after review] Report numerical residuals; at N = 10⁵ exponential rates underflow, so rates are computed
   and stored as logs. Separate membership mass from conditional fixation rates in the report.
5. **Controls:** the free modal arm at the same cells (already known), and the clique arm `eq(THEM, ME)` with m = 1 at
   the same cells, so the club is placed between "universal and leaky" and "syntactic and closed".
6. **Static theorem check:** verify by enumeration that no class in the language is a neutral entrant into
   all-club-FairBot other than club members, and that no club member is suckerable. These two facts are the
   drift-closure of K; if either fails the arm is wrongly built.

## RE predictions (Fable)

1. **K is empty in the base language** at n ≤ 9, and non-empty with the `CLUB` atom, containing club-FairBot
   `and(BOX(THEM(ME)),CLUB(THEM))` and its `BOX1` and `THEM(THEM)` variants. At n = 8, K has between 3 and 30
   classes and μ-mass below 0.01. *Falsifier:* K non-empty in the base language, or K at n = 8 with μ-mass above 0.05.
2. **K is drift-closed:** no neutral entrant into all-club-FairBot outside K, no suckerable member. *Falsifier:* either
   found by enumeration.
2b. **The joint operator has a unique maximal fixed point** at n = 6 and 7 [after review], reached from the full set,
   and the empty set is the only other fixed point; no cycles. *Falsifier:* two incomparable maximal fixed points, or a
   cycle. Sol expects this to be where the construction is most likely to break.
3. **Residence in K is exponential** [after review: restated]. Every exit from the top club state is deleterious with
   rate below e^(−0.2·N); the fitted exit slope is steeper than −3 over N ∈ [10², 10³]. Conditional on entry, π(K)
   ≥ 0.99 at every N ≥ 10² at both n, since entry is neutral and there is no competing closed network in the language
   (the base language has none, prediction 1). *Falsifier:* any strict or neutral exit, or π(K) < 0.9 at any cell. Sol's
   caveat stands: stationary dominance needs the entry and the absence of rivals, which 1 and 4 test.
4. **Entry equals FairBot's exactly** [after review: sharpened]. The 2×2 payoff matrices of club-FairBot and FairBot
   against D coincide (both defect on D, both cooperate with themselves), so ρ(club-FairBot | all-D) = ρ(FairBot | all-D)
   to numerical precision. Hitting time from all-D into K is within 2× of the clique's at m = 1, scaled by the μ-ratio.
   *Falsifier:* the matrices differ, or the rates differ by more than 10⁻⁶.
5. **Collateral is large:** the unsuckerable-but-excluded mass (FairBot's family, PrudentBot) exceeds K's own mass by
   at least 10× at n = 8. The club's old-sense universality is exactly 0. *Falsifier:* excluded unsuckerable mass below
   K's mass.
6. **Span is small but not a clique** [after review: diagnostic fixed]. The within-K mutual-cooperation graph at n = 8
   has a component containing at least 2 behaviourally distinct members, so the club is not a disjoint union of
   copy-cliques; but K holds fewer than 10% of the self-cooperating classes. *Falsifier:* the within-K graph is a
   disjoint union of singleton behavioural classes (a clique in disguise), or K exceeds 10%.
7. **Cutoff dependence** [after review: now a measurement with a weak prediction]. Guarding restricts whom a program
   cooperates with, not whether it keeps self-cooperating after the joint solution changes, so losses are possible.
   Weak prediction: at most 20% of the members at n drop out at n + 1, and every non-guarded member (if any) drops out
   by n = 9. *Falsifier:* more than half of the members at n = 7 gone at n = 8.

**What it would mean.** If 2–3 hold, closure without syntax exists and the price-rate and moat results are bypassed by
a semantic membership test, at the cost of an imposed fixed point and of defecting on every tolerant program. The
normative question then becomes whether that collateral is acceptable, which the RS doubts; 5 quantifies it. If 6
fails toward a clique, the club is self-recognition with extra steps and REJECTED's clique verdict applies. If 3 fails,
the extensional stand-in for the intensional proof is not sound at this cutoff, which is itself a result about
truncation.
