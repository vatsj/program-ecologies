# Spec: rock–paper–scissors and mixed actions, 2026-10-23

Status: draft by the RE; reviewed by gemini-4-argon (folded [after review]), then gpt-6.1-sol (`--mode amended`, folded [after second review]); to be run by a
Sonnet subagent. Proposed by the RS (2026-10-23): "I suspect we're gonna need strong notions of mixed actions to
recover equilibria in adversarial games. I propose rock paper scissors for starters? We should recover the
mixed-strategy equilibrium pretty straightforwardly." REJECTED.md checked: the shared coin `X` (one draw per match) and
the biased-coin library `X_θ` were dropped for the PD (REJECTED lines on `X`); this spec uses only the per-evaluation
fair coin of IMPLEMENTATION §1 and a new fair binary coin, so it re-opens nothing. THEORY §5's indeterminacy rule
names RPS under the replicator as the textbook genuine limit cycle; the chain here only ever resolves two-type
contests and reports any three-type replicator fate it meets.

## Why

Every game so far had a pure efficient outcome. RPS has a unique equilibrium, the uniform mixture, and every pure
program is exploitable by its counter. Two questions:

1. **What does "recover the mixed equilibrium" mean in an ecology of programs?** Three readings, weakest first:
   (W) the population's time-averaged play is uniform; (M) the population is a mixture of pure programs that is
   uniform at each time; (I) individual programs mix, and the programs that hold the population are unexploitable.
   (W) is guaranteed by symmetry and says nothing. (I) is the RS's "strong notion".
2. **Does source reading select for individual mixing?** In a zero-sum game the minimax theorem says a transparent
   commitment to the minimax mixture costs nothing: a reader who sees exactly how you randomize gains nothing against
   uniform play, while it fully exploits any non-uniform program. The RE's conjecture is that exact readers create
   selection for individual mixing that an ecology of constants never has.

## Design

**Game.** Actions R, P, S; payoff +1 win, −1 loss, 0 tie; symmetric, one population, no roles (`ROLE` is omitted:
a public correlating signal is payoff-irrelevant here and its level assignment would break the cyclic symmetry;
declared deviation from IMPLEMENTATION §1). **Forfeit:** an evaluation that does not terminate within the depth cap
yields no action, which loses to every action (−1) and ties with a forfeit (0). This keeps the pure arm free of
hidden coins; the uniform-default rule is run as a sensitivity cell (below), not as the main rule.

**Grammar** (closed under the cyclic relabeling R → P → S → R, which commutes with every constructor):

```
A ::= R | P | S | X | next A | coin A A | cr A | Q Q
Q ::= ME | THEM | ↑A
```

- `X`: uniform over {R, P, S}, independent per evaluation (IMPLEMENTATION §1 with k = 3).
- `next A`: the action that beats A's action (R → P → S → R).
- `coin A B`: a fresh fair private coin picks A or B; only the chosen branch is evaluated.
- `Q Q` (application) and `↑`: as in the weak arm (`THEM(ME)` = what the opponent plays against me).
- `cr A` (the exact counter; [after second review] renamed from `br`, since it is not a best response once forfeits
  exist): best response to the exact distribution of A's action *conditional on A acting*; ties broken uniformly at
  random among the tied responses (the reader's own coin). If A forfeits with probability p_f, `cr A` forfeits with
  probability p_f (so surely when p_f = 1). `cr` reads the distribution exactly (within the bounded subevaluation),
  never a sample.
- [After review] **Forfeit propagates through `next`:** `next(forfeit) = forfeit`. [After second review] A genuine
  best response would answer a certain forfeit with a tie over all three actions, i.e. `X`, so exact readers would
  ground each other through the cap, a cap-made coin. The main exact arm therefore uses `cr`; the genuine best
  response `br` (forfeit is beaten by any action) runs as a sensitivity cell, **exact-br**.
- [After second review] **Depth accounting:** one depth counter per root evaluation; each application consumes one
  level, and a simulated or read opponent evaluation runs on the remaining budget (so a read sees the opponent with
  less depth than the opponent's own root evaluation has). For every pair checked at several caps, the inferred
  distribution of the opponent's action is reported beside its actual one.

**Semantics.** Each program's action against an opponent is a distribution over {R, P, S, forfeit}, computed exactly
(rationals or float64 with a declared tolerance) by depth-capped recursion; mass on paths deeper than the cap D goes
to forfeit and is reported. Simulation by application uses independent coins, so the two players' actual actions are
independent given the programs, and the payoff is the expectation over the product distribution. D = 12 in the main
cells. [After both reviews] A depth sweep D ∈ {11, 12, 13, 20} runs exhaustively on every pair among the named
classes and the classes holding the top 0.99 of π or of lottery mass, and on 1,000 sampled other pairs; truncated mass
and payoff differences are reported. The sweep detects cap dependence on the pairs that matter; it does not prove its
absence elsewhere.

**Arms** (grammar restrictions):
- **pure**: R, P, S, next, application (no `X`, `coin`, `cr`). Mixing can only happen across programs.
- **sample**: pure plus `X` and `coin`. Readers can only simulate (sample) the opponent.
- **exact**: sample plus `cr`. Readers see the opponent's mixed action exactly.
- Sensitivity: **exact-uniform**: as exact, but non-termination yields a uniform action instead of forfeit (this
  builds a coin into the evaluator and is a check on the forfeit rule, not evidence of mixing); **exact-br** (above).
- [After second review] Benchmarks: **constants** (R, P, S only) and **constants + X**.
- [After review] **sample-matched**: the exact grammar with `cr` replaced by a second unary constructor that behaves
  exactly as `next` (a simulating counter). It has the exact arm's grammar size and prior shape, so exact − matched
  isolates exact reading from added reader prior mass. [After second review] Stripping, deduplication and row lumping
  can treat the duplicate differently from `cr`, so the aggregated spelling mass per behavioural class, founder
  frequencies and twin handling are audited and reported for both arms after every reduction.

**Prior and enumeration.** Length prior 2^−nodes over spellings, enumerated bottom-up to n nodes (n = 6 primary; n = 7
where it fits the budget), with the weak arm's stripping rules (IMPLEMENTATION §2). Behavioural classes by exact
payoff rows, with the solver audit's twin handling.

**Objects.**
1. **The ε → 0 chain** (diagnostic, as for every arm): `chain_log.LogChain` with closure-seeded exploration
   (IMPLEMENTATION §4), w = 0.3, N ∈ {10², 10³, 10⁴}. Any polymorphic state whose replicator fate is a three-type cycle
   is reported, not forced: the indeterminacy rule (THEORY §5) applies.
2. **The ε = 0 seed lottery** (primary object for the Turing goal, DEFERRED 1): one population per island, (N, I) =
   (100, 64), mN = 1, 40 runs, horizon 2,000, paired founders across arms. [After second review] A size cell (400, 16),
   20 runs, horizon 2,000, in the pure, exact and sample-matched arms, plus a continuation of 10 primary runs per arm to
   10,000; without them, lottery conclusions are limited to finite-population establishment at N = 100.

**Statistics.** Per arm, object and N (for the lottery: per island at the horizon, then over islands; confidence
intervals clustered by run, since migration couples a run's islands). [After second review] RPS is zero-sum, so every
outcome is Pareto-efficient: this experiment tests equilibrium (unexploitable) play, not efficiency.
- **action exploitability** (ordinary RPS): the best expected payoff of a constant action against the population's
  mixed play;
- **program exploitability** e(p): the maximum expected payoff any program of the enumerated grammar earns against p;
  and **in-population program exploitability**: the maximum expected payoff any program of the grammar earns against
  the population mixture (a uniform population of constants has action exploitability 0 and program exploitability
  1, so both are reported);
- each resident's unilateral program-deviation regret against the population;
- **individual mixing**: the share of mass whose action distribution against the population (and against itself) is
  within 0.05 of uniform in total variation;
- **forfeit share** of plays, and the action frequencies over non-forfeit plays;
- support and transition structure, π weights, and exits (CLAUDE.md rule 4);
- the named classes' shares: constants, `X`, the simulating counter `next(THEM(ME))`, the exact counter
  `cr(THEM(ME))`, grounded readers such as `coin X (next(THEM(ME)))` and `coin X (cr(THEM(ME)))`. [After both reviews]
  `X` is a one-node program that earns 0 against everyone and is unexploitable; it is not behaviourally equivalent to
  the guarded readers (they beat constants) but can be neutral with them inside a support, which is shown pairwise.
  π(`X`) and π(guarded readers) are reported separately in every arm;
- [after review] in the lottery, the time-averaged number of behavioural classes alive per island and the island's
  homozygosity over generations 1,500–2,000, so that a monomorphic mixing island (I) is told apart from a polymorphic
  island of pure programs (M) or a cycling one.

**Validation before any counted cell.** Hand-computed payoffs for the named classes (every pair among the ten
smallest named programs, by hand in the notes); cyclic symmetry of the payoff tensor and of π (relabeling invariance
to 10⁻⁹); the D = 12 vs D = 20 check; reproduction of a two-type contest from `chain.py` on a toy table.

## Predictions (the RE's; falsifiers in brackets)

1. *Statics.* Against `X` every program earns exactly 0 unless it forfeits [any non-forfeiting program earns ≠ 0
   against `X`]. `cr(THEM(ME))` against itself forfeits [it terminates with positive probability]. `next(THEM(ME))`
   beats every constant and every program that terminates against it deterministically [a counterexample].
2. *(W) is trivial as an ensemble statement.* Relabeling equivariance of the payoff tensor and of π holds to 10⁻⁹
   [any violation: an implementation bug]. [Revised after second review] Symmetry fixes the ensemble, not each
   trajectory: pooled lottery action frequencies are uniform within run-clustered intervals, and per-run asymmetry
   (non-ergodic lotteries can break symmetry within a run) is reported, not predicted.
3. *Pure arm.* The holders are simulating readers whose self-play forfeits, not a mixture: π-weighted forfeit share
   of self-play ≥ 0.5 and π mass of constants ≤ 0.2 at N = 10⁴; individual mixing is impossible (no coin), so the arm
   "recovers" the game's value through mutual non-termination, not the equilibrium [constants hold ≥ 0.5 of π].
   *Counter-prediction (gemini-4-argon), registered:* the depth cap creates a ladder in the pure arm (shallower
   readers beat deeper ones at the boundary), or readers tie at 0 and drift neutrally while constants cycle.
4. *Sample arm.* Individual mixing appears but readers exploit non-uniform mixers only partially, so π-weighted
   exploitability is positive and falls with N more slowly than in the exact arm [sample-arm exploitability at most
   the exact arm's at every N].
5. *Exact arm: selection for individual mixing.* At N = 10⁴, π-weighted exploitability ≤ 0.05 and individual mixing
   ≥ 0.8 of π mass [exploitability > 0.15 or mixing < 0.5]. In the lottery, at least 0.8 of islands have
   in-population exploitability ≤ 0.05 [below 0.5]. [After review] Exact reading itself does the work: exact −
   sample-matched exploitability is negative at N = 10⁴ [≥ 0]. *Counter-prediction (gemini-4-argon), registered:*
   `X` (one node) holds most of the mixing mass in both arms as a neutral shadow, so exact beats sample only through
   the reader prior mass, and the matched control erases the difference. *Counter-prediction (gpt-6.1-sol),
   registered:* `X` already supplies minimax protection in both stochastic arms and readers pay recursive-forfeit
   exposure, so mixing is substantial in both and exact − matched has no robust sign until truncation is controlled.
6. *Uniform default.* In exact-uniform, forfeits vanish and the readers' self-play becomes uniform, so the pure arm's
   loophole and the exact arm's mixing become indistinguishable in play; reported as a check on the forfeit rule's
   role [no change against exact].

The RE's confidence: 1 and 2 high; 3 medium (depends on which readers terminate); 4 low; 5 medium.
